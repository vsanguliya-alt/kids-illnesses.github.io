Toddler Fever: Emergency Signs You Must Not Ignore

Most fevers in toddlers are caused by minor viral infections and can be managed at home. However, certain symptoms indicate a medical emergency that requires immediate attention. If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH).

Seek Emergency Care Immediately (Call 112/999)

Call emergency services or go to the nearest Emergency Department immediately if your toddler has a fever and any of the following signs:

Difficulty breathing – breathing very fast, working hard to breathe (ribs pulling in), grunting, or noisy breathing.

Blue or pale skin – lips, tongue, or skin turn blue, pale, or grey. On darker skin, check the palms of the hands and soles of the feet.

Stiff neck – unable to touch chin to chest.

Non-blanching rash – a rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass. This can be a sign of meningitis.

Seizure (fit) – a febrile seizure, especially if lasting more than 5 minutes.

Extreme drowsiness – difficult to wake, confused, or not responding normally.

Signs of severe dehydration – no wet diaper for 8hours, sunken eyes, very dry lips, or no tears when crying.

Severe headache – complaint of a severe headache.

Severe pain – severe abdominal pain or pain in a limb or joint.

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider Urgently

You should seek medical advice if your toddler has a fever and:

Fever persists – lasting more than 3 days (Source: NIH).

Temperature is 104°F (40°C) or higher – this is considered a high fever and should be evaluated.

Not drinking fluids well – taking less than half their normal amount of fluids; signs of dehydration include dry mouth, no tears, or fewer wet diapers.

Unusual behavior – your child is unusually fussy, irritable, lethargic, or difficult to wake.

Complaints of severe pain – such as a severe headache, earache, or stomach pain.

Has a chronic medical condition – such as asthma, diabetes, or heart disease.

A rash – any new rash with a fever should be evaluated.

Vomiting or diarrhea – especially if unable to keep fluids down.

You are worried – trust your instincts. If you are concerned, call your healthcare provider (Source: NIH).
