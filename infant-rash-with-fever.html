Infant Rash with Fever: When to Worry

A rash in an infant can be alarming, especially when accompanied by a fever. While many rashes are harmless, the combination of fever and rash can sometimes indicate a serious infection that requires urgent medical attention. Generally, a new rash in a child with a fever should be taken seriously .

Scarlet Fever: A Common Cause

Scarlet fever is a contagious infection that mostly affects young children. The first signs can include flu-like symptoms, such as a high temperature, a sore throat, and swollen neck glands. A rash appears 12 to 48 hours later (Source: NHS).

Key features of the scarlet fever rash:

The rash makes your skin feel rough, like sandpaper (Source: NHS)

On white skin, the rash looks pink or red

On brown and black skin, it might be harder to see a change in colour, but you can still feel the rash and see the raised bumps (Source: NHS)

A white coating also appears on the tongue (Source: NHS)

Common Rashes That Can Occur with Fever

Most rashes with fever in children are caused by viral infections. These rashes are often pink to red, and the child may have other symptoms like a cough or runny nose . These rashes are usually harmless and resolve as the illness clears .

Roseola (Sixth Disease): Caused by a virus, this typically starts with a high fever for 3days. As the fever suddenly breaks, a pink, spotty rash appears on the trunk and spreads to the limbs . It is common in children under 2 years old .

Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease: This causes a fever, sore throat, and a rash of small blisters on the hands, feet, and mouth .

Fifth Disease: A viral illness that causes a &quot;slapped cheek&quot; rash on the face, followed by a lacy rash on the body. A fever may be present .

Viral Exanthem: A general term for a viral rash, which can appear as red spots or bumps all over the body, often accompanied by a low-grade fever and other cold-like symptoms .

When to Seek Emergency Care (Call 112/999)

A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass is a medical emergency . This could be a sign of meningitis or a severe infection called sepsis . If the rash is this type, or if your baby has other concerning symptoms like a stiff neck, difficulty breathing, or is very drowsy, call emergency services immediately .

If you are ever in doubt, trust your instincts and see a doctor. It&#x27;s always better to be safe, especially with a young infant .
