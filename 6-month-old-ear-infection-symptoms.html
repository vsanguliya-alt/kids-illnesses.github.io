Understanding Ear Infection Symptoms in Your 6-Month-Old

If you have a 6-month-old baby, you know they can't tell you, "My ear hurts." This makes identifying an ear infection challenging, but not impossible. The key is to recognize the specific signs and symptoms that indicate your baby may be suffering from this common childhood illness. Ear infections are very prevalent in young children; in fact, three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday (Source: NIH | Ear Infections | Otitis Media | MedlinePlus).

Common Symptoms of Ear Infections in Babies and Children

For a 6-month-old, the symptoms of an ear infection usually start quickly. While older children and adults might describe pain inside the ear (earache), your baby will show it differently (Source: NHS | Ear infections). The primary symptoms to look for in a child this age include:

Signs of Pain or Irritability: Your baby may become fussy, irritable, or cry more than usual. They might also tug or pull at their ear.

Fever: An ear infection can cause a high temperature.

Changes in Behavior: Your child might have trouble sleeping or seem more unsettled than normal.

Discharge: You may notice a discharge running out of the ear. This is a clear sign of an infection (Source: NHS | Ear infections).

Other Discomforts: Your baby might experience a feeling of pressure or fullness inside the ear, which can be very uncomfortable for them (Source: NHS | Ear infections).

While these are the hallmark signs, it's important to remember that symptoms like ear pain, a high temperature, and general fussiness can also be related to other common issues, such as baby teething, a cold, or the flu (Source: NHS | Glue ear). In fact, ear infections are often caused by viral infections like colds and flu, or bacterial infections (Source: NHS | Ear infections).

When to See a Doctor

Because a 6-month-old can't articulate their discomfort, you should consult a healthcare professional if you notice any of the key symptoms mentioned above, particularly if they are accompanied by a fever.

If your baby has symptoms that last more than a few days, or if you see discharge from the ear, it is important to seek medical advice. Most ear infections clear up within 3 days, but sometimes symptoms can last up to a week (Source: NHS | Ear infections). A doctor will be able to examine your baby's ear to determine the cause of the symptoms and recommend the appropriate course of action.
