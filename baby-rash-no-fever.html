Pink Eye in Babies: A Parent&#x27;s Guide to Contagiousness

Pink eye (conjunctivitis) is an inflammation of the thin membrane covering the inner eyelid and the white part of the eye. While common in babies, especially those under 5 years old , understanding its contagiousness is crucial.

Viral and bacterial pink eye are highly contagious and spread easily from person to person . The infection often spreads when a baby touches the discharge from their infected eye and then touches another person or surface. It can also spread through contaminated items like towels, facecloths, or pillows .

A baby with pink eye can be contagious before symptoms even appear, making consistent hygiene critical . The infection can spread from one eye to the other or to other family members. The contagious period generally lasts while discharge from the eye is present . If treated with antibiotic drops, the baby is typically no longer contagious after 24 hours of starting treatment .

Allergic conjunctivitis is not contagious and cannot be spread to others.

Important Prevention Tips

To stop the spread of pink eye:

Wash hands frequently with soap and water, especially after touching the infected eye .

Avoid sharing personal items like towels, pillows, and facecloths .

Wash linens used by the infected person separately in hot water .

When to See a Doctor

Seek immediate medical care if your newborn baby (under 30 days old) has red, sticky eyes or discharge . For older babies, contact a GP if symptoms:

Last more than 7 days .

Include pain in the eyes.

Include sensitivity to light or changes in vision .

For a child under 2 years old, you will need a prescription from a GP for treatment .
