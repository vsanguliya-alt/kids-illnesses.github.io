Fever in Children: Emergency Signs Every Parent Must Know

Fever is a common sign that a child&#x27;s body is fighting an infection . While most fevers are harmless and resolve on their own, specific signs can indicate a serious medical emergency. This guide helps you identify red flags that require immediate medical attention.

🚨 Call Emergency Services (999/911) or Go to A&amp;E Immediately

Seek emergency care if your child has a fever and any of the following:

Neurological and Mental Status Changes

First-time febrile seizure (convulsion) that lasts longer than 5 minutes Unresponsiveness, extreme drowsiness, or difficulty waking Confusion, agitation, or unusual behavior Stiff neck or inability to move chin toward chest Altered speech or trouble walking Breathing and Circulation Signs

Struggling to breathe, breathing very quickly, or grunting sounds Blue, grey, or mottled skin around lips, tongue, or nails Cold to the touch with pale or mottled skin Specific Red Flag Symptoms

Non-blanching rash – a rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass (the &quot;Glass Test&quot;) Bulging fontanelle (soft spot on top of the head in babies) Inconsolable crying or high-pitched, shrill cry Severe headache or persistent vomiting Swelling of face, eyes, or tongue 👨‍⚕️ When to Call Your Doctor (Urgent Advice)

Contact your pediatrician promptly if your child has a fever and:

Situation Action

Under 3 months old with temperature ≥38°C (100.4°F) Seek immediate medical evaluation 3–6 months old with temperature ≥39°C (102.2°F) Contact doctor Fever lasting &gt;3 days (any age) Medical evaluation needed Signs of dehydration – no wet nappy for 12 hours, sunken eyes, dry mouth, no tears Contact doctor Refusing to drink/feed (especially infants) Medical advice required Immunocompromised or underlying health condition Consult doctor ⚠️ About Febrile Seizures

Febrile seizures are convulsions triggered by a fever, affecting 2% of children aged 6 months to 5 years . Most are short (under 5 minutes) and not dangerous. During a seizure, do not put anything in the child&#x27;s mouth. Place them on their side, and call emergency services if it is their first seizure, lasts longer than 5 minutes, or they have breathing difficulty or vomiting .
