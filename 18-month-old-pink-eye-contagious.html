18-Month-Old Pink Eye: A Parent's Guide to Contagiousness

Conjunctivitis, commonly known as pink eye, is an inflammation of the conjunctiva—the thin, clear tissue lining the inside of the eyelid and covering the white part of the eye. It is a frequent eye condition in young children, often caused by viruses or bacteria, though allergies and irritants can also be culprits . Understanding if and for how long pink eye is contagious is crucial for parents.

How Contagious Is Pink Eye?

Infectious conjunctivitis (viral and bacterial) is highly contagious and spreads very easily through direct contact . The infection can be passed to others when hands become contaminated by touching an infected eye and then touching another person or surface. Sharing items like towels, pillowcases, or toys can also facilitate the spread . This is why it's a significant concern in daycare centers and early childhood settings .

Crucially, not all pink eye is contagious. Pink eye caused by allergies is not contagious and cannot be passed to others .

How Long Is It Contagious?

The general rule is that pink eye remains contagious as long as the eye is producing a discharge (tears or pus).

Bacterial Conjunctivitis: This type is contagious and can spread to others . With antibiotic drops or ointment, the contagious period is typically shortened, and children can often return to daycare or school after 24 hours of treatment.

Viral Conjunctivitis: This is a self-limiting condition that usually resolves in 1 to 2 weeks . It is highly contagious, and the infectious period can even begin before symptoms appear .

General Guidance: You do not need to stay away from school or childcare unless you or your child are feeling very unwell . However, always follow the specific policy of your child's daycare or school .

When to See a Doctor

Urgent Medical Attention: Seek immediate care if your child has symptoms like pain in the eyes, sensitivity to light, or changes in vision . For a baby less than 30 days old with red, sticky eyes, get an urgent appointment .

Contact Your GP: For an 18-month-old, especially if symptoms last more than 7 days, you should see a GP . If your child is under 2 years old, you will need a prescription from a GP for treatment .
