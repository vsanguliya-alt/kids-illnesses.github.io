Preschooler Cough Home Remedies: Safe and Effective Options

Coughing in preschoolers is incredibly common and is usually caused by viral infections like the common cold. The good news is that you usually don&#x27;t need to treat a cold or the cough that it causes, as they often resolve on their own . However, there are safe and effective home remedies that can help your child feel more comfortable.

Safe Home Remedies for Preschooler Cough

1. Honey

Honey is a time-honored remedy for coughs and is actually supported by science. Research shows that honey can be more effective than over-the-counter cough suppressants for reducing the frequency and severity of a cough and improving sleep for children over 1 year old. It coats the throat, soothing irritation and acting as a mild expectorant to help clear mucus.

Important: Never give honey to a child under 12 months old due to the risk of botulism. For a preschooler, give ½ to 1 teaspoon (2.5ml) before bed as needed.

2. Use a Cool Mist Humidifier

Running a cool-mist humidifier in your child&#x27;s bedroom can add moisture to the air, which helps soothe irritated airways and loosen mucus, making it easier to cough up .

3. Saline Nose Drops and Suctioning

If the cough is caused by post-nasal drip from a stuffy nose, saline nose drops or sprays can help thin the mucus . You can follow this with gentle nasal suctioning using a bulb syringe, particularly for younger children .

4. Keep Your Child Hydrated

Drinking plenty of fluids helps thin the mucus in the airways. Offer your child water, warm soup, or clear broths to keep them well-hydrated .

What to Avoid

Over-the-Counter Cough Medicines: You should not give cold or cough medicines to children under two, and they are generally not recommended for older children without consulting a doctor first . These medicines often contain multiple active ingredients, increasing the risk of accidental overdose .

Aspirin: Never give aspirin to children for cough or cold symptoms .

Hard Candies or Cough Drops: These are a choking hazard for young children.

When to Call the Doctor

While home remedies can be very effective, contact your pediatrician if:

The cough persists for more than 3 weeks .

Your child has a high fever or seems very unwell.

The cough is accompanied by wheezing, difficulty breathing, or chest pain.

You suspect a more serious condition like whooping cough .
