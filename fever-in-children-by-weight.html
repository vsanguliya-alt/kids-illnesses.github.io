Fever in Children: A Weight-Based Guide to Safe Dosing

Fever is a common sign that a child's body is fighting an infection, activating the immune system and making it harder for viruses and bacteria to survive (Source: NIH | Fever). While treatment depends on the cause, reducing a very high fever may involve acetaminophen or ibuprofen. However, giving a child the wrong dose can have serious side effects, as children aren't just small adults (Source: NIH | Medicines and Children). The single most important rule is to use your child's weight to determine the dose, not their age (Source: NIH | Medicines and Children).

Acetaminophen (Tylenol) Dosing by Weight

Acetaminophen can be given to children 2 years and older using a standard liquid concentration of 160 mg per 5 mL (Source: NIH | DailyMed). The general dose is 10 to 15 mg per kilogram of body weight per dose, given every 4 to 6 hours (Source: PMC | NIH). Do not give more than 5 doses in 24 hours (Source: NIH | DailyMed).

Ibuprofen (Motrin, Advil) Dosing by Weight

Ibuprofen is another option for children over 6 months old (Source: PMC | NIH). The standard dose is 10 mg per kilogram per dose, given every 6 to 8 hours (Source: Europe PMC). Do not give more than 3 doses in 24 hours (Source: Europe PMC). For the 100 mg/5 mL children's liquid, a 5 mL dose is given for a weight of 24–35 lbs (2–3 years) (Source: MetroHealth). Ibuprofen is not recommended for children under 6 months of age (Source: PMC | NIH).

Important Safety Rules

Never give aspirin to a child with a fever, as it is linked to Reye's syndrome, a rare but serious condition (Source: NIH | Fever).

If your child is under 3 months old, contact a doctor before giving any medication (Source: HealthyChildren.org).

Do not use kitchen teaspoons for dosing. Always use the device that comes with the medicine (Source: HealthyChildren.org).
