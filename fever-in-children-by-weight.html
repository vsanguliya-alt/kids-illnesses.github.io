Fever in Children: A Weight-Based Guide to Safe Dosing

When your child has a fever, giving the right dose of medicine is essential for safety. Children aren&#x27;t just small adults, and dosing must be based on weight, not age, to be both safe and effective. This guide provides weight-based dosing information for acetaminophen and ibuprofen.

✅ Acetaminophen (Tylenol) Dosage by Weight

Acetaminophen is safe for children over 3 months old when dosed correctly. The standard dose is 10 to 15 mg per kilogram of body weight, given every 4 to 6 hours as needed. Do not give more than 5 doses in 24 hours.

✅ Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) Dosage by Weight

Ibuprofen is an alternative for children over 6 months old. The standard dose is 5 to 10 mg per kilogram of body weight, given every 6 to 8 hours. Do not give more than 3 doses in 24 hours. For the 100 mg/5 mL children&#x27;s liquid, dosing corresponds with weight.

⚠️ Important Safety Rules

No aspirin: Never give aspirin to a child with a fever.

Consult a doctor: For children under 2 years of age, check with your provider before giving acetaminophen.

Age restriction: Ibuprofen is not recommended for children under 6 months.

Avoid alternation: The American Academy of Pediatrics does not recommend alternating acetaminophen and ibuprofen, as it increases the risk of dosing errors.
