Fever in Children: A Weight-Based Guide to Safe Treatment

Fever is a common part of childhood illness and a sign that the body is fighting an infection . For young children, medication doses must be calculated carefully based on weight, not age alone, to ensure safety and effectiveness .

Why Weight-Based Dosing Matters

Giving the wrong amount of medicine is one of the biggest mistakes parents make when treating a child's fever . Using a child's weight provides a more accurate dose than using their age, as children of the same age can vary significantly in size . If you do not know your child's weight, use their age, but weight is always preferred .

Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) Dosing by Weight

Acetaminophen (Tylenol) is commonly used to reduce fever and pain in children. It is important to use the correct concentration, as liquid forms are typically available as 160 mg per 5 mL .

Standard Dose: 10 to 15 mg per kilogram (kg) of body weight per dose .

How Often: Can be given every 4 to 6 hours as needed .

Maximum: Do not exceed 5 doses in 24 hours . The maximum daily dose is generally 75 mg/kg/day .

Example: For a child weighing 10 kg, a single dose would be 100 to 150 mg (which is 5 to 7.5 mL of the 160 mg/5 mL liquid).

Ibuprofen (Motrin, Advil) Dosing by Weight

Ibuprofen is another option for fever and pain relief, but it has important age restrictions.

Age Restriction: Do not use ibuprofen in children under 6 months of age unless specifically directed by a doctor. Its safety has not been established for this age group .

Standard Dose: 10 mg per kilogram (kg) of body weight per dose . Some sources suggest a range of 5 to 10 mg/kg .

How Often: Can be given every 6 to 8 hours as needed .

Maximum: Do not exceed 3 doses in 24 hours and the maximum daily dose is generally 40 mg/kg/day .

Example: For a child weighing 15 kg, a single dose would be 150 mg (which is 7.5 mL of the 100 mg/5 mL children's liquid).

Important Safety Notes

Measurement: Always use the dosing syringe or cup that comes with the medicine. Never use a kitchen teaspoon or tablespoon as this can lead to an overdose .

Age Restriction for Acetaminophen: Acetaminophen should not be given to children under 12 weeks of age without a doctor's guidance, as fever in the first 12 weeks of life requires immediate medical evaluation to rule out serious infection .

Ask Your Doctor: Always check with your child's doctor to confirm you are giving the correct dose, especially for infants or if you have any concerns .
