Baby Diarrhea: A Guide for Parents

Baby diarrhea is a common concern, characterized by loose, watery stools that occur more frequently than usual. Diarrhea is generally defined as having three or more loose or watery stools in a day (Source: NIH). It is important to note that breastfed babies naturally have soft, seedy stools, so it's the consistency and frequency that matter most. Diarrhea in babies often comes with other symptoms like fever, vomiting, or a rash.

Common Causes of Baby Diarrhea

Viral Infections

Rotavirus is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children (Source: NIH). Other viruses, such as norovirus, can also cause diarrhea.

Bacterial Infections

Harmful bacteria like E. coli can cause severe diarrhea (Source: NIH). Some strains of E. coli can cause bloody diarrhea and, in severe cases, can lead to kidney failure (Source: NIH).

Food Allergies and Sensitivities

Diarrhea can be a sign of an allergic reaction to certain foods (Source: NIH). When you introduce one new food at a time, you will be able to identify any foods that cause allergies in your baby (Source: NIH).

Other Causes

Antibiotic use, certain medical conditions, and even teething can sometimes be associated with diarrhea.

Home Care for Baby Diarrhea

The most important goal in managing baby diarrhea is preventing dehydration.

Hydration is Key

Breast milk is best for a baby (Source: NIH). Continue to nurse your baby frequently. For formula-fed babies, continue offering formula. An oral rehydration solution (ORS) may be recommended by your pediatrician for older babies to replace lost electrolytes.

What to Offer:

Breast Milk or Formula: Continue feeding as usual.

Oral Rehydration Solutions: Use a commercially prepared solution like Pedialyte, as recommended by your doctor.

Avoid: Babies should not drink juice before age 1, because it contains a lot of sugar (Source: NIH). This can actually make diarrhea worse. Avoid water as the sole fluid for rehydration, as it lacks essential salts and sugars.

Continue Feeding (If Possible)

As long as your baby is not vomiting, continue feeding them. Bland, easily digestible foods can be offered to older babies (over 6 months) who are eating solids. Good options include:

Applesauce

Bananas (mashed)

Rice cereal

Plain toast or crackers

Foods to Avoid:

Do not give your baby honey before 1 year of age (Source: NIH). Honey can contain spores that cause infant botulism.

Avoid sugary foods, fruit juices, and dairy products, which can worsen diarrhea.

Diaper Care

Frequent stools can cause diaper rash. Change diapers often, clean the area gently with warm water, and apply a barrier cream to protect the skin.

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your baby's healthcare provider immediately if you notice any of the following signs of dehydration:

Decreased urination: Fewer wet diapers than usual (less than 6 in 24 hours for infants).

Dry mouth and lips.

No tears when crying.

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the top of the head.

Unusual drowsiness or lethargy.

Cool, pale skin.

Seek immediate medical care if your baby:

Is under 3 months old with diarrhea.

Has a fever (especially in infants under 3 months).

Has diarrhea lasting more than 3 days (Source: NIH).

Has blood or mucus in the stool.

Is vomiting and unable to keep fluids down.

Is showing signs of severe dehydration (e.g., listless, floppy, or difficult to wake).

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for baby diarrhea?

Call your doctor if your baby is under 3 months old, has signs of dehydration, has a fever, has blood in the stool, or if diarrhea lasts more than 3 days.

How long does baby diarrhea usually last?

Acute diarrhea from a viral infection like rotavirus typically lasts 3 to 8 days. If diarrhea persists beyond a few days, it's important to consult your pediatrician.

What home remedies can help a baby with diarrhea?

The most important "remedy" is preventing dehydration. Continue breastfeeding or formula feeding, and offer an oral rehydration solution if recommended. To prevent dehydration, you should also maintain fluid and food intake as much as possible.

What can I feed my baby with diarrhea?

Continue breast milk or formula. If your baby is eating solids, offer bland foods like bananas, applesauce, and rice cereal. Avoid sugary drinks, juice, and honey.

What are the signs of dehydration in a baby?

Signs include fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, no tears when crying, sunken eyes or soft spot, and unusual sleepiness or irritability.

Is diarrhea a sign of teething?

While teething can cause a mild increase in stool frequency and looseness, true diarrhea (watery, frequent stools) is not a typical symptom of teething and usually indicates an infection or other cause.

Is it safe to give my baby Pedialyte?

Yes, an oral rehydration solution like Pedialyte can be safe and effective for older babies, but you should always consult your pediatrician for the correct amount and how to administer it for your baby's specific age and weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call a doctor about diarrhea?

If your child has difficulty breathing, persistent high fever, lethargy, or symptoms lasting beyond expected, contact a healthcare provider.

Is diarrhea normal at this age?

Many variations are normal. Red-flag signs (listed above) mean you should seek care.

What helps at home?

Hydration, rest, and age-appropriate comfort measures. Always follow NHS/AAP guidance for medications.

How long does it last?

Most childhood episodes resolve within a few days. Persistent or worsening symptoms need evaluation.
