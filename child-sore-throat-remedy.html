Sore Throat in Children: Remedies and When to See a Doctor

A sore throat is a common complaint in children and can be caused by viral or bacterial infections. Almost every child gets tonsillitis at least once, which is an inflammation of the tonsils at the back of the throat (Source: NIH). While there are home care measures to ease discomfort, it is important to know when medical attention is needed.

Home Care for Sore Throat

The sources provided do not list specific home remedies (such as honey, warm liquids, or saltwater gargles) for sore throat. However, they emphasize the importance of monitoring symptoms and seeking care when appropriate.

Rest and Hydration

Keep your child comfortable and ensure they drink plenty of fluids to stay hydrated.

Soft, cool foods may be easier to swallow if eating is painful.

When to Call Your Healthcare Provider

According to the NIH, you should call your child&#x27;s healthcare provider if your child (Source: NIH):

Has a sore throat for more than two days

Has trouble or pain when swallowing

Feels very sick or very weak

When to Seek Emergency Care Right Away

Get emergency care immediately if your child (Source: NIH):

Has a lot of trouble swallowing

This may indicate a more serious condition requiring urgent evaluation.

Important Complications to Know

Rheumatic Fever

Rheumatic fever is a very rare complication that can develop after a bacterial throat infection (Source: NHS). If your child is diagnosed with rheumatic fever, they will need treatment to relieve symptoms and control inflammation (Source: NHS).

Critical warning: If your child has had rheumatic fever before, it is more likely to come back. Therefore, you must get sore throats treated early (Source: NHS).

Symptoms of rheumatic fever usually appear 1 to 5 weeks after a bacterial throat infection and include (Source: NHS):

Pain and swelling in the joints (arthritis) – usually ankles, knees, wrists, or elbows; joints may also look red

Pain in the chest, breathlessness, and a fast heart rate

Jerky, uncontrollable movements in the hands, feet, and face

Tiny bumps under the skin

Pale-red patches on the skin

If your child has had a bacterial throat infection recently and develops any of these symptoms, seek medical attention immediately (Source: NHS).

Diagnosis

To diagnose tonsillitis, your child&#x27;s healthcare provider will ask about symptoms and medical history. The provider will examine the throat and neck, checking for redness, white spots on the tonsils, and swollen lymph nodes. Your child will likely have tests to check for strep throat, since it can cause tonsillitis and requires treatment (Source: NIH).
