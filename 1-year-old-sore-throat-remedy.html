1-Year-Old Sore Throat: Safe Home Remedies

A sore throat is a common symptom of a cold that can make your 1-year-old uncomfortable. A cold can cause a sore throat, a stuffy or runny nose, sneezing, and coughing (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). Most throat problems are minor and go away on their own (Source: NIH | Throat Problems). However, treating a sore throat in a toddler requires safe methods, as many medications are not recommended for young children.

What You Can Do at Home

Since you should not give cold or cough medicines to children under two, focus on safe, comforting remedies to help your child (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). Here are effective ways to relieve sore throat discomfort:

Keep Your Child Hydrated

Offer your child plenty of fluids. Warm (not hot) drinks can be especially soothing. Hydration is key to preventing dehydration and keeping the throat moist.

Provide Comfort with a Humidifier

Use a cool mist humidifier in your child&#x27;s room (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). The added moisture can help soothe the irritation in their throat and make it easier for them to breathe, especially if they also have a stuffy nose (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Soothe with Foods

For children over one year old, cool or soft foods can be soothing. Consider offering:

A small amount of warm (not hot) soup or broth.

Cold foods like yogurt or applesauce, which can be comforting for a sore throat.

Keep Air Clean and Clear

Use saline nose drops or sprays and nasal suctioning with a bulb syringe to clear a stuffy nose (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). This can help if the sore throat is exacerbated by post-nasal drip.

When to Call the Doctor

Most sore throats get better on their own within a week. However, you should call your health care provider if your child has a sore throat for more than two days or has trouble swallowing. Also, contact a doctor immediately if the sore throat is accompanied by:

A high fever.

A white or yellow coating on the tonsils (which may indicate strep throat).

Swollen glands in the neck.

Difficulty breathing or swallowing.

Signs of dehydration.
