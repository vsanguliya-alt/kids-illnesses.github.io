Newborn Vomiting: Home Care and When to Seek Help

Vomiting in newborns can be alarming, but most of the time, it is not serious . It is often caused by a mild stomach infection or simply by the immaturity of your baby's digestive system. The most important goal of home care is to prevent dehydration . However, for babies under 3 months old, any vomiting should be discussed with a healthcare provider .

Home Care Guidelines

The focus is on keeping your baby hydrated while avoiding further stomach irritation.

Feeding and Fluids

Continue Breastfeeding: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that breastfed infants with vomiting should continue breastfeeding on demand; breast milk should never be interrupted . Offer each breast for 1 to 2 minutes every 10 minutes .

For Formula-Fed Babies: Continue offering formula, but you can try smaller, more frequent feeds. For example, offer half the normal amount every 1 to 2 hours .

Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS): If your baby is not getting enough fluids from breast milk or formula, ask your pediatrician if you need to use an ORS like Pedialyte . The ORS can be given in small, frequent amounts (e.g., 5 mL every minute) using a spoon or syringe .

Do Not Give Plain Water to newborns .

Careful Positioning

Keep your baby upright for 20 to 30 minutes after feeding to help prevent reflux .

Do not place your baby on their abdomen to sleep; always follow safe sleep guidelines (back to sleep) .

Recognizing Dehydration

Dehydration is the greatest risk for a vomiting newborn. Contact your doctor immediately if you notice signs of dehydration :

No wet diaper for 6 hours or more .

Dry mouth, lips, or tongue .

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot on the head .

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot on the head .

No tears when crying .

Red Flags: When to Call a Doctor Immediately

Call 911 or go to the emergency room immediately if your baby:

Is very sick, limp, floppy, or hard to wake up .

Has projectile vomiting (forceful vomiting that shoots out) . This is a classic sign of pyloric stenosis, a treatable condition requiring urgent evaluation .

Has green (bilious) or bloody vomit .

Has a swollen or rigid abdomen .

Has a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or more .

Contact your doctor now or seek immediate care if:

Your baby is getting sicker or has new symptoms .

Your baby has signs of dehydration .

Your baby is under 3 months old and is vomiting .

Vomiting continues for more than 6 hours .
