Stomach Bug in Children: What Parents Need to Know

Gastroenteritis is an inflammation of the lining of the stomach and intestines. Some people call viral gastroenteritis the "stomach flu," but this name is not medically correct (Source: NIH).

Main Symptoms

The main symptoms of gastroenteritis include vomiting and diarrhea (Source: NIH). The illness is usually not serious in healthy people, but it can sometimes lead to dehydration or cause severe symptoms (Source: NIH).

When to Contact a Healthcare Provider Immediately

It is important to contact a provider right away if your child has any of the following warning signs (Source: NIH):

Warning Sign Description

Change in mental state Such as irritability or lack of energy

Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days Persistent diarrhea beyond 48 hours

Six or more loose stools in a day High frequency of diarrhea

Severe pain in the abdomen (belly) or rectum Intense abdominal or rectal pain

Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus Sign of possible internal bleeding or infection

Symptoms of dehydration Thirst, dry mouth, headache, dark-colored urine, urinating less than normal

Understanding Dehydration

Dehydration is the most serious complication of a stomach bug. Watch for these signs (Source: NIH):

Thirst – your child may ask for drinks frequently

Dry mouth – lips and mouth appear dry

Headache – may indicate fluid loss

Dark-colored urine – urine is darker than usual

Urinating less than normal – fewer wet diapers or trips to the toilet

Home Care

While the sources provided do not list specific home treatments, the key principle is to prevent dehydration by encouraging your child to drink plenty of fluids. If your child cannot keep fluids down or shows signs of dehydration, seek medical attention.

What Stomach Bug Is Not

It is important not to confuse gastroenteritis with other conditions:

Reflux (GER) occurs when food or stomach acid flows back from your child's stomach into the esophagus. This is different from an infection and does not cause diarrhea (Source: NIH).

Reflux in infants (spitting up) happens when food or milk comes back up. This is normal in many babies and is not the same as a stomach bug (Source: NIH).
