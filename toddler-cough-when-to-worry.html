When to Worry About a Toddler&#x27;s Cough: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Coughs are one of the most common reasons parents take their child to the doctor. While most toddler coughs are harmless and resolve on their own, knowing when to seek medical attention is key.

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your pediatrician if your toddler&#x27;s cough is accompanied by any of the following:

Duration

A cough that lasts more than 3 weeksA cough that gets worse by the third weekA cough and stuffy nose that persist for more than 10 days without improvingFever

A fever over 102°F (38.9°C)A fever that lasts more than 3 daysA fever that returns after being gone for 24 hours or moreBreathing or Chest Symptoms

Wheezing, whistling, or a harsh sound when breathingLabored breathing or muscles pulling in around the neck or ribsChest pain, even when not coughingOther Concerning Signs

Nonstop coughing spellsCoughing that causes vomiting more than three timesEar or sinus painSigns of dehydration (dry mouth, sunken eyes, fewer wet diapers)When to Seek Emergency Care (Call 112/999)

Go to the emergency room immediately if your toddler:

Has severe trouble breathing — struggling for each breath, can barely speak or cryDevelops a blue or dusky color around the lips, face, or nailsIs lethargic — difficult to wake or seems &quot;out of it&quot;Is unconscious or has stopped breathingCoughs up blood or pink-tinged mucusHas severe chest painSpecial Cough Types to Watch For

Barking cough (croup): If your child has a cough that sounds like a seal or small dog barking, especially at night, it may be croup. While many cases can be managed at home with steam, if the cough causes trouble breathing, seek emergency care.

Whooping cough: A cough with a &quot;whoop&quot; sound or that causes vomiting after coughing fits may be pertussis, which requires medical treatment.

COVID: Testing is the only way to tell if a child&#x27;s cough is caused by COVID. If your child has a cough and other COVID symptoms, or has been exposed, consider testing.

Trust your instincts. If your child looks or acts very sick or you think they need to be seen, seek medical care right away.
