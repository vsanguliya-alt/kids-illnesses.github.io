Fever in a 6-Month-Old: A Weight-Based Medication Guide

When your 6-month-old has a fever, it&#x27;s essential to give the correct dose of medication. The single most important rule is to calculate the dose based on your child&#x27;s weight, not their age. This is because children of the same age can vary significantly in size, and giving too little won&#x27;t be effective, while too much can be dangerous .

✅ Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) Dosage

Acetaminophen is generally safe and effective for infants over 3 months old. The standard dose is 10 to 15 mg per kilogram (mg/kg) of body weight, given every 4 to 6 hours as needed . Do not give more than 5 doses in a 24-hour period.

Acetaminophen is commonly available as a 160 mg per 5 mL liquid. The table below provides dosing based on weight :

Always use the dosing syringe or cup that comes with the medicine for accurate measurement .

✅ Ibuprofen Dosage

Ibuprofen is another option for fever and pain relief. Important: Ibuprofen should not be given to infants under 6 months of age unless directed by a doctor . For a 6-month-old, the dose is 5 to 10 mg/kg, given every 6 to 8 hours as needed .

Important Safety Rules

No aspirin: Never give aspirin to a child with a fever.

Consult a doctor for infants: For children under 2 years, it&#x27;s recommended to check with your pediatrician before giving acetaminophen to ensure correct dosing and safety .

Avoid alternating medications: The American Academy of Pediatrics does not recommend routinely alternating between acetaminophen and ibuprofen, as this can increase the risk of dosing errors .

Fever in the first 12 weeks: Fever in a baby under 3 months of age requires immediate medical attention .
