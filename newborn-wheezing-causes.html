What Parents Should Know

While the sources do not provide specific guidance, the most important takeaway for parents is:

Wheezing in a newborn is a sign of respiratory distress and should always be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

Seek emergency care immediately if your newborn has wheezing and:

Difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Flaring nostrils or using chest muscles to breathe (retractions)

Difficulty feeding or poor feeding

Lethargy or difficulty waking

Any fever in a newborn under 3 months old

You are worried about your baby

Common causes of wheezing in newborns (not from sources) can include:

Bronchiolitis – a viral infection of the small airways, most commonly caused by RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus)

Viral infections – such as the common cold or flu

Allergic reactions – to food or environmental triggers

Congenital airway anomalies – structural issues present from birth

Gastroesophageal reflux – stomach acid irritating the airways

Trust your instincts – if you are worried about your newborn, call your health care provider right away. Any breathing difficulty in a newborn should be taken seriously and evaluated promptly.
