Fever in a 9-Month-Old: Recognizing Emergency Signs

Fever is a common sign that your baby&#x27;s body is fighting an infection, and most cases are harmless and resolve on their own. However, it can be difficult to know when a fever signals something more serious. This guide will help you recognize the emergency signs that require immediate medical attention for your 9-month-old.

🚨 When to Go to the Emergency Room or Call 911

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department immediately if your baby has a fever and any of the following:

🩸 Severe Symptoms of Illness

Vomit that is yellow-green, green, or looks like ground coffee .

A stiff neck and eye pain when looking at bright lights .

A sudden, severe headache or tummy ache .

Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or not responding as usual .

Blue, grey, pale, or blotchy skin, lips, or tongue. On brown or black skin, check the palms of the hands and soles of the feet .

🫁 Breathing and Circulation Problems

Severe difficulty breathing: Your baby is gasping, choking, or unable to get words out (babies may make grunting noises or their stomach may suck under their ribcage) .

👀 Other Critical Signs

A seizure (fit): This is a febrile seizure. Call 911 immediately if it is your baby&#x27;s first seizure, if the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, or if your baby has trouble breathing during it. During the seizure, place your baby on their side on a soft surface (recovery position), and do not put anything in their mouth.

A non-blanching rash: A rash that does not fade or turn white when you press a glass against it. This can be a sign of a serious bacterial infection like meningitis.

Signs of severe dehydration: Sunken eyes, no tears when crying, a sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the head, and no wet diapers for 8 to 12 hours.

🟡 When to Call Your Pediatrician Immediately

Contact your baby&#x27;s doctor right away if the fever is accompanied by:

Age-specific temperature: For a 9-month-old, you should call the doctor if the fever is 39°C (102.2°F) or higher or if a fever of any temperature lasts for more than 3 days.

Persistent vomiting or diarrhea: Especially if your baby is unable to keep fluids down.

Signs of dehydration: A dry mouth, cracked lips, no tears, and fewer wet diapers than usual.

Your baby appears very ill: You know your baby best. If they are inconsolable, have an unusually high-pitched cry, or seem &quot;off,&quot; it is always best to call your doctor for advice.

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your baby&#x27;s fever, contacting your pediatrician is always the safest choice.
