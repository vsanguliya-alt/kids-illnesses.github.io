Sore Throat in an 18-Month-Old: Safe Home Remedies

A sore throat is a common symptom of a cold that can make your 18-month-old uncomfortable. A cold can cause a sore throat, a stuffy or runny nose, sneezing, and coughing . Most throat problems are minor and go away on their own . However, treating a sore throat in a toddler requires safe methods, as many medications are not recommended for young children.

What You Can Do at Home

Since you should not give cold or cough medicines to children under two, focus on safe, comforting remedies to help your child . Here are effective ways to relieve sore throat discomfort:

Keep Your Child Hydrated

Offer your child plenty of fluids. Warm (not hot) drinks can be especially soothing. Hydration is key to preventing dehydration and keeping the throat moist.

Provide Comfort with a Humidifier

Use a cool mist humidifier in your child's room . The added moisture can help soothe the irritation in their throat and make it easier for them to breathe, especially if they also have a stuffy nose.

Soothe with Foods

For children over one year old, cool or soft foods can be soothing. Consider offering:

A small amount of warm (not hot) soup or broth.

Cold foods like yogurt or applesauce, which can be comforting for a sore throat.

When to Call the Doctor

Most sore throats get better on their own within a week. However, you should call your health care provider if your child has a sore throat for more than two days or has trouble swallowing, as these can be signs of tonsillitis . Tonsillitis can be caused by viruses or bacteria such as strep throat . Its symptoms can include a severe sore throat and a white or yellow coating on the tonsils . Contact your health care provider if your child has a sore throat for more than two days or has trouble or pain when swallowing .

Also, contact a doctor immediately if the sore throat is accompanied by:

A high fever.

Swollen glands in the neck .

Difficulty breathing or swallowing.

Signs of dehydration.
