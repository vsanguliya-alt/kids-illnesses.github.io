1-Year-Old: Is It a Cold or the Flu?

Determining whether your 1-year-old has a cold or the flu (influenza) can be tricky, but distinguishing between them is important because the flu can be more serious for young children. Here’s how to tell them apart, with key differences coming from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) .

🤧 Cold vs. Flu: Key Differences

The main difference is the sudden onset. Flu symptoms typically start abruptly, while a cold comes on gradually .

Vomiting and diarrhea are also more common with the flu in children .

🩺 What to Do If Your Child Is Sick

If you suspect the flu, contact your pediatrician, especially since children under 5 are at high risk for complications .

Focus on comfort and hydration: Ensure your child gets rest and drinks fluids to prevent dehydration.

Fever and pain: For children over 6 months, your pediatrician may recommend a weight-based dose of acetaminophen or ibuprofen . Never give aspirin to a child.

Call a doctor before giving cold/cough medicines, as they are generally not recommended for children under 4 .

🚨 When to Contact a Doctor

Consult a doctor immediately if your 1-year-old:

Is under 3 months old and has a fever.

Has a fever lasting more than 3 days.

Has difficulty breathing, a stiff neck, or is unusually lethargic.

Shows signs of dehydration (no wet diapers for 8–12 hours, no tears).
