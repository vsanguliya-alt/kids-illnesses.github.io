Fever in Children: A Los Angeles Parent's Guide

Managing a fever in your child can be stressful, especially when navigating the healthcare system in a large city like Los Angeles. Fevers are a common sign that your child's body is fighting off an infection, but knowing when to treat a fever at home versus when to seek care is essential. A fever is a body temperature that is higher than normal. A fever is not a disease. You get a fever because your body is trying to kill the virus or bacteria that caused the infection (Source: NIH).

What Is a Fever?

A fever is a body temperature that is higher than normal. The AAP defines a fever as a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher. While a fever can be uncomfortable for your child, it is a sign that their immune system is working. Other causes of fevers include medicines, heat illness, and autoimmune diseases, but infections are the most common cause (Source: NIH).

When to Call Your Pediatrician or Seek Care

For Infants Under 3 Months: A fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher is a medical emergency. Seek immediate care at an emergency room.

For Children 3 Months to 2 Years: Call your pediatrician if the fever lasts more than 24 hours.

For Children 2 Years and Older: Call your pediatrician if the fever lasts more than 3 days.

When to Go to the Emergency Room

Take your child to the emergency department if they have a fever and any of these signs:

Difficulty breathing or breathing fast.

Dehydration: fewer than 5 wet diapers in 24 hours, or no tears when crying.

Extreme drowsiness or difficulty waking up.

Stiff neck or severe headache.

An unexplained rash.

Blue lips, tongue, or nails.

A seizure (especially if it lasts longer than 5 minutes).

Home Treatment

Treat the child, not the number. If your child has a fever but is still playing, drinking fluids, and acting normally, they may not need medication (Source: NIH). If the fever is very high, your health care provider may recommend taking an over-the-counter medicine such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen (Source: NIH).

Acetaminophen (Tylenol) can be given to children of all ages, with correct dosing. For infants under 3 months, consult your pediatrician before giving any medication.

Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) can be used in children over 6 months old.

Never give aspirin to children.

Keep your child well-hydrated with breast milk, formula, or an electrolyte solution for vomiting. Dress them in light clothing and keep the room cool. Never use cold baths, ice, or alcohol rubs to treat a fever, as they can cause shivering, which raises the body temperature.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for a fever?

Call your doctor immediately if your child is under 3 months old with any fever of 100.4°F or higher. For older children, call if the fever lasts more than 3 days, or if the child appears very ill.

How long does a fever last?

Most fevers from common viral infections last 24 hours to 3days.

What home remedies can help a fever?

Home care focuses on comfort and hydration. Keep your child in light clothing, offer plenty of fluids, and use acetaminophen or ibuprofen if they are uncomfortable. Avoid cold baths and bundling.

What is a febrile seizure?

Febrile seizures can occur in children aged 6 months to 5 years during a rapid rise in temperature. They usually stop within 5 minutes and do not cause brain damage. Call your doctor if one occurs.

Is it safe to alternate acetaminophen and ibuprofen?

Alternating is generally not recommended due to the risk of dosing errors. Choose one medication and use it as directed.
