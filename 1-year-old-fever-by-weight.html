Fever Medication for a 1-Year-Old: A Weight-Based Guide

When your 1-year-old has a fever, knowing the correct dose of medication is essential for safety. Children aren’t simply small adults, and their medicine dose must be based on their weight, not their age, to be both safe and effective. Giving the wrong amount is one of the most common mistakes parents make. This guide provides weight-based dosing information, but always confirm the correct dose with your child's pediatrician.

✅ Why Weight Matters

The most accurate way to determine the right dose of acetaminophen or ibuprofen is by using your child's weight in kilograms or pounds. If you don't know your child's exact weight, you can use their age as a guide, but weight is always preferred. Pediatric formulations are standardized: liquid syrup acetaminophen is 160 mg per 5 mL.

✅ Acetaminophen (Tylenol) Dosage by Weight

Acetaminophen is generally safe for children over 3 months old. The standard single dose is 10 to 15 mg per kilogram of body weight.

✅ Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) Dosage by Weight

Ibuprofen is an alternative for children over 6 months old. The standard single dose is 10 mg per kilogram of body weight.

📏 Dosing Safety Tips

Use the Right Tool: Always use the syringe or cup that comes with the medicine. Never use a kitchen teaspoon to measure, as this can lead to an overdose.

Know Your Child's Weight: Keep a record of your child's most recent weight for accurate dosing. For a 1-year-old, this is typically around 18–23 pounds.

No Aspirin: Never give aspirin to a child with a fever.
