Fever Medication for a 1-Year-Old: A Weight-Based Guide

When your 1-year-old has a fever, knowing the correct dose of medication is essential for safety. The most accurate way to determine the right dose of acetaminophen or ibuprofen is by using your child's weight, not their age. Giving the wrong amount is one of the most common mistakes parents make. This guide provides weight-based dosing information, but always confirm the correct dose with your child's pediatrician.

Acetaminophen (Tylenol) Dosage by Weight

Acetaminophen is generally safe for children over 3 months old. The standard single dose is 10 to 15 mg per kilogram of body weight.

Acetaminophen Dosage Table for 1-Year-Olds (Syrup 160 mg/5 mL)

Weight (lbs) Weight (kg) Dose (mL)

18–23 lbs 8–10 kg 3.75 mL

24–29 lbs 11–13 kg 5 mL

30–35 lbs 14–16 kg 6.25 mL

How Often: Every 4 to 6 hours as needed.

Maximum: No more than 5 doses in 24 hours.

Important: Consult a doctor before giving acetaminophen to a child under 2 years of age to ensure correct dosing and safety.

Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) Dosage by Weight

Ibuprofen is an alternative for children over 6 months old. The standard single dose is 10 mg per kilogram of body weight.

Ibuprofen Dosage Table for 1-Year-Olds (Liquid 100 mg/5 mL)

Weight (lbs) Weight (kg) Dose (mL)

18–23 lbs 8–10 kg 4 mL

24–29 lbs 11–13 kg 5 mL

30–35 lbs 14–16 kg 6.25 mL

How Often: Every 6 to 8 hours as needed.

Maximum: No more than 3 doses in 24 hours.

Important: Do not use ibuprofen in children under 6 months of age.

Dosing Safety Tips

Use the Right Tool: Always use the syringe or cup that comes with the medicine. Never use a kitchen teaspoon to measure, as this can lead to an overdose.

Know Your Child's Weight: Keep a record of your child's most recent weight for accurate dosing. For a 1-year-old, this is typically around 18–23 pounds.

No Aspirin: Never give aspirin to a child with a fever.

Avoid Alternating: The routine alternation of acetaminophen and ibuprofen is not generally recommended, as it increases the risk of dosing errors.
