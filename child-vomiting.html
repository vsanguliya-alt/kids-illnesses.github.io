Child Vomiting: A Parent's Guide

Vomiting is a common symptom in children, and while it can be distressing, it is usually not a sign of a serious problem. Understanding the causes, knowing how to manage it at home, and recognizing warning signs can help you navigate this challenging situation.

Common Causes of Vomiting

Vomiting in children is most often caused by a viral infection of the stomach and intestines (gastroenteritis). Other common causes include:

Gastroenteritis (Stomach Bug): This is the most frequent cause of vomiting and diarrhea in children. It is highly contagious and usually resolves on its own.

Food Poisoning: Eating contaminated food can cause sudden vomiting and diarrhea.

Motion Sickness: Some children are prone to vomiting during car or boat travel.

Coughing: A severe cough can trigger the gag reflex and lead to vomiting.

Ear Infections: An ear infection can cause nausea and vomiting, especially in younger children.

Reye's Syndrome: A very rare but serious condition that can develop after a child recovers from a virus like flu or chickenpox. Symptoms often start 1 to 3 weeks after the illness and include persistent vomiting and changes in behavior (Source: NHS). Never give aspirin to a child with a viral illness, as it has been linked to Reye's syndrome.

Home Care for Vomiting

When your child is vomiting, the most important focus is preventing dehydration. Here’s how to manage at home:

1. Rest the Stomach

Stop eating and drinking for 30 minutes to give the stomach a chance to settle.

Avoid solid foods until the vomiting has stopped for at least 4 hours.

Do not give over-the-counter anti-vomiting (anti-emetic) medications unless prescribed by a doctor.

2. Rehydration

The most effective way to rehydrate a child is to use an oral rehydration solution (ORS) like Pedialyte. These solutions contain the right balance of salt and sugar to replace lost fluids and electrolytes.

If you don't have an ORS, you can offer small sips of water or a diluted fruit juice (half juice, half water). Avoid sugary drinks, which can worsen diarrhea.

For breastfeeding infants, continue to nurse on demand. Breast milk is easily digested and provides excellent hydration.

3. How to Give Fluids

Offer small, frequent amounts of fluid (e.g., 5ml or 1teaspoons) every 10minutes.

Use a syringe, spoon, or medicine cup for precise, small amounts.

If your child keeps the fluid down for 30minutes, gradually increase the amount.

4. Returning to Food

Once your child has not vomited for 4hours and is holding down liquids, you can slowly introduce bland foods such as:

Crackers, toast, or plain bread

Rice or plain pasta

Bananas (mashed)

Applesauce

Avoid fatty, spicy, or sugary foods for a day or two.

When to Call the Doctor or Seek Emergency Care

Contact your pediatrician immediately if your child:

Is under 6 months old and is vomiting.

Has signs of dehydration, such as:

Dry mouth and lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the head (in infants)

Urinating less often (fewer than 6 wet diapers in 24 hours for infants, or no urine for 6hours for older children)

Lethargy or excessive sleepiness

Has been vomiting for more than 24 hours.

Has a fever (especially if over 102°F / 38.9°C).

Has blood or bile (greenish color) in the vomit.

Has severe abdominal pain.

Has a stiff neck or severe headache.

Seek emergency care (call 911) if your child:

Shows signs of severe dehydration (e.g., listless, floppy, or difficult to wake).

Is having trouble breathing.

Has a seizure.

Has a sudden, severe headache or a stiff neck.

Has vomit that contains blood or looks like coffee grounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for my child's vomiting?

Call your doctor if your child is under 6 months old, has signs of dehydration, has been vomiting for more than 24 hours, has a fever, or if the vomit contains blood or bile.

How long does a stomach bug (vomiting) last in a child?

Most viral gastroenteritis causes vomiting that lasts 1 to 3 days, with improvement typically beginning within 12hours.

What home remedies can help with a child's vomiting?

The best home remedy is resting the stomach (no food or drink for 30 minutes) and then offering tiny, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution or water. Never give anti-vomiting medicines without a doctor's prescription.

What is cyclical vomiting syndrome in children?

Cyclical vomiting syndrome is a rare condition that causes severe episodes of vomiting, often starting in children around 5 years old, followed by periods of feeling well.

Can my child drink milk while vomiting?

Avoid milk during the first 12 hours of vomiting, as it can be difficult to digest and may worsen the nausea. Once your child has stopped vomiting and is ready for bland foods, milk can be reintroduced gradually.

Can teething cause vomiting in a baby?

No. Teething is not a direct cause of vomiting. While babies might be fussy or drool more, vomiting should not be attributed to teething and usually indicates a viral or other illness.

How can I prevent my child from getting dehydrated when vomiting?

The key to preventing dehydration is early and frequent rehydration. Use an oral rehydration solution (ORS) to replace lost fluids and electrolytes, offering small amounts every 10minutes.
