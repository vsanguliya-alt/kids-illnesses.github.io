Ear Infections and Antibiotics in Newborns

Ear infections are incredibly common in early childhood. In fact, three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday. The infection usually affects the middle ear and is called otitis media (Source: NIH).

When Are Antibiotics Needed?

Not every ear infection requires antibiotics. Your health care provider will diagnose an ear infection by looking inside the ear with an instrument called an otoscope (Source: NIH).

Antibiotics are typically required in specific situations:

Severe infections – your provider will determine if the infection is severe enough to warrant antibiotics (Source: NIH)

Infections in young babies – newborns and young infants are at higher risk for complications and may require antibiotics (Source: NIH)

Additionally, if your newborn has a perforated eardrum (a hole or tear in the eardrum), they may need antibiotics. A perforated eardrum usually gets better on its own within 2 months, but treatment such as antibiotics may be necessary if you have an ear infection, or to prevent getting an ear infection while the eardrum heals (Source: NHS).

Glue Ear and Antibiotics

Glue ear is a condition where fluid builds up in the middle ear without active infection. A GP should be able to tell if it&#x27;s glue ear by looking for fluid inside the ear. Antibiotics may be prescribed if glue ear causes an ear infection (Source: NHS).

If glue ear lasts a long time, it can affect a child&#x27;s speech development and progress at school (Source: NHS).

Signs of Ear Infection in Newborns

If your newborn isn&#x27;t old enough to say &quot;My ear hurts,&quot; look for these signs (Source: NIH):

Fluid draining from the ear

Tugging or pulling at the ear

Fussiness or irritability

Difficulty sleeping

Fever

Hearing problems, such as:

Being difficult to understand

Asking people to repeat what they say

Asking for the TV or music to be turned up loud

Struggling to hear people far away

Becoming easily distracted when people are talking

Finding it hard to concentrate or seeming tired and irritable because it&#x27;s harder for them to listen (Source: NHS)

Other Causes of Ear Pain

Not all ear pain means an ear infection that needs antibiotics. Other causes include (Source: NHS):

Cause Key Feature

Earwax build-up May cause hearing loss or fullness

Object stuck in the ear Do not try to remove it yourself – see a GP

Perforated eardrum Particularly after a loud noise or accident

Baby teething Pain in gums, not inside ear

Dental abscess Pain with swallowing

Glue ear Fluid in middle ear without infection

Always follow your healthcare provider&#x27;s guidance regarding antibiotics for your newborn&#x27;s ear infection. Never use leftover antibiotics or give medications prescribed for someone else.
