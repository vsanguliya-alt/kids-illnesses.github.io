Cough in an 18-Month-Old: When to Worry

A cough in an 18-month-old is common, often part of a viral cold. While it can be uncomfortable, you don't usually need to treat a cold or the cough that it causes (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). However, there are important signs that indicate a cough may be a sign of a more serious problem requiring immediate medical attention.

🚨 Emergency Signs: When to Call 911 or Go to A&E

Seek emergency care immediately if your child's cough is accompanied by any of the following:

Blue or gray lips or face: This is a sign of low oxygen levels.

Difficulty breathing: This includes fast breathing, working hard to breathe (using chest or neck muscles), or making a "whooping" sound when breathing in after a coughing fit. Whooping cough (pertussis) is a serious respiratory infection that can cause coughing fits, and about half of babies under one year old who get it need hospital care (Source: NIH | Whooping Cough).

Severe coughing fits: Fits of rapid coughing that are followed by a high-pitched "whoop" sound, or coughing that leads to vomiting or exhaustion are signs of a serious infection like whooping cough (Source: NIH | Whooping Cough).

A seizure.

🟡 When to Call Your Pediatrician Urgently

Contact your child's doctor immediately if the cough is accompanied by:

A high fever.

Signs of dehydration, such as fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, or no tears when crying.

A cough that has not improved after a week or is getting worse.

Wheezing or a high-pitched sound when breathing in (stridor).

Symptoms that suggest whooping cough: They may start with cold-like symptoms, but watch for coughing fits that get worse, especially at night, and are followed by a whooping sound, vomiting, or exhaustion (Source: NIH | Whooping Cough).

What You Can Do at Home

For a mild cough, there are safe ways to help your child feel more comfortable at home:

Do not give cough medicine to children under two years old (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Use a cool-mist humidifier in your child's room (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Use saline nose drops and a bulb syringe to clear a stuffy nose, which can help reduce coughing (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Always trust your instincts. If you are worried about your child's cough, contact your pediatrician for advice.
