Preschooler Rash with Fever: What Parents Need to Know

A rash accompanied by a fever is a common combination in young children that often indicates a viral or bacterial infection. While many of these illnesses are mild and self-limiting, understanding the specific characteristics of the rash can help you know when to seek medical care.

Scarlet Fever: A Bacterial Cause

One of the more notable causes of a rash with fever in a preschooler is scarlet fever. Scarlet fever is a contagious infection that mostly affects young children. The first signs can include flu-like symptoms, such as a high temperature, a sore throat, and swollen neck glands. A rash appears 12 to 48 hours later. The rash makes your skin feel rough, like sandpaper (Source: NHS).

Key features of the scarlet fever rash:

On white skin, the rash looks pink or red

On brown and black skin, it might be harder to see a change in colour, but you can still feel the rash and see the raised bumps (Source: NHS)

A white coating also appears on the tongue (Source: NHS)

Viral Causes: Fifth Disease

Fifth disease (erythema infectiosum), caused by parvovirus B19, is a common viral illness in preschool-aged children. It often causes a bright red rash on their cheeks, sometimes called a &quot;slapped cheek&quot; rash (Source: NIH). The rash can spread to the arms, legs, and trunk with a lacy appearance. Fifth disease may be accompanied by a mild fever, runny nose, or headache. The illness is usually mild and resolves on its own.

Other Common Viral Rashes

Many viral infections in children cause a rash with fever. These rashes are often described as &quot;viral exanthems&quot; and can appear as red spots or bumps all over the body. The rash usually appears after the fever has been present for a few days and is generally harmless.

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider if your child has a rash with fever and:

The rash does not fade when pressed with a glass (non-blanching rash) – this requires immediate medical attention

Your child has difficulty breathing

Your child is lethargic or difficult to wake

Your child has a stiff neck

Your child is not drinking fluids or shows signs of dehydration

The fever lasts more than 3 days

The rash is painful, blistered, or oozing

You are worried about your child
