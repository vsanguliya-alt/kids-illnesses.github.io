Newborn Constipation Relief: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Constipation in newborns is defined as having hard, dry, pellet-like poos that are difficult and painful to pass, often accompanied by straining and crying . While babies may grunt and strain normally, if your newborn cries in pain or passes hard stools, they are likely constipated .

Recognizing Normal Patterns

Newborns typically pass stool every day for the first month or two . However, patterns vary: breastfed babies often poo more frequently than formula-fed babies . Your baby is not constipated if their poo is soft, even if they have not passed stool for 1days .

Safe Home Remedies

Always consult your pediatrician before trying any remedy, especially for a newborn. If recommended, gentle options include:

For babies 1 month and older: Try a small amount of water (or apple/pear juice, which contains sorbitol that softens stool). For babies over 3 months, prune juice may be used. Limit juice to 120 ml or less .

Gently massage your baby&#x27;s tummy to help stimulate bowels .

Bicycle leg movements: Gently move your baby&#x27;s legs as if pedaling a bike, or hold knees to chest and release, to help with bowel movement .

What to Avoid

Never use mineral oil, stimulant laxatives, or enemas to treat newborn constipation . Do not give juice to babies under 1 month old.

When to Call a Doctor Immediately

Contact your healthcare provider right away if your constipated newborn has:

A swollen or bloated tummy Vomiting (especially green) or fever Not eating and seems unusually tired Not passed their first poo (meconium) within 48 hours of birth Blood in their poo A very pale stool color Is not gaining weight or losing weight In rare cases, constipation with these symptoms can be linked to serious conditions like Hirschsprung disease .
