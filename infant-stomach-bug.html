What Parents Should Know

While the sources do not provide specific guidance, the most important concern with any infant gastrointestinal illness is dehydration. Infants can become dehydrated quickly, which can be dangerous.

Signs of dehydration in infants include:

Fewer wet diapers than usual (less than 6 in 24 hours)

Dry mouth and lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot on the head

Unusual drowsiness or irritability

When to seek medical help:

Your baby is under 3 months old with any vomiting or diarrhea

Your baby is not feeding well or refusing to eat

Your baby has signs of dehydration

Your baby has a fever

Vomiting or diarrhea lasts more than 24 hours in an infant

You see blood in the stool or vomit

You are worried about your baby

Trust your instincts – if you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away.
