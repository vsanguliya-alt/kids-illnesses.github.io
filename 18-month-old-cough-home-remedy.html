18-Month-Old Cough: Safe Home Remedies

Coughing is a common symptom in toddlers and is often part of a viral cold. While it can be uncomfortable for your child, there are safe and effective ways to help them feel better at home. It's important to remember that you should not give cold or cough medicines to children under two years old (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Safe Home Care for a Cough

These remedies are safe, gentle, and can provide significant relief for your toddler's cough.

1. Use a Cool Mist Humidifier

Adding moisture to the air is one of the most effective ways to soothe a cough. Use a cool mist humidifier in your child's room, especially while they are sleeping (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). The moist air helps to ease dryness and irritation in the airways, making it easier for them to breathe.

2. Use Saline Nose Drops and Nasal Suctioning

A cough is often worse when a stuffy nose causes mucus to drip down the back of the throat (post-nasal drip). Use saline nose drops or sprays to loosen mucus and then use a bulb syringe to suction it out (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). This is a safe and effective method to clear your child's airways and reduce coughing.

3. Keep Your Child Comfortable

Elevating your child's head slightly while they sleep can help with post-nasal drip, but always follow safe sleep guidelines for a toddler.

4. Offer Plenty of Fluids

Keeping your child well-hydrated is essential, especially with any illness. Offer them plenty of water, milk, or warm, clear fluids to soothe their throat.

When to Call the Doctor

While most coughs are mild and resolve on their own, it is important to know the signs that require medical attention. Call your child's health care provider if the cough:

Is accompanied by a high fever.

Has not improved after a few days.

Is causing difficulty breathing. Seek emergency care immediately if your child is working hard to breathe, breathing faster than usual, or has a "whooping" sound when coughing. Whooping cough is a serious infection that can cause coughing fits and require hospitalization in young children (Source: NIH | Whooping Cough).
