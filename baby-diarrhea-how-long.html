Baby Diarrhea: How Long It Lasts and What to Do

Diarrhea in a baby is common and often short-lived, but it requires careful attention. Understanding the typical duration of diarrhea and knowing when to seek help is essential for your baby&#x27;s health.

Understanding the Duration

Acute diarrhea—which is diarrhea that lasts a short time—is a common problem and usually lasts about one or two days, but it may last longer . In young babies, a common cause of diarrhea is a virus like rotavirus, which is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children . For rotavirus infections specifically, vomiting and diarrhea may last from three to eight days . These viral infections typically run their course within this timeframe.

Causes of Diarrhea in Babies

Diarrhea in babies can be caused by various factors. It&#x27;s important to note that certain foods can trigger diarrhea in sensitive infants. When you introduce one new food at a time, you will be able to identify any foods that cause allergies in your baby. Allergic reactions include a rash, diarrhea, or vomiting . Some harmful bacteria, like E. coli, can also cause severe diarrhea . Babies under 1 year old should not be given juice because it contains a lot of sugar, which can worsen diarrhea .

Signs of Dehydration and When to Call the Doctor

Infants are especially vulnerable to dehydration because they have smaller fluid reserves. Signs of dehydration in a baby include:

Sunken eyes

A sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the top of the head

No wet diapers for 3 hours or more

No tears when crying

Dry mouth or lips

You should contact your child&#x27;s healthcare provider immediately if your baby&#x27;s diarrhea:

Lasts more than a few days . If acute diarrhea persists beyond a few days, it could be a sign of a more serious problem.

Is accompanied by a high fever .

Causes signs of dehydration, such as a dry mouth, no tears when crying, or fewer wet diapers.

Contains blood or pus .

Is associated with severe abdominal pain (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

What You Can Do at Home

While your baby has diarrhea, you can help them feel better by:

Keeping them hydrated by offering small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution (like Pedialyte) or breastmilk/formula.

Monitoring their wet diapers to ensure they are not becoming dehydrated.

Contacting your pediatrician for advice, especially if you are worried about your baby&#x27;s health.
