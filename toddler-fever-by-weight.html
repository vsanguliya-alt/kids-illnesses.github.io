Dosing Fever Medication for Toddlers: By Weight, Not Age

When your toddler has a fever, giving the correct dose of medication is essential for safety and effectiveness. The most accurate way to determine the right dose is by your child&#x27;s weight in kilograms (kg), not their age . If you do not know your child&#x27;s weight, you can use age as a guide, but weight is always preferred .

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) Dosing

Paracetamol is a safe and effective first-line medication for reducing fever and pain in toddlers .

Standard single dose: 10–15 mg per kg of body weight per dose . For liquid paracetamol, common concentrations are 120 mg/5 mL or 160 mg/5 mL—always check the label.

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 60–75 mg per kg per day . For example, a 15 kg toddler (approx. 33 lbs) would have a daily maximum of 900–1125 mg. Do not give more than 4 doses in 24 hours .

Frequency: Give every 4–6 hours as needed . Wait at least 4 hours between doses.

Age restrictions: Paracetamol is not recommended for children under 2 years without a doctor&#x27;s guidance . For children under 3 months, always consult a doctor first .

Example for a 15 kg toddler (about 33 lbs):

Using liquid paracetamol 160 mg/5 mL :

Dose: 15 mg × 15 kg = 225 mg per dose

Volume: (225 mg / 160 mg) × 5 mL = 7 mL

Ibuprofen Dosing

Ibuprofen is also effective for fever and pain in toddlers .

Standard single dose: 5–10 mg per kg of body weight per dose . A common starting dose is 7.5 mg/kg .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 30–40 mg per kg per day . Do not give more than 3 doses in 24 hours .

Frequency: Give every 6–8 hours as needed . Wait at least 6 hours between doses.

Age restrictions: Do not give ibuprofen to children under 6 months of age unless directed by a doctor .

Example for a 15 kg toddler (about 33 lbs):

Using children&#x27;s ibuprofen liquid 100 mg/5 mL:

Dose: 7.5 mg × 15 kg = 112.5 mg per dose

Volume: (112.5 mg / 100 mg) × 5 mL = 5.6 mL

Important Safety Rules

Choose one medication. For most children, the American Academy of Pediatrics does not recommend alternating between paracetamol and ibuprofen, as it increases the risk of dosing errors .

Use the correct measuring tool. Always use the syringe or dosing cup that comes with the medicine. Never use a kitchen teaspoon or tablespoon .

Check for combination products. Do not give medicines with more than one active ingredient to children under 6 years of age .

Treat the child, not the number. The decision to give fever medication should be based on your child&#x27;s discomfort, not solely on the thermometer reading . Antipyretics are meant to improve well-being, not to normalize temperature.

If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away.
