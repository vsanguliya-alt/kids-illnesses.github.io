Preschooler Diarrhea: How Long Does It Last?

Diarrhea is a common symptom in preschoolers, defined as having loose, watery stools three or more times in one day (Source: NIH). The duration depends on the underlying cause, but most cases are acute and resolve within a few days.

Typical Duration

For viral infections, such as rotavirus—the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children—vomiting and diarrhea may last from three to eight days (Source: NIH). Rotavirus is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children (Source: NIH).

Causes of Diarrhea

The most common causes of diarrhea include (Source: NIH):

Bacteria from contaminated food or water

Viruses such as the flu, norovirus, or rotavirus

Diseases that affect the stomach, small intestine, or colon, such as Crohn&#x27;s disease

Problems with how the colon functions, such as irritable bowel syndrome

Some people also get diarrhea after stomach surgery, because sometimes the surgeries can cause food to move through your digestive system more quickly (Source: NIH).

Acute vs. Chronic Diarrhea

Acute diarrhea is diarrhea that lasts a short time (Source: NIH)

Chronic diarrhea – diarrhea that lasts at least four weeks – can be a symptom of a chronic disease. Chronic diarrhea symptoms may be continual, or they may come and go (Source: NIH)

Diarrhea lasting more than a few days may be a sign of a more serious problem (Source: NIH).

Signs of Dehydration to Watch For

The most serious complication of diarrhea in preschoolers is dehydration. Monitor your child closely for:

Decreased urination

Dry mouth or lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes

Unusual drowsiness or irritability

Thirst

When to Call the Doctor

It is important to contact a provider right away if your child has any of the following (Source: NIH):

Change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy

Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days

High fever

Vomiting often

Six or more loose stools in a day

Severe pain in the abdomen (belly) or rectum

Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus

Symptoms of dehydration, such as thirst, dry mouth, headache, dark-colored urine, and urinating less than normal

What to Do at Home

Continue offering plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration

Offer oral rehydration solutions if your child is at risk of dehydration

Offer bland foods as tolerated (BRAT diet: bananas, rice, applesauce, toast)

Avoid sugary drinks and juices, which can worsen diarrhea

Never give anti-diarrheal medications to children without a doctor&#x27;s prescription
