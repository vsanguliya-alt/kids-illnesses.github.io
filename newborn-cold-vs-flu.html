Cold vs. Flu in Newborns: What Parents Need to Know

The common cold is a mild infection of your upper respiratory tract, which includes your nose and throat. More than 200 different viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common type (Source: NIH). The flu (influenza) is a different illness that can be serious or even deadly, especially for newborn babies and people with certain chronic illnesses (Source: NIH).

Why Newborns Are at Higher Risk

Newborn babies have immature immune systems and are more vulnerable to severe infections. The flu can be especially dangerous for them, leading to complications such as pneumonia, dehydration, and worsening of underlying conditions (Source: NIH). This is why it is critical to take any respiratory symptoms in a newborn seriously.

Cold Symptoms

The symptoms of a common cold usually start a few days after you become infected with the virus (Source: NIH). Cold symptoms are generally milder than flu symptoms and may include:

Runny or stuffy nose

Sneezing

Cough

Mild fever (in some cases)

Flu Symptoms

The flu comes on more suddenly and is often more severe. While the sources do not list specific flu symptoms, they emphasize that the flu can be serious for newborns (Source: NIH). Symptoms may include high fever, severe cough, body aches, and extreme tiredness.

Telling the Difference

Sometimes people have trouble figuring out whether they have a cold or the flu (Source: NIH). In newborns, telling the difference can be especially difficult because babies cannot describe their symptoms. The key difference is often the severity: the flu tends to come on suddenly and cause more severe symptoms, while a cold develops gradually and is milder.

When to Call the Doctor

It can be scary when your baby is sick, especially when it is not an everyday problem like a cold or a fever (Source: NIH). You should always contact your healthcare provider if your newborn has any signs of illness, including:

Fever (temperature of 38°C / 100.4°F or higher)

Cough

Difficulty breathing

Poor feeding

Unusual fussiness or lethargy

Any symptoms that concern you

Important Safety Rules

Do not give cold or cough medicines to children under two years of age (Source: NIH)

Talk with your child&#x27;s health care provider before giving your child any cold and cough medicines (Source: NIH)

Never give aspirin to a child (Source: NIH)

Treatment

There is no cure for the common cold, but there are treatments that can make you feel better while you wait for the cold to go away on its own (Source: NIH). For newborns, the best &quot;treatment&quot; is supportive care: ensuring adequate fluids, keeping them comfortable, and monitoring for any warning signs. The flu may require antiviral medications, especially in newborns, so prompt medical evaluation is essential.
