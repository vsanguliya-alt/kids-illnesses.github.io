Ear Infection Antibiotics for a 6-Month-Old: What Parents Need to Know

The Wait-and-See Approach

Ear infections (acute otitis media) are extremely common in babies. In fact, five out of six children will experience an ear infection before their third birthday . The good news is that most ear infections go away on their own without antibiotic treatment .

For babies under 6 months, however, the approach is different. Babies in this age group almost always receive antibiotics . This is because:

They are not yet fully vaccinated against certain bacteria

There is no research about the safety of skipping antibiotics for babies under 6 months

Complications from ear infections can be more severe in young babies, as bacteria trapped behind the eardrum can spread to other parts of the body When Antibiotics Are Recommended

For babies over 6 months, the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends a "wait-and-see" approach for infections that are not severe . The best course is often to watch the child for 2 to 3 days before prescribing antibiotics .

Antibiotics are generally recommended immediately if your baby has:

Severe pain Constant high fevers (higher than 39°C / 102.2°F) Fluid or discharge from the ear An infection in both ears (if under 2 years of age) First-Line Antibiotic Treatment

If antibiotics are needed, amoxicillin is almost always the first choice . It is effective against the most common bacteria that cause ear infections.

The recommended dose for children is typically 90 mg per kilogram of body weight per day, divided into two or three doses . A 5-day course of antibiotics is usually sufficient for most ear infections .

When to Seek Emergency Care

Seek emergency medical attention immediately if your baby has:

Swelling, redness, or tenderness behind the ear (could indicate mastoiditis) Neck pain or stiffness Lethargy or unusual irritability A stiff neck, confusion, or is difficult to wake Severe headache Facial weakness or drooping
