Baby Cold: A Parent's Guide to Symptoms, Care, and When to Seek Help

It can be scary when your baby is sick, especially when it is not an everyday problem like a cold or a fever (Source: NIH). The common cold is one of the most frequent illnesses in babies. While it is usually mild, it can be concerning for parents. This guide provides evidence-based information on how to recognize a cold in your baby, how to care for them at home, and when to call the doctor.

What is a Common Cold?

A cold is a viral infection of the upper respiratory tract, which includes the nose and throat. More than 200 different viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common type. Babies are particularly susceptible to colds because their immune systems are not yet fully developed. It is normal for a baby to have several colds in their first year.

Symptoms of a Cold in Babies

Cold symptoms usually start a few days after exposure to the virus. In babies, symptoms can include:

Runny or stuffy nose

Sneezing

Cough

Slight fever (not always present)

Fussiness or irritability

Poor feeding (because they can't breathe well)

Difficulty sleeping

Red, watery eyes

How to Care for Your Baby at Home

There is no cure for the common cold. Treatment focuses on making your baby comfortable while their body fights the virus. Do not give cold or cough medicines to children under two (Source: NIH). Here are safe and effective ways to help your baby:

1. Keep Nasal Passages Clear

A stuffy nose is a major source of discomfort. You can use:

Saline nose drops or sprays: Place a few drops in each nostril to loosen mucus.

Nasal suctioning with a bulb syringe: Gently suction out the loosened mucus after using saline drops.

2. Use a Cool-Mist Humidifier

Dry air can irritate a baby's airways. Adding moisture to the air with a cool-mist humidifier can help ease coughing and nasal congestion. Be sure to clean the humidifier regularly to prevent mold growth.

3. Ensure Adequate Hydration

Keep your baby well-hydrated by offering breast milk or formula more frequently than usual. Staying hydrated helps thin mucus and prevents dehydration.

4. Rest

Encourage your baby to rest. Sleep is essential for the body to heal.

What to Avoid

Do not give over-the-counter cold medicines to babies under 2 years of age (Source: NIH).

Avoid using honey in any form for babies under 12 months of age due to the risk of infant botulism.

Do not use vapor rubs on babies under 2 years of age.

Do not give aspirin to any child for a cold or viral illness.

When to Call the Doctor

It is important to know the warning signs for more serious problems (Source: NIH). Contact your baby's healthcare provider if your baby:

Is under 3 months old and has any signs of a cold (fever, runny nose, cough).

Has a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher (for babies under 3 months) or a fever lasting more than 3 days for older babies.

Is not feeding well or has signs of dehydration (fewer wet diapers).

Has trouble breathing or is breathing rapidly.

Has a cough that is getting worse or lasts more than a week.

Is unusually sleepy or difficult to wake.

Has ear pain (pulling at ears) or persistent crying.

Trust your intuition - if you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH).

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for my baby's cold?

Call your doctor if your baby is under 3 months old with any cold symptoms, has a fever, difficulty breathing, is not feeding well, or if you are worried about your baby.

How long does a baby cold usually last?

A cold typically lasts for 7 to 10 days in a baby. The symptoms may peak around days 3 to 5 and then gradually improve.

What home remedies can help a baby's cold?

Home remedies include using a cool-mist humidifier, using saline nose drops and a bulb syringe to clear a stuffy nose, and offering extra breast milk or formula.

Can I give my baby cough medicine?

No. Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines should not be given to children under 2 years of age due to the risk of serious side effects.

How can I help my baby sleep with a cold?

Elevate the head of their crib (with a towel under the mattress) and use a cool-mist humidifier in their room to help them breathe easier.

Is breast milk good for a baby with a cold?

Yes, breast milk is excellent for a baby with a cold. It provides essential fluids, comfort, and antibodies to help fight the infection.

What are the signs of dehydration in a baby with a cold?

Signs of dehydration include fewer wet diapers (less than 6 in 24 hours for infants), a dry mouth, no tears when crying, and unusual sleepiness or lethargy.
