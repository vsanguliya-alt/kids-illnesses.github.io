Stomach Bug in a 3-Year-Old: Home Care and When to Seek Help

Stomach bugs (gastroenteritis) are common in young children. The illness is typically caused by viruses and leads to vomiting and diarrhea. While seeing your child sick is concerning, most cases can be managed at home. The most important goal is to prevent dehydration, which happens when a child loses more fluid than they take in.

How to Care for Your Child at Home

The best way to help your child recover is to provide small amounts of fluid frequently. Start with 1 teaspoon (5 mL) every 5 minutes. If they keep that down, gradually increase the amount. For a 3-year-old, good options include:

Oral Rehydration Solutions (ORS) like Pedialyte or Dioralyte.

Water, given little and often.

Breast milk or formula for infants.

Avoid fruit juice and fizzy drinks, as they can make diarrhea worse.

Food can wait. Do not worry if your child does not want to eat for a while. Once vomiting has stopped for 8 hours, you can slowly start offering bland foods like toast, rice, or applesauce.

When to Call Your Pediatrician

Call your child&#x27;s doctor right away if your 3-year-old has any of these signs, which could indicate dehydration or a more serious problem:

No wet diaper for 6 to 8 hours.

Sunken eyes.

Dry mouth or lips, or crying without tears.

Vomiting that lasts more than 24 hours.

High fever (over 102°F / 38.9°C).

Bloody diarrhea or vomit.

Severe or worsening tummy pain.

🚨 Seek Immediate Emergency Care If Your Child Has:

These are signs of a serious condition:

Green or bloody vomit.

Being very sleepy, confused, or hard to wake up.

Breathing very fast or struggling to breathe.

A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass (the &quot;glass test&quot;).
