Fever in Children: When to Call the Doctor

Fever is one of the most common reasons parents worry about their child. While most fevers are harmless and resolve on their own, there are clear guidelines on when you should call your healthcare provider.

The Most Important Rule

Trust your intuition. If you are worried about your baby or toddler, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH). This is the single most important piece of advice for parents.

When to Call the Doctor for a Toddler (1–3 Years)

If you are worried about your toddler—for any reason—call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH). You do not need to wait for a specific temperature threshold if your child appears unwell.

When to Call the Doctor for an Infant

For babies, the advice is even more urgent. Trust your intuition – if you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH).

General Guidelines for Seeking Medical Advice

While the sources provided do not specify exact temperature cutoffs, they emphasize the following principles:

Situation Action

You are worried about your child Call your health care provider right away – do not hesitate

Your child appears unusually irritable or lethargic Seek medical advice

Your child shows signs of dehydration (dry mouth, no urine output) Call your provider

Fever persists beyond 3 days Contact your provider for guidance

Specific Conditions Related to Fever

Rheumatic fever is a very rare complication that can develop after a bacterial throat infection. If you or your child are diagnosed with rheumatic fever, you&#x27;ll have treatment to relieve the symptoms (Source: NHS). While rare, this highlights why a fever accompanied by a sore throat should be evaluated by a healthcare provider.

When to Seek Emergency Care (Not Just a Call)

Call emergency services immediately if your child has:

Difficulty breathing

Blue or grey skin, tongue, or lips

Sudden confusion or drowsiness

Stiff neck

Seizure

Signs of severe dehydration

Key Takeaway

You do not need to wait for a specific temperature to call the doctor. If your child looks or acts unwell, or if you are simply worried, contact your healthcare provider. This is especially important for infants under 3 months, where fever can be a sign of serious infection.
