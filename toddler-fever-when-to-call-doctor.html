Toddler Fever: When to Call the Doctor

If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH). Fever is a common symptom in toddlers, and most fevers are harmless and caused by viral infections. However, there are specific guidelines on when you should call your healthcare provider or seek emergency care.

When to Call Your Healthcare Provider

You should call your pediatrician or family doctor if your toddler has a fever and any of the following:

Fever lasting more than 3 days – a persistent fever needs medical evaluation (Source: NIH).

Temperature of 104°F (40°C) or higher – this is considered a high fever and should be evaluated.

Not drinking fluids well – signs of dehydration include dry mouth, no tears, or fewer wet diapers.

Unusual behavior – your child is unusually fussy, irritable, lethargic, or difficult to wake.

Complaints of severe pain – such as a severe headache, earache, or stomach pain.

Has a chronic medical condition – such as asthma, diabetes, or heart disease.

A rash – any new rash with a fever should be evaluated.

Vomiting or diarrhea – especially if unable to keep fluids down.

You are worried – trust your instincts. If you are concerned, call your healthcare provider (Source: NIH).

When to Seek Emergency Care (Call 112/999)

Seek emergency care immediately if your toddler has a fever and:

Difficulty breathing – fast breathing, struggling to breathe, or grunting.

Blue or pale skin – lips, tongue, or skin turn blue or pale.

Stiff neck – unable to touch chin to chest.

Non-blanching rash – a rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass.

Seizure – a febrile seizure, especially if lasting more than 5 minutes.

Extreme drowsiness – difficult to wake or confused.

Signs of severe dehydration – no wet diaper for 8hours, sunken eyes, very dry lips.
