Child Sore Throat: A Parent's Guide

A sore throat is a common complaint in children. It is often a symptom of a viral or bacterial infection. This guide provides evidence-based information on recognizing, managing, and knowing when to seek medical care for your child's sore throat.

Causes of Sore Throat

The throat is a tube that carries food to your esophagus and air to your windpipe and larynx. Most sore throats in children are caused by viral infections, such as the common cold or flu. These will usually get better on their own without medical treatment.

However, a sore throat can also be caused by a bacterial infection, most commonly strep throat. Bacterial infections such as strep throat can also cause tonsillitis, an infection of the tonsils (the lumps of tissue at the back of the throat) (Source: NIH). Almost every child in the United States gets tonsillitis at least once (Source: NIH).

Symptoms of Tonsillitis

The symptoms of tonsillitis include (Source: NIH):

A sore throat, which may be severe

A white or yellow coating on the tonsils

Swollen glands in the neck

Pain or trouble when swallowing

When to Call the Doctor

You should call your health care provider if your child (Source: NIH):

Has a sore throat for more than two days

Has trouble or pain when swallowing

Feels very sick or very weak

When to Seek Emergency Care

You should get emergency care right away if your child (Source: NIH):

Has a lot of trouble swallowing

Diagnosis

To diagnose tonsillitis, your child's health care provider will first ask about your child's symptoms and medical history. The provider will look at your child's throat and neck, checking for things such as redness or white spots on the tonsils and swollen lymph nodes. Your child will probably also have one or more tests to check for strep throat, since it can cause tonsillitis and it requires treatment (Source: NIH).

Home Care for Sore Throat

For a mild sore throat, you can help your child feel better with these home remedies:

Encourage rest: Sleep is essential for recovery.

Offer plenty of fluids: Water, warm tea, or broth can soothe the throat and prevent dehydration.

Provide soft foods: Offer easy-to-swallow foods like soup, yogurt, or applesauce.

Use a cool-mist humidifier: Moist air can soothe a sore throat.

Important Complications

Rheumatic fever is a very rare complication that can develop after a bacterial throat infection. If you've had rheumatic fever once, it's more likely to come back, so make sure to get sore throats treated early (Source: NHS). Symptoms of rheumatic fever usually appear 1 to 5 weeks after you've had a bacterial throat infection and can include joint pain, chest pain, and jerky movements (Source: NHS).

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for my child's sore throat?

Call your doctor if your child has a sore throat for more than two days, has trouble swallowing, or feels very sick or weak.

How long does a sore throat last in a child?

A viral sore throat usually lasts 3 to 7 days and often improves without treatment. A bacterial sore throat (strep throat) may require antibiotics to clear up.

What home remedies can help a child's sore throat?

Provide rest, plenty of fluids, soft foods, and use a cool-mist humidifier. For older children, warm saltwater gargles can help, but do not give honey to children under one year old.

Is my child's sore throat contagious?

Yes, most sore throats caused by viruses or bacteria (like strep throat) are contagious. Practice good handwashing and avoid sharing food and drinks.

What are the signs of strep throat in a child?

Signs include a severe sore throat, fever, swollen lymph nodes, and white patches on the tonsils. A rapid strep test can confirm the diagnosis.

Can tonsillitis be treated at home?

Mild viral tonsillitis can be managed at home with rest, fluids, and over-the-counter pain relief. However, bacterial tonsillitis requires antibiotics prescribed by a doctor.

What is rheumatic fever and how is it related to a sore throat?

Rheumatic fever is a rare but serious complication of untreated strep throat. It can cause joint pain, heart problems, and other symptoms, so it's important to treat bacterial throat infections promptly.
