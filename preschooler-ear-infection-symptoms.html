Ear Infection Symptoms in Preschoolers: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Ear infections are common in young children. The symptoms of an ear infection usually start quickly and include pain inside the ear (earache), a feeling of pressure or fullness inside the ear, itching and irritation in and around the ear, and scaly skin in and around the ear. In some cases, you may also notice discharge running out of the ear (Source: NHS).

Symptoms in Preschoolers

Preschoolers may be able to tell you their ear hurts, but they may not always describe it clearly. Look for these additional signs that may indicate an ear infection (Source: NHS):

Tugging or pulling at the ear

Fussiness or irritability – more than usual

Difficulty sleeping – lying down can increase ear pressure and pain

Fever – a high temperature

Poor feeding or loss of appetite – refusing to eat or drink

Crying more than usual – especially when lying down or swallowing

Hearing problems, such as:

Being difficult to understand when speaking

Asking people to repeat what they say

Asking for the TV or music to be turned up loud

Struggling to hear people far away

Becoming easily distracted when people are talking

Finding it hard to concentrate or seeming tired and irritable because it&#x27;s harder for them to listen (Source: NHS)

What Else Could Be Causing Ear Pain?

Not all ear pain means an ear infection. Other causes of ear pain include (Source: NHS):

Cause Key Feature

Glue ear Fluid in the middle ear without infection; may cause hearing problems

Earwax build-up May cause hearing loss or fullness

Object stuck in the ear Do not try to remove it yourself – see a GP

Perforated eardrum Particularly after a loud noise or accident

Baby teething Pain in gums, not inside ear

Dental abscess Pain with swallowing

Glue Ear in Preschoolers

Glue ear is much more common in children. It occurs when fluid builds up in the middle ear without active infection. Other symptoms may include hearing sounds like ringing or buzzing (tinnitus) (Source: NHS). If glue ear lasts a long time, it can affect a child&#x27;s speech development and progress at school (Source: NHS). A GP should be able to tell if it&#x27;s glue ear by looking for fluid inside the ear (Source: NHS).

Perforated Eardrum

Symptoms of a perforated eardrum usually start suddenly after an injury (such as getting hit on your ear) or a sudden change in air pressure (such as flying on a plane). Most of the time symptoms affect 1 ear and include a ringing or buzzing sound (Source: NHS).

Types of Ear Problems

Ear infections can be caused by viral or bacterial infections. However, ear problems can also be caused by:

Viruses like colds and flu

Something irritating the ear canal, such as eczema, water, or wearing earplugs

Fungal or bacterial infections

Inner ear problems like labyrinthitis (Source: NHS)

Duration of Symptoms

Most ear infections clear up within 3 days, although sometimes symptoms can last up to a week (Source: NHS).

When to See a Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider if your preschooler has:

Ear pain with a change in hearing

Ear pain with pain when swallowing

Ear pain with a high temperature

Discharge running out of the ear

Symptoms that last more than 3 days
