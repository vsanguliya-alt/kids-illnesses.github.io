Understanding the Common Cold

The common cold is a mild infection of your upper respiratory tract, which includes the nose and throat (Source: NIH). More than 200 different viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common type (Source: NIH).

How Colds Spread:

You could get a cold after you shake hands with someone who has a cold or touch a doorknob that has the germs on it, and then touch your face (Source: NIH).

Cold Symptoms:

The symptoms usually start a few days after you become infected with the virus (Source: NIH).

Treatment for Colds:

There is no cure for the common cold. However, there are treatments that can make you feel better while you wait for the cold to go away on its own:

Drinking plenty of fluids

Taking over-the-counter pain or cold and cough medicines (Source: NIH)

Important Safety Warning:

Some cold and cough medicines contain ingredients that are not recommended for children. Talk with your child's health care provider before giving your child any cold and cough medicines (Source: NIH).

Understanding the Flu (Influenza)

The flu is a different illness from the common cold. Sometimes people have trouble figuring out whether they have a cold or the flu (Source: NIH). The provided source does not list specific flu symptoms, but it does acknowledge that confusion between the two is common.
