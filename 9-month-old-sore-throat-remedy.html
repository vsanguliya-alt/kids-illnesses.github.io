9-Month-Old Sore Throat: Safe Home Remedies

A sore throat in a 9-month-old can be a concerning symptom, often part of a viral cold. It's important to know that many medications are not recommended for babies, so focusing on safe, comforting remedies is the best approach. A sore throat can occur with a stuffy or runny nose and coughing.

What You Can Do at Home

Since you should not give cold or cough medicines to children under two, focus on safe ways to help your baby feel more comfortable. Here are effective ways to relieve sore throat discomfort:

Keep Your Baby Hydrated

Offer your baby plenty of fluids. Breast milk or formula should be offered frequently. If your baby is over 6 months, you can also give small sips of water. Hydration is key to preventing dehydration and keeping the throat moist.

Provide Comfort with a Humidifier

Use a cool mist humidifier in your baby's room. The added moisture can help soothe the irritation in their throat and make it easier for them to breathe, especially if they also have a stuffy nose.

Keep Air Clean and Clear

Use saline nose drops or sprays and nasal suctioning with a bulb syringe to clear a stuffy nose. This can help if the sore throat is exacerbated by post-nasal drip.

When to Call the Doctor

While most sore throats get better on their own within a week, you should call your health care provider if your baby has a sore throat or if they have trouble swallowing. Sore throat can be a symptom of tonsillitis, which may also include swollen glands in the neck.

Also, contact a doctor immediately if the sore throat is accompanied by:

A high fever.

Swollen glands in the neck.

Difficulty breathing or swallowing.

Signs of dehydration (fewer wet diapers, no tears, dry mouth).
