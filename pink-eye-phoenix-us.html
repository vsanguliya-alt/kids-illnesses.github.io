Pink Eye in Children: A Phoenix Parent's Guide

Pink eye, also known as conjunctivitis, is one of the most common eye conditions in children . It refers to an inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin, transparent layer of tissue that covers the white part of the eye and lines the inner eyelid . The condition causes the blood vessels in the eye to swell, leading to the characteristic pink or red appearance .

Recognizing the Symptoms

Your child with pink eye may show several signs, often beginning in one eye and potentially spreading to the other. Common symptoms include:

Redness in the white of the eye or inner eyelid

Itchiness or a gritty feeling, as if something is in the eye

Swollen eyelids

Watery eyes with tearing

Discharge – clear and watery, or thick and green/yellow, which can cause the eyelids to stick together, especially after sleep

The type of discharge can help identify the cause. A watery discharge often points to a viral infection, while a thick, purulent (pus-like) discharge is more common with bacterial conjunctivitis . Allergic conjunctivitis typically causes watery discharge, itchiness, and is often accompanied by a runny nose and sneezing .

Causes and Spread

Pink eye in children can be caused by viruses, bacteria, or allergies . Viral and bacterial conjunctivitis are highly contagious and can spread easily among children, especially in daycare and school settings .

The infection often spreads when a child touches discharge from an infected eye and then touches another person or a surface . It can also be transmitted by sharing contaminated items like towels, washcloths, pillowcases, or eye drops . Additionally, coughing and sneezing can spread the virus or bacteria through respiratory droplets .

Treatment and Home Care

Treatment for pink eye depends on the cause, and in most cases, it will clear up on its own . For mild symptoms, you can help your child feel more comfortable with simple home care:

Clean the eye: Gently wipe away any discharge with a clean, warm, damp washcloth. Use a fresh cloth for each eye and each cleaning to prevent spreading the infection .

Use cool compresses: A cool, damp washcloth placed over the eye can soothe irritation and reduce swelling .

Lubricating eye drops: Over-the-counter artificial tears can help relieve dryness and the gritty feeling .

For bacterial conjunctivitis, your child's doctor may prescribe antibiotic eye drops or ointment . Make sure to wash your hands thoroughly before and after applying the drops . Viral conjunctivitis, like a cold, cannot be cured with antibiotics and must run its course, usually within one to two weeks . Allergic conjunctivitis requires avoiding the allergy trigger; allergy medication or eye drops may be recommended .

When to Call the Doctor

While pink eye often resolves on its own, it is important to seek medical advice if your child experiences:

Eye pain or sensitivity to light

Changes in vision, such as blurred vision

Symptoms that do not improve after a few days, especially if they worsen

Swelling or redness that becomes more severe

A fever or other signs of a serious infection

Intense redness in one or both eyes

Symptoms lasting more than two weeks

For infants under 4 weeks old, conjunctivitis is a medical emergency that requires an immediate medical evaluation .

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does pink eye last in children?

Viral pink eye usually clears up in 1 to 2 weeks, while bacterial pink eye lasts up to 10 days but often improves within 2days of starting antibiotic drops .

When should I call the doctor for pink eye?

Call your doctor if your child has eye pain, vision changes, severe redness, a fever, or if symptoms do not improve after a few days. For a newborn, contact a doctor immediately for any sign of pink eye .

What home remedies can help pink eye?

The best home remedies are to clean the eye with a warm, damp washcloth and use a cool compress to soothe irritation. Do not use red-reducing drops, and never use unproven remedies like breastmilk .

Is pink eye very contagious in children?

Yes, viral and bacterial pink eye are highly contagious and spread easily through direct contact with eye discharge or contaminated objects. Good hygiene, like frequent handwashing, is key to preventing its spread .

Should I keep my child home from school with pink eye?

Children with bacterial pink eye can return 24 hours after starting antibiotics, when symptoms improve. For viral pink eye, it is safe to return when tearing and discharge are greatly improved. Always check with your child care or school for their policy .
