Treating Fever in an 18-Month-Old by Weight

When your 18-month-old has a fever, giving the right dose of medicine is essential for safety. The key rule is always dose by your child's weight in kilograms (kg), not by their age. Using weight ensures the dose is both safe and effective for their specific size .

This guide provides a quick reference for the two most common fever medicines. Always confirm the correct dose and concentration with your child's pediatrician before giving any medication. Acetaminophen (Tylenol) Dosage

Acetaminophen is the preferred first-line medicine for a feverish child who is uncomfortable .

Standard Dose: 10 to 15 mg per kilogram of body weight .

How Often: Every 4 to 6 hours as needed .

Maximum: Do not exceed 5 doses in 24 hours .

Ibuprofen is an effective alternative, but it has an important age restriction.

Age Restriction: Not recommended for children under 6 months of age .

Standard Dose: 10 mg per kilogram of body weight . A lower dose of 5 mg/kg is sometimes used for fevers under 102.5°F .

How Often: Every 6 to 8 hours as needed .

Maximum: Do not exceed 3 doses in 24 hours .

Use the Right Tool: Always use the dosing syringe or cup that comes with the medicine, not a kitchen spoon .

No Aspirin: Never give aspirin to children under 16 with a fever .

Focus on Comfort: Only give medicine if your child is uncomfortable, not just because the temperature is high. A sleeping child usually does not need to be woken for medicine .

Avoid Physical Cooling: Do not use cold baths, ice, or fanning to lower a fever, as these methods can cause discomfort and are not recommended .

Avoid Alternating: The routine alternation of acetaminophen and ibuprofen is not generally recommended, as it increases the risk of dosing errors. Use one medicine consistently .
