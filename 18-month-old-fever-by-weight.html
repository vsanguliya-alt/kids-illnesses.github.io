Fever Medication for an 18-Month-Old: A Weight-Based Guide

When your 18-month-old has a fever, giving the right dose of medicine is essential for safety. The key rule is always dose by your child's weight in kilograms, not by their age. Using weight ensures the dose is both safe and effective for their specific size.

This guide provides a quick reference for paracetamol and ibuprofen, the two most common fever medicines. Always confirm the correct dose and concentration with your child's pediatrician before giving any medication.

Paracetamol (Calpol, Tylenol) Dosage

Paracetamol is the preferred first-line medicine for a feverish child who is uncomfortable. It is generally safe for children over 3 months old.

Standard Dose: 10 to 15 mg per kilogram of body weight per dose .

How Often: Every 4 to 6 hours as needed.

Maximum: Do not exceed 4 doses in 24 hours.

Ibuprofen (Nurofen, Motrin) Dosage

Ibuprofen is an effective alternative, but it has an important age restriction. Do not use ibuprofen in children under 6 months of age without a doctor's guidance.

Standard Dose: 10 mg per kilogram of body weight per dose.

How Often: Every 6 to 8 hours as needed.

Maximum: Do not exceed 3 doses in 24 hours.

Important Safety Rules

Use the Right Tool: Always use the dosing syringe or cup that comes with the medicine, never a kitchen spoon, to avoid an overdose.

No Aspirin: Never give aspirin to children under 16 with a fever, as it is linked to Reye's syndrome, a rare but serious condition.

Focus on Comfort: Only give medicine if your child is uncomfortable, not just because the temperature is high. A sleeping child usually does not need to be woken for medicine.

Avoid Alternating: The routine alternation of paracetamol and ibuprofen is not generally recommended, as it increases the risk of dosing errors. Use one medicine consistently unless advised otherwise by a doctor.
