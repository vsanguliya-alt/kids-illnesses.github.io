Preschooler Fever: Emergency Signs You Must Not Ignore

Most fevers in children are harmless and caused by viral infections. However, certain symptoms accompanying a fever indicate a medical emergency.

Seek Emergency Care Immediately (Call 112/999)

Take your child to the nearest Emergency Department immediately if they have a fever and any of the following signs:

Breathing difficulty: Breathing very fast, working hard to breathe (ribs pulling in), or noisy grunting . Also, blue lips, tongue, or nails .

Altered mental state: Difficult to wake, very sleepy, confused, or does not respond normally .

Stiff neck: Inability to move the chin to the chest .

Non-blanching rash: A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass . This can be a sign of meningitis.

Seizure (fit): A febrile seizure, especially if lasting more than 5 minutes .

Severe dehydration: Signs include no wet nappy for 8hours, sunken eyes, and very dry lips .

Extreme irritability: Continuous crying or a weak, high-pitched cry that cannot be settled .

Severe pain: Complaint of severe headache, abdominal pain, or pain in a limb/joint .

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider Urgently

You should seek medical advice if your child has a fever and:

Fever persists: Lasting more than 5 days or fever over 39°C (102.2°F) in a child 3months old .

Poor feeding: Taking less than half their normal amount of fluids .

Is generally unwell: Looking pale, ashen, or mottled, or you feel your child looks very sick .

Trust your instincts. If you are worried that your child may be seriously ill, seek medical attention immediately, even if they do not have a high temperature .
