Vomiting in a Baby: A Home Care Guide

Vomiting in a baby can be distressing, but in many cases, it can be safely managed at home. Common health problems in babies include colds, coughs, fevers, and vomiting (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems). The primary goal is to support your baby&#x27;s recovery and prevent dehydration. This guide provides clear, step-by-step home care advice and when to seek medical help.

Understanding Vomiting in Babies

Vomiting is often a symptom of a viral infection or stomach bug. While it can be messy and upsetting, the body is usually working to expel a virus or irritant. The most common risk associated with vomiting in young babies is dehydration, which can happen quickly because they have small fluid reserves.

Step-by-Step Home Care

1. Stop Food for a Short While

Give your baby&#x27;s stomach a rest by stopping all solid foods (if they have started them) for a few hours. Do not stop fluids.

2. Start with Small Amounts of Fluid

This is the most crucial step. Start offering small, frequent sips of fluid every 10–15 minutes. Give only 1 to 2 teaspoons (5–10 mL) at a time to avoid overwhelming their stomach. If they keep that down, you can slowly increase the amount.

3. Choose the Right Fluids

Offer oral rehydration solution (ORS) like Pedialyte, which is formulated to replace lost electrolytes and fluids. You can also give breastmilk or formula. Avoid fruit juice, sugary drinks, or fizzy drinks, as they can worsen dehydration.

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your pediatrician immediately if your baby shows any of these signs, which could indicate dehydration or a more serious problem:

No wet diaper for 6 to 8 hours.

Sunken eyes.

Dry mouth or lips, or crying without tears.

Vomiting that lasts more than 24 hours.

High fever (over 102°F / 38.9°C).

Bloody vomit or green-colored (bilious) vomit.

Severe or worsening tummy pain.

If your baby is lethargic or unusually sleepy.

Trust your intuition - if you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems).

🚨 Seek Immediate Emergency Care If Your Baby Has:

A stiff neck, confusion, or is difficult to wake.

Breathing very fast or struggling to breathe.

A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass (the &quot;glass test&quot;).

Signs of severe dehydration (very sunken eyes, no urination, skin that doesn&#x27;t flatten when gently pinched).
