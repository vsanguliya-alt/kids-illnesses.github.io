Diarrhea in Children: A Houston Parent's Guide

Diarrhea is a common illness in children. It is defined as having three or more loose, watery stools in a single day (Source: NIH). While most cases are mild and short-lived, diarrhea can lead to serious dehydration if not managed properly. Rotavirus is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children, and the infection often causes vomiting as well (Source: NIH).

Common Causes

The most frequent causes of diarrhea in children include viruses, bacteria, and food allergies.

Viral Infections: Rotavirus and norovirus are common culprits. These viruses are highly contagious and spread easily in daycare settings (Source: NIH).

Bacterial Infections: Harmful bacteria like E. coli can cause severe diarrhea. Some types produce a toxin leading to bloody diarrhea, which can sometimes cause kidney failure (Source: NIH). This is often linked to contaminated food or water.

Food Allergies: Diarrhea can be a symptom of a food allergy or intolerance (Source: NIH). Common triggers include cow's milk, soy, and eggs.

When to Call Your Pediatrician

It is critical to recognize the warning signs of dehydration and more serious illness. You should contact your child's health care provider right away if they have:

Diarrhea lasting more than 24 hours (Source: NIH).

Signs of dehydration, such as decreased urination, dry mouth, or no tears when crying.

Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus (Source: NIH).

A high fever.

Severe abdominal pain.

Vomiting that prevents them from keeping fluids down.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for diarrhea?

Call your doctor if your child has diarrhea lasting more than 24 hours, has signs of dehydration, has bloody stools, a high fever, or severe abdominal pain.

How long does diarrhea last?

Acute diarrhea from a viral infection typically lasts a few days, but can sometimes last up to a week (Source: NIH).

What home remedies can help with diarrhea?

The most important thing is to keep your child hydrated. Offer small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution (Pedialyte) or, for older children, diluted clear liquids like water or broth. Avoid sugary drinks like juice or soda, which can worsen diarrhea.

What should I feed my child with diarrhea?

Continue to offer your child their usual diet as they are able to tolerate it. For babies, continue breastfeeding or formula feeding. For older children, bland foods like crackers, toast, and bananas are good options. Avoid fatty, spicy, or sugary foods.

How can I prevent diarrhea?

Good handwashing is key to preventing the spread of viruses and bacteria. Also, practice safe food handling to avoid food poisoning. The rotavirus vaccine can prevent severe rotavirus diarrhea.
