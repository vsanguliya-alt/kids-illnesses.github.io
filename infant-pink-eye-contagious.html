Is Infant Pink Eye Contagious? A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Pink eye (conjunctivitis) is a common eye condition in young children. Whether it is contagious depends entirely on the cause. This guide explains the different types, how they spread, and when to seek medical help for your infant.

Types of Pink Eye and Contagiousness

Infectious conjunctivitis (viral or bacterial): This type is contagious and can spread to other people . It is the form parents need to be most cautious about.

Allergic or irritant conjunctivitis: This type is NOT contagious. It is caused by allergies or reactions to things like soap or chlorine .

How Infectious Pink Eye Spreads

Viral and bacterial conjunctivitis spread very easily, especially among young children in group settings . The infection passes through:

Direct contact with discharge from an infected eye .

Contact with contaminated items, such as towels, pillows, toys, or face cloths .

Touching the eyes after contact with respiratory droplets from a cough or sneeze .

Contagious Period and Prevention

A person is contagious while they have discharge from their eye .

To reduce the spread of infection to others:

Wash hands regularly with warm soapy water, especially after touching the eyes .

Avoid sharing towels, pillows, and face cloths . Wash these items in hot water.

Keep your child home from daycare or school while the eye is weeping or until they have been on antibiotic treatment for 24 hours . Some UK guidance suggests exclusion is not always required, but it is prudent to follow your childcare setting&#x27;s policy .

When to See a Doctor Immediately

Pink eye in a newborn (under 30 days old) is a medical emergency that requires urgent attention . Call your doctor or get an urgent appointment immediately if your newborn has red, sticky eyes . Infections at this age can be serious and require prompt treatment .

For older infants, seek medical care if you notice:

Pain in the eyes .

Sensitivity to light .

Changes in vision, such as blurred vision .

Intense eye redness .

Symptoms that get worse or do not improve after a few days .

Yellow or green discharge, swelling, or if your child has a fever .
