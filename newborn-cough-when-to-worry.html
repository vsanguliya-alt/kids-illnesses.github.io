Newborn Cough: When to Worry

It can be scary when your baby is sick (Source: NIH). A cough in a newborn is always a reason to pay close attention because their immune systems are immature and even a common cold can become serious. While many coughs are harmless, certain signs indicate a medical emergency.

When to Seek Emergency Care (Call 112/999)

Take your newborn to the Emergency Department immediately if they have a cough and any of the following signs:

Difficulty Breathing: Fast breathing (more than 60 breaths per minute), flaring nostrils, grunting sounds with each breath, or the skin sucking in around the ribs (retractions).

Blue or Pale Skin: Their skin, lips, or tongue turn blue, pale, or grey. On darker skin, check the palms of the hands and soles of the feet.

Cyanosis: Any blue or dusky appearance around the mouth or face.

Limp or Floppy: Your baby is not responding like they normally do, is difficult to wake up, or is extremely drowsy.

Choking or Gagging: Your baby is having trouble swallowing, choking, or gagging.

Fever in a Newborn: Any fever (temperature of 38°C / 100.4°F or higher) in a baby under 3 months old requires immediate medical evaluation.

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider Urgently

You should call your pediatrician or a health helpline if your newborn has a cough and:

Is under 3 months old with any cough, even without a fever.

Has a temperature of 38°C or above (if over 3 months).

Is not feeding well – taking less than half of their normal amount of milk.

Is irritable or lethargic – fussy, crying more than usual, or appearing tired and limp.

Has vomiting or diarrhea – especially if unable to keep fluids down.

Has signs of dehydration – fewer wet diapers (less than 6 in 24 hours), dry mouth, no tears when crying, or sunken eyes.

Has a cough lasting more than 2weeks – a prolonged cough could indicate a more serious condition.

General Advice

Trust Your Instincts: You know your baby best. If you are worried, call your healthcare provider right away (Source: NIH).

Do Not Give Cold Medicines: Use caution when giving cough and cold products to kids; they are not safe for newborns (Source: NIH).

Do Not Give Aspirin: Never give aspirin to children (Source: NIH).
