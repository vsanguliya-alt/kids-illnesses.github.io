Flu Symptoms in Newborns: What Parents Need to Know

The flu (influenza) can be serious or even deadly, especially for newborn babies (Source: NIH). This is because newborns have immature immune systems and are more vulnerable to severe infections and complications from the flu (Source: NIH).

Common Flu Symptoms in Newborns

Symptoms of the flu come on suddenly and may include (Source: NIH):

Fever or feeling feverish/chills – may be high; often comes on suddenly

Cough – dry or persistent

Sore throat – may cause feeding difficulties

Runny or stuffy nose – nasal congestion

Body aches – may cause fussiness or irritability

Fatigue – unusual tiredness or lethargy

Vomiting and diarrhea – more common in newborns than in older children (Source: NIH)

Why Newborns Are at Higher Risk

Newborns are more likely to have complications from the flu, especially if they have certain chronic health conditions (Source: NIH). These complications can include pneumonia, dehydration, and worsening of underlying conditions. This is why it is critical to take any flu symptoms in a newborn seriously.

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider

If your newborn has symptoms of flu and is very sick or you are worried about your illness, contact your health care provider (Source: NIH). You should call your healthcare provider immediately if your newborn has flu symptoms and:

Is under 3 months old with any fever

Has difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Has blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Is limp, floppy, or difficult to wake

Is not feeding well or refuses to feed

Shows signs of dehydration (fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, no tears)

Has a seizure

Has symptoms that improve but then return or worsen

Diagnosis

To diagnose the flu, health care providers will first do a medical history and ask about your symptoms (Source: NIH). They may also perform a rapid flu test using a nasal swab.

Prevention

The flu vaccine is the best way to prevent flu in newborns. For infants under 6 months, the best protection is vaccination of all household members and caregivers. Additionally, avoiding contact with sick individuals and practicing good hand hygiene can help reduce the risk of infection.
