Ear Infection Symptoms in Children: A Parent's Guide

Ear infections are the most common reason parents bring their child to a doctor. Three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday. The infection usually affects the middle ear and is called otitis media (Source: NIH).

Common Symptoms

The symptoms of an ear infection usually start quickly and include (Source: NHS):

Pain inside the ear (earache) – this is the most common symptom

Discharge running out of the ear

A feeling of pressure or fullness inside the ear

Itching and irritation in and around the ear

Scaly skin in and around the ear

Symptoms in Young Children and Babies

Young children and babies with an ear infection may not be able to tell you their ear hurts. Look for these additional signs (Source: NHS):

Being difficult to understand when speaking

Asking people to repeat what they say

Asking for the TV or music to be turned up loud

Struggling to hear people far away

Becoming easily distracted when people are talking

Finding it hard to concentrate or seeming tired and irritable because it's harder for them to listen

Ear Pain: Not Always an Infection

Ear pain does not always mean an ear infection. Other causes of ear pain include (Source: NHS):

Cause Key Feature

Earwax build-up May cause hearing loss or fullness

Object stuck in the ear Do not try to remove it yourself – see a GP

Perforated eardrum Particularly after a loud noise or accident

Baby teething Pain in gums, not inside ear

Dental abscess Pain with swallowing

Glue ear Fluid in middle ear without infection

Glue ear is much more common in children. If glue ear lasts a long time, it can affect a child's speech development and progress at school (Source: NHS). A GP should be able to tell if it's glue ear by looking for fluid inside the ear.

When to Be Concerned

See your healthcare provider if your child has (Source: NHS):

Ear pain with a change in hearing

Ear pain with pain when swallowing

Ear pain with a high temperature

Symptoms that last more than 3 days (most ear infections clear up within 3 days, although sometimes symptoms can last up to a week)

Distinguishing Ear Infection from Other Conditions

Ear infections are caused by viral or bacterial infections. However, ear problems can also be caused by:

Viruses like colds and flu

Something irritating the ear canal, such as eczema, water, or wearing earplugs

Fungal or bacterial infections of the ear canal

Inner ear problems like labyrinthitis (Source: NHS)
