What the Sources Tell Us

The following information is available from the provided excerpts:

Scarlet fever is a contagious infection that mostly affects young children. The first signs include a high temperature (fever), a sore throat, and swollen neck glands. A rash appears 12 to 48 hours later. The rash makes your skin feel rough, like sandpaper. On white skin the rash looks pink or red. On brown and black skin it might be harder to see a change in colour, but you can still feel the rash and see the raised bumps. A white coating also appears on the tongue (Source: NHS).

Important: Scarlet fever includes a fever as a key symptom. Therefore, a rash without fever in a toddler is unlikely to be scarlet fever.

Common causes of rash without fever in toddlers (not from sources) may include:

Contact dermatitis – irritation from soaps, fabrics, or other substances

Eczema – dry, itchy, red patches of skin

Heat rash – small pink or red spots from overheating

Hives – raised, itchy welts from allergic reactions

Viral rashes – some viruses cause rashes without significant fever

When to call the doctor:

The rash spreads quickly

The rash is painful or itchy

The rash has blisters or oozing

Your child has other symptoms (fussiness, poor eating, lethargy)

The rash does not improve within a few days

You are worried about your child
