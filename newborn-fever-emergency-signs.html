Newborn Fever: Emergency Signs You Must Not Ignore

It is important to know how to help your sick baby, and to know the warning signs for more serious problems (Source: NIH). A fever in a newborn is not just a symptom—it is a red flag that requires immediate attention. It can be scary when your baby is sick, especially when it is not an everyday problem like a cold or a fever (Source: NIH).

Seek Emergency Care Immediately (Call 112/999)

Take your newborn to the nearest Emergency Department immediately if they have a fever and any of the following signs:

Age under 3 months: Any fever (temperature of 38°C / 100.4°F or higher) in a newborn under 3 months old requires immediate medical evaluation. Do not wait.

Difficulty breathing: Breathing that is fast, labored, or accompanied by grunting or wheezing.

Blue or pale skin: Their skin, lips, or tongue turn blue, pale, or grey. On darker skin, check the palms of the hands and soles of the feet.

Limp or floppy: Your baby is not responding like they normally do, is difficult to wake up, or is extremely drowsy.

Stiff neck: Your baby has a stiff neck or seems sensitive to light.

Seizure (fit): Having a seizure (febrile convulsion). A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes is a medical emergency.

Non-blanching rash: A rash that does not fade when you press a glass against it – this can be a sign of meningitis.

Signs of severe dehydration: No wet diapers for 6 hours, sunken eyes, or a sunken soft spot on the head.

Contact Your Healthcare Provider Urgently

You should seek medical advice from your healthcare provider if your newborn has a fever and:

Is between 3 and 6 months old with a temperature of 38.9°C (102°F) or higher.

Has a fever lasting more than 3 days.

Is not feeding well or refusing to eat.

Is unusually fussy or irritable.

Has vomiting or diarrhea with signs of dehydration.

Has a fever after being in a very hot place (possible heatstroke).

Treatment for Fever

Treatment depends on the cause of your fever. If the fever is very high, your health care provider may recommend taking an over-the-counter medicine such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen. However, never give aspirin to a child with a fever. For newborns, do not give any medication without first consulting a healthcare provider.
