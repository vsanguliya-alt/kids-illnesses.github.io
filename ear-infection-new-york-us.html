Ear Infection in Children: A New York Parent's Guide

Ear infections are incredibly common in young children. In fact, three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday (Source: NIH). Understanding the symptoms, knowing how to manage them at home, and recognizing when to seek medical care is essential.

Understanding Ear Infections

Most ear infections affect the middle ear and are called otitis media (Source: NIH). They are often caused by viral or bacterial infections, frequently following a cold or flu (Source: NHS). When the Eustachian tube—which connects the middle ear to the back of the nose—becomes swollen or blocked, fluid can build up, leading to infection (Source: NHS).

Recognizing the Symptoms

The symptoms of an ear infection usually start quickly and include:

Earache: Pain inside the ear, which can be severe (Source: NHS).

Discharge: Fluid or pus running out of the ear (Source: NHS).

Pressure or Fullness: A feeling of pressure inside the ear (Source: NHS).

Itching and Irritation: In and around the ear (Source: NHS).

Scaly Skin: Around the ear (Source: NHS).

In young children and babies, symptoms may also include irritability, pulling at the ear, difficulty sleeping, fever, and hearing problems.

When to Call Your Pediatrician or Seek Care

You should contact your pediatrician if your child has symptoms of an ear infection and:

Is under 6 months old.

Has a fever of 102°F (38.9°C) or higher.

Has discharge from the ear.

Has symptoms that do not improve after 2days.

Is in severe pain.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for an ear infection?

Call your doctor if your child is under 6 months old, has a high fever, has discharge from the ear, or if symptoms last more than 2days.

How long does an ear infection last?

Most ear infections clear up within 3 days, although sometimes symptoms can last up to a week (Source: NHS).

What home remedies can help with ear pain?

Use a warm (not hot) compress on the ear, keep your child elevated, and provide pain relief such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen, following the correct dose for their age and weight.

Does my child need antibiotics for an ear infection?

Not always. Many ear infections are viral and clear up on their own. Antibiotics are only prescribed for bacterial infections or if symptoms are severe and not improving (Source: NHS).

How can I prevent ear infections?

Preventive measures include keeping your child's vaccinations up to date, avoiding secondhand smoke, and practicing good handwashing to reduce colds and flu.

Local Guidance for New York, US

United States (AAP guidance): Fever is a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher. For infants under 3 months with any fever, seek care immediately. Acetaminophen (Tylenol) and ibuprofen (Motrin/Advil) are standard antipyretics — ibuprofen only for babies over 6 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call a doctor about infection?

If your child has difficulty breathing, persistent high fever, lethargy, or symptoms lasting beyond expected, contact a healthcare provider.

Is infection normal at this age?

Many variations are normal. Red-flag signs (listed above) mean you should seek care.

What helps at home?

Hydration, rest, and age-appropriate comfort measures. Always follow NHS/AAP guidance for medications.

How long does it last?

Most childhood episodes resolve within a few days. Persistent or worsening symptoms need evaluation.
