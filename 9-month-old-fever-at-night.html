Fever in a 9-Month-Old at Night: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Fevers in young babies are a common concern, especially when they spike at night. While it can be alarming to wake up and find your baby burning up, it&#x27;s important to remember that a fever is a typical sign of the body fighting off an infection. Nighttime fevers often appear higher because the body&#x27;s temperature naturally rises in the evening.

Comforting Your Baby at Night

When your 9-month-old has a fever at night, the primary focus should be on their comfort rather than just the number on the thermometer. If your baby is sleeping soundly, they generally do not need to be woken up for medicine.

Focus on comfort and hydration:

Keep them comfortable: Dress your baby in light, breathable layers. Avoid over-bundling, which can cause overheating.

Encourage fluids: Offer small, frequent sips of breastmilk, formula, or water during the night to prevent dehydration.

Medication: If the fever is making your baby miserable or causing pain, you can use age-appropriate doses of paracetamol or ibuprofen. Never give aspirin to children under 16.

When to Call the Doctor or Seek Emergency Care

It&#x27;s wise to have a low threshold for seeking medical advice for a fever in a young baby.

Call NHS 111 for urgent advice if your baby:

Cannot keep fluids down or has fewer wet nappies than usual.

Has a fever that lasts more than a few days or is accompanied by other symptoms like a rash, difficulty swallowing, or severe tiredness.

🚨 Call 999 or go to A&amp;E immediately if your baby has a fever and any of these &quot;red flag&quot; symptoms:

Green or yellow-green vomit.

A stiff neck and pain when looking at bright lights.

A sudden, severe headache or tummy ache.

Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or not responding as usual.

Severe difficulty breathing (gasping, grunting, or their stomach sucking under their ribcage).

Skin, lips, or tongue that look blue, grey, pale, or blotchy (check palms and soles on brown or black skin).

Trust your instincts. If your child looks or acts very ill, seek medical attention immediately.
