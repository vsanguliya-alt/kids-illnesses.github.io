Toddler Diarrhea: How Long Does It Last?

Diarrhea—loose, watery stools occurring three or more times in a day—is a common issue in toddlers, most often caused by a viral infection. In most cases, the illness resolves on its own within a specific timeframe.

Typical Duration

For a standard viral stomach bug (gastroenteritis), diarrhea typically lasts between 5 to 7 days and should stop within 2 weeks . This is the most common scenario for toddlers in daycare or nursery settings.

Other common infections have slightly different timelines:

Rotavirus: A viral infection often causing severe watery diarrhea usually resolves within 3 to 7 days .

Shigella infection: A bacterial gut infection typically clears up on its own within a week .

Acute vs. Persistent Diarrhea

Healthcare providers classify diarrhea based on how long it lasts :

Acute diarrhea: Lasts less than 14 days and is almost always caused by an infection.

Persistent diarrhea: Lasts between 2 and 4 weeks. This may require further investigation.

Chronic diarrhea: Lasts longer than 4 weeks and is often linked to underlying conditions like food allergies or inflammatory bowel disease.

When to Contact a Doctor

Seek medical advice if your toddler&#x27;s diarrhea:

Lasts longer than 24 to 48 hours, especially with a fever .

Is accompanied by a fever of 102°F (38.9°C) or higher .

Contains blood or mucus .

Is accompanied by vomiting that lasts more than 12–24 hours .

Watch closely for signs of dehydration, including :

No wet diaper for 6 hours or no urine for 8 hours.

Dry mouth and lips.

No tears when crying.

Sunken eyes.

Unusual sleepiness, lethargy, or irritability.

Seek emergency care immediately if your toddler :

Seems dehydrated, has sunken eyes, or has passed no urine for 12 hours.

Has blood in their stool.

Is unable to keep down any fluids.

Becomes extremely pale, lethargic, or difficult to wake.
