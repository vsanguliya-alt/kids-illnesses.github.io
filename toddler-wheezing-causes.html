Common Causes of Wheezing in Toddlers (Not from Sources)

Wheezing can be caused by several conditions, including:

Asthma – a chronic condition causing airway inflammation and narrowing

Bronchiolitis – a viral infection of the small airways, most commonly caused by RSV

Viral infections – such as the common cold or flu

Allergic reactions – to food or environmental triggers

Croup – a viral infection causing swelling in the upper airway

Pneumonia – an infection of the lungs

Inhaled object – a small object stuck in the airway

When to Seek Medical Help

Seek emergency care immediately if your toddler has wheezing and:

Difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Flaring nostrils or using chest muscles to breathe (retractions)

Difficulty feeding or poor feeding

Lethargy or difficulty waking

You are worried about your child

Contact your healthcare provider if your toddler has wheezing and:

Has a fever

Has a cough that lasts more than 3 weeks

Is not eating or drinking well

You are concerned

If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH). Well-child visits are important to your toddler's health (Source: NIH). It is always better to be safe and seek medical advice when in doubt.
