Newborn Rash with Fever: When to Worry

A rash in a newborn can be alarming, especially when accompanied by a fever. While many rashes are harmless, the combination of fever and rash can sometimes indicate a serious infection that requires urgent medical attention. Babies commonly have skin problems, like diaper rash or cradle cap (Source: NIH), but these typically do not cause fever.

Scarlet Fever: A Common Cause

Scarlet fever is a contagious infection that mostly affects young children. The first signs of scarlet fever can be flu-like symptoms, including a high temperature, a sore throat and swollen neck glands (a large lump on the side of your neck). A rash appears 12 to 48 hours later (Source: NHS).

Key features of the scarlet fever rash:

The rash makes your skin feel rough, like sandpaper (Source: NHS)

On white skin, the rash looks pink or red

On brown and black skin, it might be harder to see a change in colour, but you can still feel the rash and see the raised bumps (Source: NHS)

A white coating also appears on the tongue (Source: NHS)

When to Seek Emergency Care (Call 112/999)

A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass is a medical emergency. This could be a sign of meningitis or a severe infection called sepsis. If the rash is this type, or if your baby has other concerning symptoms like a stiff neck, difficulty breathing, or is very drowsy, call emergency services immediately.

Seek emergency care immediately if your newborn has a rash with fever and:

Is under 3 months old with any fever

Has difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Has blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Is limp, floppy, or difficult to wake

Has a stiff neck or seems sensitive to light

Has a seizure

Is not feeding well or refuses to feed

Shows signs of dehydration

Contact Your Healthcare Provider Urgently

You should contact your healthcare provider if your newborn has a rash with fever and:

The rash does not get better within a few days or worsens

The rash is accompanied by fluid-filled blisters or oozing

Your baby is unusually fussy or irritable

Your baby has vomiting or diarrhea

Common Rashes That Can Occur with Fever

Most rashes with fever in newborns are caused by viral infections. These rashes are often pink to red, and the child may have other symptoms like a cough or runny nose. These rashes are usually harmless and resolve as the illness clears.

Roseola (Sixth Disease): Caused by a virus, this typically starts with a high fever for 3days. As the fever suddenly breaks, a pink, spotty rash appears on the trunk and spreads to the limbs. It is common in children under 2 years old.

Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease: This causes a fever, sore throat, and a rash of small blisters on the hands, feet, and mouth.
