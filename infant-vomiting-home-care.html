Infant Vomiting: Home Care and When to Seek Help

Vomiting is common in babies and is often caused by a viral stomach bug, such as rotavirus, which can cause vomiting and watery diarrhea lasting 3 to 8 days . The main risk of vomiting is dehydration, which means the body has lost too much fluid . Infants are especially prone to dehydration because their bodies are less efficient at conserving water . Here is how to care for your baby at home and when to call the doctor.

How to Care for Your Baby at Home

The focus of home care is preventing dehydration.

Continue Feeding

Breastfed babies: Continue breastfeeding. Offer milk more often and in smaller amounts if needed .

Bottle-fed babies: Continue offering formula. If not tolerated, an oral rehydration solution (ORS) can be used for the first 12 hours, then return to formula in small, frequent amounts .

Do not give plain water to infants under 1 year as the primary fluid replacement .

Offer Small Amounts of Fluid Frequently

For babies under 1 year: Offer 1 teaspoon (5 ml) every 5minutes . Use a syringe or medicine cup for precise amounts.

After 4 hours without vomiting: Gradually increase the amount.

Avoid sugary drinks like juice or soda, as they can worsen diarrhea .

When to Resume Solid Foods

For the first 24 hours, it is best to avoid solid foods, as liquids are less likely to trigger further vomiting . Once your baby can keep fluids down for 6hours, you can slowly introduce bland foods like crackers, rice, or soup for older infants.

When to Call the Doctor Immediately

Contact your pediatrician or seek urgent care if your baby:

Has signs of dehydration: This is the most important reason to seek care . Signs include:

Fewer wet diapers than usual (less than 6 in 24 hours) Dry mouth and tongue, and no tears when crying Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot on the head Unusual drowsiness, fussiness, or lethargy Is under 6 months old and is vomiting Has been vomiting for more than 24 hours (for infants) Is unable to keep any fluids down Has a fever Has blood or green (bilious) fluid in the vomit – this requires emergency evaluation Emergency Warning Signs (Call 911/112)

Seek emergency care immediately if your baby:

Is difficult to wake up, limp, or unresponsive Has a stiff neck Has a rash that does not fade when pressed Has projectile (forceful) vomiting that shoots across the room – this can be a sign of pyloric stenosis, a serious condition requiring surgery Trust your instincts — if you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away.
