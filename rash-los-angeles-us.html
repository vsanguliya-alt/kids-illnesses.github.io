Rash in Children: A Los Angeles Parent's Guide

A rash can be a worrying sight for any parent. In Los Angeles, a rash can be caused by many things—from common skin irritations and allergies to specific infections that are more prevalent in the region, like Valley fever . Understanding the possible causes, knowing how to treat a rash at home, and recognizing when to seek medical care is key.

Common Causes of Rashes

Rashes are very common in children and can have a wide range of causes. These include:

Allergic Reactions: From foods, medications, or contact with allergens like plants or certain materials.

Infections: Viral infections like measles or chickenpox, bacterial infections like impetigo, or fungal infections like ringworm.

Skin Irritants: Exposure to harsh soaps, detergents, or chemicals.

Eczema: A common chronic condition linked to allergies and asthma.

Heat and Friction: Excessive sweating or tight clothing can cause heat rash.

Local Concerns: Valley Fever and Measles

In Los Angeles, it's important to be aware of local health concerns. Valley fever, transmitted by fungal spores, can cause a distinctive rash, and measles is another illness that presents with fever and rash .

Valley Fever (Coccidioidomycosis): This fungal infection is more common in the Southwestern U.S., including the Los Angeles area. A rash, often on the lower legs, can be a symptom of acute Valley Fever. If you suspect Valley Fever, contact your doctor, especially if symptoms persist or worsen .

Measles: Measles is a highly contagious viral infection that can cause a high fever followed by a generalized rash. If you suspect measles, it is a serious public health concern and requires immediate medical attention .

When to Call a Doctor

You should contact your pediatrician if your child's rash is accompanied by any of the following:

A fever

Difficulty breathing

Swelling of the face or mouth

The rash is painful, blistered, or oozing

Your child is very irritable or lethargic

If the rash does not fade when you press a glass against it

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for a rash?

Call your doctor immediately if the rash is accompanied by a fever, difficulty breathing, or if it appears suddenly and spreads quickly. Also, call if your child seems very unwell .

How long does a viral rash last?

Most viral rashes last anywhere from a few days to a week and often clear up on their own once the underlying infection has run its course.

What home remedies can help a child's rash?

For mild rashes, keep the skin cool and dry. You can also use a cool compress and an over-the-counter 1% hydrocortisone cream to soothe itching, but always consult your doctor before using any new treatment on a child.

How do I know if a rash is serious?

A rash is serious if it does not fade with pressure, is accompanied by a high fever, or makes your child difficult to wake or very unwell.

What does Valley fever rash look like?

The rash associated with Valley fever often appears as tender red bumps on the lower legs, but it can also be a generalized red rash.

Is it okay to give my child Benadryl for a rash?

Benadryl (diphenhydramine) can be given to children over 1 year of age for allergic rashes, but you should always check with your pediatrician for the correct dose based on your child's weight.
