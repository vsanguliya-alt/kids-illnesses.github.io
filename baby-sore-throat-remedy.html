Baby Sore Throat: Safe Home Remedies

A sore throat in a baby can be a concerning symptom, often part of a viral cold. Most throat problems are minor and go away on their own. However, treating a sore throat in a baby requires safe methods, as many medications are not recommended for young children.

What You Can Do at Home

Focus on safe, comforting remedies to help your baby feel better. Here are effective ways to relieve sore throat discomfort:

Keep Your Baby Hydrated

Offer your baby plenty of fluids. Breast milk or formula should be offered frequently. If your baby is over 6 months, you can also give small sips of water. Hydration is key to preventing dehydration and keeping the throat moist.

Provide Comfort with a Humidifier

Use a cool mist humidifier in your baby's room. The added moisture can help soothe the irritation in their throat and make it easier for them to breathe, especially if they also have a stuffy nose.

Keep Air Clean and Clear

Use saline nose drops or sprays and nasal suctioning with a bulb syringe to clear a stuffy nose. This can help if the sore throat is exacerbated by post-nasal drip.

When to Call the Doctor

Most sore throats get better on their own within a week. However, you should call your health care provider if your baby has a sore throat for more than two days or has trouble swallowing. Sore throat can be a symptom of tonsillitis, which may also include swollen glands in the neck .

Also, contact a doctor immediately if the sore throat is accompanied by:

A high fever.

Swollen glands in the neck .

Difficulty breathing or swallowing.

Signs of dehydration.

A sore throat can be caused by a bacterial throat infection, which in rare cases can lead to rheumatic fever if not treated early . If your baby has a sore throat that is not getting better, especially with other symptoms, seek medical advice.
