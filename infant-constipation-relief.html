Infant Constipation Relief: A Parent's Guide

Constipation in infants can be distressing for both baby and parents. It typically means your baby is having bowel movements less often than usual, or the stools are hard, dry, and difficult to pass . However, straining or grunting alone does not always indicate constipation—babies have weak stomach muscles, and straining is common if the stool passed is soft .

Here is an evidence-based guide to recognizing and relieving infant constipation.

Signs Your Baby May Be Constipated

Hard, dry, pellet-like stools that do not soak into the nappy Straining with crying or signs of pain during a bowel movement Fewer bowel movements than usual—fewer than 3 times in a week can be a potential indicator A firm or swollen abdomen, fussiness, or signs of cramping Blood on the surface of hard stool (this may indicate anal fissures from straining) What Is Normal?

Bowel patterns vary widely among infants and depend on feeding type:

Breastfed babies rarely get constipated. They may poo after every feed or go up to a week without a stool—as long as the stool is soft, this is normal .

Formula-fed babies tend to have bulkier, more frequent stools . If a formula-fed infant has not passed stool for 4 days and it is hard and dry, this is considered constipation .

Home Care for Constipation

For Babies Under 6 Months (Exclusively Milk-Fed)

Ensure correct formula preparation: Always follow the instructions on the tin. Adding extra water or swapping scoops between formulas can cause problems .

Offer extra breastfeeds: If you are breastfeeding, offer additional feeds to ensure your baby is getting enough fluid .

For babies over 1 month: If constipation persists, a small amount of apple or pear juice may help. The sugars in these juices draw fluid into the intestines and help loosen stool . A general guide is 1 ounce (about 30 mL) per month of life, up to 4 months . Do not give juice to babies under 1 month old .

Tummy massage and leg exercises: Gently massage your baby's tummy in a clockwise direction, or move their legs in a gentle bicycling motion to help stimulate the bowels .

For Babies Over 6 Months (Eating Solids)

Add fiber-rich foods: Offer pureed prunes, pears, peaches, peas, beans, and whole-grain cereals (e.g., barley or whole wheat) . Avoid rice cereal, which is low in fiber .

Offer water: If your baby is over 6 months, offer small amounts of water in a cup with meals .

Important Safety Precautions

Do not give laxatives, enemas, or stimulant suppositories to a baby unless specifically prescribed by a healthcare provider .

Do not add sugar, rusks, or solid foods to your baby's bottle .

Avoid mineral oil for infant constipation .

When to Contact a Healthcare Provider Immediately

Seek urgent medical advice if your baby is constipated and shows any of these signs :

A swollen or bloated tummy

Vomiting or fever

Blood in the diaper or stool (not just on the surface of a hard stool)

Refusing to eat or appearing unusually tired or lethargic

Not gaining weight or losing weight

Severe pain and distress when trying to pass stool

If simple dietary and comfort measures do not provide relief after a few days, contact your baby's healthcare provider for further guidance .
