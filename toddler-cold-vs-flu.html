Cold vs. Flu in Toddlers: What Parents Need to Know

The common cold is a mild infection of your upper respiratory tract (which includes your nose and throat). More than 200 different viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common type (Source: NIH). The flu (influenza) is a different illness that can be more severe, especially in young children.

Cold Symptoms

The symptoms of a common cold usually start a few days after you become infected with the virus (Source: NIH). Cold symptoms are generally mild and may include:

Runny or stuffy nose

Sneezing

Cough

Sore throat

Mild fever (in some cases)

Flu Symptoms

The flu comes on more suddenly and is often more severe. While the sources do not list specific flu symptoms for toddlers, flu symptoms may include high fever, severe cough, body aches, and extreme tiredness. Symptoms of the flu come on suddenly.

Telling the Difference

In toddlers, telling the difference between a cold and the flu can be especially difficult because children cannot always describe their symptoms well. The key difference is often the severity: the flu tends to come on suddenly and cause more severe symptoms, while a cold develops gradually and is milder.

Treatment

There is no cure for the common cold. But there are treatments that can make you feel better while you wait for the cold to go away on its own (Source: NIH):

Drinking plenty of fluids

Taking over-the-counter pain or cold and cough medicines

Important Safety Rules:

Some cold and cough medicines contain ingredients that are not recommended for children. Talk with your child&#x27;s health care provider before giving your child any cold and cough medicines (Source: NIH).

Some cold and cough medicines contain pain relievers. Some contain other active ingredients (Source: NIH).

Never give aspirin to a child with a cold or flu.

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider if your toddler has:

Fever that lasts more than 3 days

Difficulty breathing

Symptoms that improve but then return or worsen

Signs of dehydration

You are worried about your child

Prevention

The flu vaccine is the best way to prevent flu in toddlers. The vaccine is recommended for all children aged 6 months and older. To prevent the common cold, practice good hand hygiene and avoid close contact with sick individuals.
