Infant Diarrhea: How Long Does It Last?

Diarrhea in infants is defined as having more frequent, loose, or watery stools than normal. The duration depends on the underlying cause. While many cases are mild and self-limiting, it is important to know when to seek medical attention.

Typical Duration

For viral infections, such as rotavirus—a common cause of severe diarrhea in infants—vomiting and diarrhea may last from three to eight days (Source: NIH). The infection often begins with vomiting and fever, followed by diarrhea that can be watery and frequent.

Causes of Diarrhea in Infants

Diarrhea can be caused by several factors, including:

Viral infections – such as rotavirus, which causes severe diarrhea lasting 3–8 days (Source: NIH)

Bacterial infections – such as E. coli, a harmful bacteria that can cause severe diarrhea (Source: NIH)

Allergic reactions – to foods or formula, which can present with a rash, diarrhea, or vomiting (Source: NIH)

Juice consumption – because it contains a lot of sugar, babies should not drink juice before age 1 (Source: NIH)

Signs of Dehydration to Watch For

The most serious complication of diarrhea in infants is dehydration. Because babies are small, they can lose fluids quickly. Monitor your baby closely for:

Decreased urination (fewer wet diapers than usual)

Dry mouth or lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot on the head

Unusual drowsiness or irritability

Cool, pale skin

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider immediately if your infant has diarrhea and:

Is under 3 months old

Has signs of dehydration

Has diarrhea lasting more than 3 days

Has a fever (especially in infants under 3 months)

Has blood or mucus in the stool

Is not feeding well or refusing to eat

Is vomiting and unable to keep fluids down

Is unusually fussy or lethargic

What to Do at Home

Continue breastfeeding or formula feeding – breast milk or formula provides the best hydration and nutrition for infants

Offer small, frequent feedings if your baby can tolerate them

Do not give juice to infants under 1 year old – it contains too much sugar and can worsen diarrhea (Source: NIH)

Never give anti-diarrheal medications to infants without a doctor&#x27;s prescription

Watch closely for signs of dehydration
