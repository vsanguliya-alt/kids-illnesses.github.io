Fever in a 9-Month-Old: When to Call the Doctor

Fever is a common sign that your baby&#x27;s body is fighting an infection. While most fevers are harmless, there are clear guidelines to help you decide when to contact your pediatrician.

What is a Fever?

A fever is defined as a body temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher. The normal body temperature is around 37°C, and it&#x27;s important to remember that fever itself is not dangerous—it is the body&#x27;s natural way of coping with viruses and bacteria.

When to Call the Doctor for a 9-Month-Old

You should call your pediatrician in the following situations:

Fever lasts more than 2–3 days without other symptoms .

Your baby has a fever and is showing signs of dehydration —not drinking well, urinating less than once every 8 hours, no tears when crying, or dry mouth [citation:7].

The fever is accompanied by other concerning symptoms like a severe headache, earache, stiff neck, or repeated vomiting/diarrhea [citation:8].

You are worried about your baby&#x27;s condition, even if you&#x27;re not sure why [citation:7].

Signs That Require Immediate Medical Attention

You should contact your doctor right away or go to the emergency room if your baby has a fever and any of the following &quot;red flags&quot;:

Difficulty breathing — fast breathing, retractions (sucking in muscles under the ribs), wheezing, or grunting [citation:8].

Altered consciousness — extreme lethargy, difficulty waking up, or unusual irritability.

A stiff neck or bulging fontanelle (soft spot on the head) [citation:8].

A rash that does not fade when pressed (non-blanching rash).

A seizure (febrile convulsion), especially if it lasts longer than 5 minutes.

Signs of severe dehydration.

Fever lasting 5 days or more.

Important Considerations

For infants under 3 months of age, any fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher requires immediate medical attention.

If your baby looks well, is drinking, and behaving normally, you can wait and monitor the fever for a day or two.

If you are ever unsure about your baby&#x27;s condition, it is always best to trust your instincts and contact a healthcare professional [citation:7].
