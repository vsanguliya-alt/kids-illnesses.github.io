Fever in Children: Emergency Signs You Must Not Ignore

Fever is a common symptom in young children, and most cases are caused by mild viral infections that resolve on their own. However, certain signs accompanying a fever indicate a medical emergency. Contact your healthcare provider or seek emergency care immediately if your child shows any of the following warning signs.

Emergency Signs in Infants and Children

According to the NIH, it is critical to contact a provider right away if your child has any of the following (Source: NIH):

Change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy – this is a key red flag in children (Source: NIH)

Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days (Source: NIH)

Six or more loose stools in a single day (Source: NIH)

Severe pain in the abdomen (belly) or rectum (Source: NIH)

Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus – this indicates possible internal bleeding (Source: NIH)

Symptoms of dehydration, such as:

Thirst and dry mouth

Headache

Dark-colored urine

Urinating less than normal (Source: NIH)

Signs of Severe Illness or Anaphylaxis

Fever accompanied by these symptoms requires immediate emergency attention (Source: NHS):

Sudden swelling of the lips, mouth, throat, or tongue

Breathing very fast or struggling to breathe – you may become very wheezy or feel like you&#x27;re choking or gasping for air

Throat feels tight or struggling to swallow

Skin, tongue, or lips turn blue, grey, or pale – on darker skin, this may be easier to see on the palms of the hands or soles of the feet

Suddenly becoming very confused, drowsy, or dizzy

Fainting (Source: NHS)

Fever with Rash: Scarlet Fever Alert

Scarlet fever is a contagious infection that mostly affects young children. The first signs can include flu-like symptoms such as a high temperature, a sore throat, and swollen neck glands (a large lump on the side of the neck) (Source: NHS). If your child has a fever with a sore throat and a rash, seek medical evaluation.

Key Takeaway

Trust your instincts. If your child looks or acts significantly unwell, or if you are worried, seek medical help. Never delay seeking emergency care for a child with fever who is not responding normally, has difficulty breathing, or shows signs of dehydration.
