Diarrhea in a 6-Month-Old: How Long It Lasts and What to Do

Diarrhea in a 6-month-old can be concerning, but it is often a short-term issue. Most cases of acute diarrhea—which is diarrhea that lasts a short time—resolve on their own . This guide will help you understand the typical duration of diarrhea and when it may signal a more serious problem.

How Long Does Diarrhea Typically Last?

Acute diarrhea usually lasts about one or two days, but it may last longer . In many cases, it goes away on its own without the need for medical treatment.

In young children, a common cause of diarrhea is a virus like rotavirus, which is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children . For rotavirus infections specifically, vomiting and diarrhea may last from three to eight days . These viral infections typically run their course within this timeframe, but you should monitor your child closely for signs that they are recovering or if the condition is worsening.

When to Call the Doctor

While acute diarrhea is often mild, there are times when you should contact your child&#x27;s healthcare provider. You should seek medical advice immediately if your baby&#x27;s diarrhea:

Lasts more than a few days . If acute diarrhea persists beyond a few days, it could be a sign of a more serious problem.

Is accompanied by a high fever .

Causes signs of dehydration, such as a dry mouth, no tears when crying, or fewer wet diapers.

Contains blood or pus .

Is associated with severe abdominal pain (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Understanding Dehydration Risk

Infants are especially vulnerable to dehydration because they have smaller fluid reserves. Signs of dehydration in a 6-month-old include:

Sunken eyes

A sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the top of the head

No wet diapers for 3 hours or more

No tears when crying

Dry mouth or lips

What You Can Do at Home

While your baby has diarrhea, you can help them feel better by:

Keeping them hydrated by offering small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution (like Pedialyte) or breastmilk/formula.

Monitoring their wet diapers to ensure they are not becoming dehydrated.

Contacting your pediatrician for advice, especially if you are worried about your baby&#x27;s health.
