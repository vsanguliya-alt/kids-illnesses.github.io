Toddler Constipation Relief: A Parent's Guide

Constipation is a common issue in toddlers, affecting up to 30% of children . It typically involves having fewer bowel movements than normal and passing hard, dry, or painful stools .

Recognizing the Signs

A toddler may be constipated if they:

Have fewer than three complete bowel movements a week (unless exclusively breastfed) Pass dry, hard stools that are large or small like "rabbit droppings" Experience pain or straining during bowel movements Cross legs, rock, or twist to avoid passing stool Have a swollen abdomen or bloating Have soiling accidents in their underwear Dietary Changes

Increase Fiber Intake

Gradually add more high-fiber foods to your child's diet. Good sources include :

Fruits: pears, prunes, peaches, plums, apples with skin, berries

Vegetables: broccoli, carrots, green peas

Whole grains: whole wheat bread, oatmeal, bran cereals

Legumes: beans, lentils, chickpeas

The "P" Fruit Rule: Fruits starting with "P" are particularly helpful—prunes, peaches, pears, pineapple, and papaya .

Boost Fluids

Fiber needs water to work effectively . Ensure your toddler drinks plenty of water throughout the day . Prune or apple juice can also help due to their sorbitol content .

Limit Dairy and Processed Foods

Excessive milk and cheese can be binding . For children aged 1 year and above, limit milk to no more than 360ml per day . Avoid foods low in fiber, such as chips, fast food, and processed snacks .

Behavioral Strategies

Post-meal toilet routine: Have your child sit on the toilet for 5minutes after meals to take advantage of the natural reflex triggered by eating .

Reward system: Use a sticker chart to encourage regular toilet use .

Take a break from potty training: If your child is holding in stool, pause potty training until the constipation resolves .

When to See a Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider if constipation:

Lasts more than 2 weeks or doesn't improve with home treatment .

Is accompanied by vomiting, fever, swollen belly, weight loss, blood in stool, or severe abdominal pain .

Important: Never give a child laxatives unless directed by a doctor .
