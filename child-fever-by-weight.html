How to Dose Fever Medication for Children: By Weight, Not Age

When your child has a fever, giving the correct dose of medication is essential for safety and effectiveness. The biggest mistake parents make is giving the wrong amount . Always dose antipyretic (fever-reducing) medications by your child&#x27;s weight in kilograms (kg), not their age . If you do not know your child&#x27;s weight, use their age as a guide, but weight is always preferred .

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) Dosing

Paracetamol is a safe and effective fever reducer for children .

Standard single dose: 10–15 mg per kg of body weight .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 60 mg/kg per day . A common safe maximum is 75 mg/kg/day, but 60 mg/kg/day is often cited for children .

Frequency: Give every 4–6 hours as needed . Do not give more than 4–5 doses in 24 hours .

Age restriction: Do not give paracetamol to infants under 3 months of age without a doctor&#x27;s guidance. Fever in a baby under 12 weeks requires immediate medical attention .

Important: Liquid paracetamol for children is typically available as 160 mg per 5 mL. Always use the oral syringe or dosing cup provided with the medicine .

Ibuprofen Dosing

Ibuprofen is also effective for fever and pain in children .

Standard single dose: 5–10 mg per kg of body weight .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 30–40 mg/kg per day .

Frequency: Give every 6–8 hours as needed . Do not give more than 3–4 doses in 24 hours .

Age restriction: Do not give ibuprofen to infants under 6 months of age unless directed by a doctor, as its safety in this age group is not established .

Combination Products and Alternating Medications

Combination products: Avoid giving children under 6 years of age medicines that contain more than one active ingredient (e.g., a cold medicine that includes paracetamol). This reduces the risk of accidental overdose .

Alternating medications: The American Academy of Pediatrics generally does not recommend alternating between paracetamol and ibuprofen, as it can increase the risk of dosing errors . Use one medication consistently, and only consider switching if it is not effective, under the guidance of a pediatrician.

Clinical Guideline Principle

The decision to give fever medication should be based on the child&#x27;s degree of suffering and impairment, not solely on the number on the thermometer . Antipyretics should not be given routinely; they are intended to make the child more comfortable . Treat the child, not the number.
