Baby Cough: Safe Home Remedies and When to Seek Help

A cough in a baby can be concerning, but it&#x27;s often a normal part of a viral cold. It&#x27;s important to know that you should not give cold or cough medicines to children under two years old (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). Instead, there are safe and gentle ways to help your baby feel more comfortable at home.

Safe Home Care for a Cough

These remedies are safe, gentle, and can provide significant relief for your baby&#x27;s cough.

1. Use a Cool Mist Humidifier

Adding moisture to the air is one of the most effective ways to soothe a cough. Use a cool mist humidifier in your baby&#x27;s room, especially while they are sleeping (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). The moist air helps to ease dryness and irritation in the airways, making it easier for them to breathe.

2. Use Saline Nose Drops and Nasal Suctioning

A cough is often worse when a stuffy nose causes mucus to drip down the back of the throat (post-nasal drip). Use saline nose drops or sprays to loosen mucus and then use a bulb syringe to suction it out (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines). This is a safe and effective method to clear your baby&#x27;s airways and reduce coughing.

3. Offer Plenty of Fluids

Keeping your baby well-hydrated is essential, especially with any illness. Offer them breastmilk, formula, or small amounts of water (if they are over 6 months) frequently.

4. Keep Your Baby Comfortable

Elevating your baby&#x27;s head slightly while they sleep can help with post-nasal drip, but always follow safe sleep guidelines.

When to Call the Doctor

While most coughs are mild and resolve on their own, it is important to know the signs that require medical attention. Call your child&#x27;s health care provider immediately if the cough:

Is accompanied by a high fever.

Has not improved after a few days.

Is causing difficulty breathing. Seek emergency care immediately if your baby is working hard to breathe, breathing faster than usual, or has a &quot;whooping&quot; sound when coughing. Whooping cough (pertussis) is a serious infection that can cause severe coughing fits, especially in young babies (Source: NHS | Whooping cough).
