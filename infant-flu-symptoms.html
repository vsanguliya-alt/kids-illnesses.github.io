Flu Symptoms in Infants: What Parents Need to Know

Symptoms of the flu (influenza) come on suddenly and may include fever or feeling feverish with chills (Source: NIH). Infants may also have vomiting and diarrhea as part of their flu illness (Source: NIH).

Common Flu Symptoms in Infants

In babies, flu symptoms can include:

Fever – may be high; often comes on suddenly

Chills or shivering – baby may feel cold to the touch

Cough – dry or persistent

Sore throat – may cause feeding difficulties

Runny or stuffy nose – nasal congestion

Body aches – may cause fussiness or irritability

Fatigue – unusual tiredness or lethargy

Vomiting and diarrhea – more common in infants than in older children (Source: NIH)

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider

If your infant has symptoms of flu and is very sick or you are worried about your illness, contact your health care provider (Source: NIH). Certain people are more likely to have complications from the flu, including infants and young children (Source: NIH).

Seek immediate medical attention if your baby has flu symptoms and:

Is under 3 months old with any fever

Has difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Has blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Is limp, floppy, or difficult to wake

Is not feeding well or refuses to feed

Shows signs of dehydration (fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, no tears)

Has a seizure

Has symptoms that improve but then return or worsen

Diagnosis

To diagnose the flu, health care providers will first do a medical history and ask about your symptoms (Source: NIH). They may also perform a rapid flu test using a nasal swab.

Prevention

The flu vaccine is the best way to prevent flu in infants and children. The vaccine is recommended for all children aged 6 months and older. For infants under 6 months, the best protection is vaccination of all household members and caregivers.
