When Should You Worry About Baby Fever At Night?

Nighttime fevers in babies are common and often harmless, but knowing when to worry and when to monitor at home is essential for every parent. Trust your intuition—if you have concerns about your baby's health, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH | Uncommon Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Here is what pediatricians want you to know about managing fever at night.

When Is Fever at Night Normal?

Fevers often spike in the evening due to the body's natural circadian rhythm. If your baby has a mild fever (below 100.4°F / 38°C in a baby under 3 months, or below 102°F / 38.9°C in an older baby) but is sleeping comfortably, feeding well during waking hours, and is easily rousable, you can monitor at home without waking them for medication. Sleep supports recovery, and treatment is meant for comfort, not to normalize the temperature.

When Should You Worry? (Red Flags at Night)

You should worry and seek medical attention immediately if your baby has fever and any of these signs:

Age under 3 months: Any fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher in a baby under 3 months is a medical emergency—do not wait for morning. Call your pediatrician or go to the ER immediately (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Difficulty breathing: Fast breathing, flaring nostrils, grunting, or chest retractions (skin pulling in around ribs) are red flags requiring urgent care (Source: NIH | Uncommon Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Chest Pain in Infants and Children, American Academy of Family Physicians).

Lethargy or difficulty waking: If your baby is unusually difficult to wake, floppy, or not interacting with you, this is an emergency.

Seizure: Febrile seizures—jerking movements, eye rolling, or loss of consciousness—require immediate emergency care.

Dehydration: Fewer wet diapers than usual (less than 4–6 in 24 hours for an older baby), sunken fontanelle (soft spot), dry mouth, or no tears when crying are signs of dehydration.

Fever lasting more than 3 days: If fever persists beyond 72 hours without improvement, contact your pediatrician.

Fever above 104°F (40°C) that does not respond to medication.

What to Do at Night

If your baby has a fever but is sleeping peacefully:

Do NOT wake them to give medication. Sleep is more important for recovery (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Do NOT overdress them—dress in light clothing and keep the room comfortably cool.

Do NOT use cold baths or alcohol rubs—these can cause shivering and raise body temperature.

If your baby wakes up and is uncomfortable, you may give a weight-appropriate dose of acetaminophen (if over 3 months) or ibuprofen (if over 6 months), following package instructions and your pediatrician's guidance.

The Bottom Line

Nighttime fevers are usually harmless, but they can be stressful. If you are ever uncertain, call your pediatrician—they are used to middle-of-the-night calls. It is always better to check than to worry. Trust your intuition—if something feels wrong, seek medical attention (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor for a baby fever at night?

Call immediately if your baby is under 3 months with a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, has difficulty breathing, is lethargic, has a seizure, or shows signs of dehydration. If you are unsure, call your pediatrician—they are available 24/7 for advice (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Q: How long does a baby fever last at night?

Most viral fevers in babies last 2 to 3 days. If the fever persists beyond 72 hours without improvement, contact your pediatrician for evaluation (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Q: Should I wake my baby to give fever medicine at night?

No. If your baby is sleeping comfortably, do not wake them to give medication. Sleep supports recovery. Only give fever medicine if your baby wakes up and is uncomfortable (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Q: What home remedies help a baby's fever at night?

Keep the room comfortably cool, dress your baby in light clothing, and offer extra fluids if they wake up. Do not use cold baths or alcohol rubs—these can be dangerous. Use medication only if your baby is uncomfortable and dose strictly by weight (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Q: Can teething cause a fever at night?

No. Teething does not cause a true fever (above 100.4°F / 38°C). If your baby has a fever at night, it is likely due to an infection, not teething. Contact your pediatrician if you are concerned (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Q: What if my baby has a fever with no other symptoms at night?

This is common with viral infections. If your baby is under 3 months old, call your pediatrician immediately. If your baby is older and is comfortable, feeding well, and easily rousable, you can monitor at home. However, if the fever is above 104°F (40°C) or lasts more than 3 days, contact your pediatrician (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).

Q: When should I go to the ER for a baby fever at night?

Go to the ER immediately if your baby has a fever with difficulty breathing, a seizure, is difficult to wake, shows signs of dehydration (dry mouth, sunken soft spot, no tears), is under 3 months with a fever, or if the fever is above 104°F (40°C) and not responding to medication (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems: MedlinePlus – Sick Baby? When to Seek Medical Attention, Mayo Foundation for Medical Education and Research).
