Ear Infection Symptoms in a Baby: A Parent's Guide

Ear infections are a common childhood ailment. In fact, three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday. This guide will help you recognize the signs of an ear infection in your baby, especially when they can't tell you what's wrong.

What Is an Ear Infection?

The infection usually affects the middle ear and is called otitis media. It occurs when fluid builds up behind the eardrum and becomes infected, often following a cold or respiratory infection.

Recognizing the Signs and Symptoms

Since a baby cannot tell you "My ear hurts," you need to look for other signs. Common symptoms of an ear infection in a baby include:

🚨 Key Signs to Watch For

Discharge running out of the ear : This is a clear indicator that the eardrum may have ruptured .

Fever: Your baby may develop a high temperature .

Irritability or fussiness: A sudden change in mood can be a sign of discomfort.

Trouble sleeping: Lying down can increase ear pain, leading to more night wakings.

Poor appetite.

A feeling of pressure or fullness inside the ear .

Itching and irritation in and around the ear .

Scaly skin in and around the ear .

Additionally, young children and babies with an ear infection may also:

Rub or pull their ear.

Not react to some sounds.

Be irritable or restless.

Have a fever.

Be off their food.

Keep losing their balance.

🔍 What to Do

Most ear infections clear up within 3 days, although sometimes symptoms can last up to a week . If your baby is showing these symptoms, especially if they have a fever or fluid draining from the ear, you should contact your child's health care provider. They can make a proper diagnosis by looking inside the ear with an instrument called an otoscope .

When to Seek Emergency Care

While most ear infections are manageable, it's crucial to know when to seek immediate medical attention. Seek medical help if your baby has symptoms of a serious infection:

Swelling, redness, or tenderness behind the ear (could indicate a dangerous bone infection called mastoiditis).

A stiff neck or severe headache.

Facial weakness or drooping.

A high fever over 104°F (40°C) that does not respond to fever-reducing medication.

Lethargy or confusion.

Always trust your instincts. If your baby looks or acts very ill, seek medical help immediately.
