Baby Runny Nose: Safe Treatment Options

A runny nose is a common symptom of a cold in babies. While it can be uncomfortable for your child, there are safe and effective ways to help them feel better without resorting to unnecessary medications.

Understanding a Runny Nose

A runny nose is often caused by a viral infection like the common cold. It can also be a symptom of non-allergic rhinitis, which happens when the inside of your nose becomes irritated, causing a blocked or runny nose (Source: NHS | Non-allergic rhinitis). The first signs of whooping cough are also similar to a cold, such as a runny nose and sore throat (Source: NHS | Whooping cough). While there is no cure for the cold itself, you can manage the symptoms to keep your baby comfortable.

Safe Treatment Options

Instead of medication, you can use these safe and proven methods to relieve your baby's runny nose:

1. Nasal Suctioning with a Bulb Syringe

Use nasal suctioning with a bulb syringe, which can be very helpful in children under a year old. This is a very effective way to clear mucus from your baby's nasal passages, making it easier for them to breathe and feed. For best results, use it after applying saline drops.

2. Use of Saline Drops

Saline nose drops or sprays are gentle and can help thin and loosen nasal mucus, making suctioning more effective. A pharmacist can advise you about medicines that can help, such as salt water nasal sprays or solutions to rinse out the inside of your nose (Source: NHS | Non-allergic rhinitis). This is a safe, medication-free option for babies.

3. Using a Cool-Mist Humidifier

Dry air can worsen a stuffy nose. Using a cool mist humidifier in your baby's room can add moisture to the air and help soothe their irritated nasal passages.

When to Seek Medical Attention

While a runny nose is usually not serious, you should contact your pediatrician if it is accompanied by:

A high fever.

Signs of dehydration, such as fewer wet diapers.

Difficulty breathing that worries you.

Symptoms that last longer than a week.

Be aware that a runny nose can also be a symptom of a serious allergic reaction called anaphylaxis, along with sneezing (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis). If this is accompanied by swelling of the lips or tongue, difficulty breathing, or a rash (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis), call 911 immediately, as this is a medical emergency (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis). Also, contact your doctor if your baby is under 6 months old and has symptoms of whooping cough, which begins like a cold (Source: NHS | Whooping cough).
