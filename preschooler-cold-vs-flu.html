Cold vs. Flu in Preschoolers: What Parents Need to Know

The common cold is a mild infection of your upper respiratory tract (which includes your nose and throat). More than 200 different viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common type (Source: NIH). The flu (influenza) is a different illness that can be more severe. Sometimes people have trouble figuring out whether they have a cold or the flu (Source: NIH).

Cold Symptoms

The symptoms of a common cold usually start a few days after you become infected with the virus (Source: NIH). Cold symptoms are generally milder than flu symptoms and may include:

Runny or stuffy nose

Sneezing

Cough

Sore throat

Mild fever (in some cases)

Flu Symptoms

The flu comes on more suddenly and is often more severe. While the sources do not list specific flu symptoms for preschoolers, acute coughs are the kind you most often get with a cold, flu, or acute bronchitis (Source: NIH). Flu symptoms may include high fever, severe cough, body aches, and extreme tiredness. Symptoms of the flu come on suddenly.

Telling the Difference

Sometimes people have trouble figuring out whether they have a cold or the flu (Source: NIH). In preschoolers, telling the difference can be especially difficult because children cannot always describe their symptoms well. The key difference is often the severity: the flu tends to come on suddenly and cause more severe symptoms, while a cold develops gradually and is milder.

Treatment

There is no cure for the common cold. But there are treatments that can make you feel better while you wait for the cold to go away on its own (Source: NIH):

Drinking plenty of fluids

Taking over-the-counter pain or cold and cough medicines

Important Safety Rules:

Some cold and cough medicines contain ingredients that are not recommended for children. Talk with your child&#x27;s health care provider before giving your child any cold and cough medicines (Source: NIH).

Some cold and cough medicines contain pain relievers. Some contain other active ingredients (Source: NIH).

Never give aspirin to a child with a cold or flu.

Cough Treatment Note: If you have a cold or the flu, antihistamines may work better than non-prescription cough medicines (Source: NIH). However, always consult your healthcare provider before giving any medication to your preschooler.

When to Call the Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider if your preschooler has:

Fever that lasts more than 3 days

Difficulty breathing

Symptoms that improve but then return or worsen

Signs of dehydration

You are worried about your child
