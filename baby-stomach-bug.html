Stomach Bug in a Baby: Home Care and When to Seek Help

Stomach bugs (gastroenteritis) are common in babies. The illness is typically caused by viruses and leads to vomiting and diarrhea. While seeing your baby sick is concerning, most cases can be managed at home. The most important goal is to prevent dehydration, which happens when a baby loses more fluid than they take in.

How to Care for Your Baby at Home

The best way to help your baby recover is to provide small amounts of fluid frequently. Start with 1 teaspoon (5 mL) every 5 minutes. If they keep that down, gradually increase the amount. Good options include:

Oral Rehydration Solutions (ORS) like Pedialyte or Dioralyte.

Breast milk or formula, given little and often.

Water, if your baby is over 6 months old, given in small sips.

Avoid fruit juice and fizzy drinks, as they can make diarrhea worse.

Food can wait. Do not worry if your baby does not want to eat for a while. Once vomiting has stopped for 8 hours, you can slowly start offering bland foods like rice cereal or applesauce.

When to Call Your Pediatrician

Call your baby&#x27;s doctor right away if your baby has any of these signs, which could indicate dehydration or a more serious problem:

No wet diaper for 6 to 8 hours.

Sunken eyes.

Dry mouth or lips, or crying without tears.

Vomiting that lasts more than 24 hours.

High fever (over 102°F / 38.9°C).

Bloody diarrhea or vomit.

Severe or worsening tummy pain.

Understanding Reflux vs. Stomach Bug

It&#x27;s important to distinguish between a stomach bug and reflux, which is when food or milk comes back up from your baby&#x27;s stomach into the esophagus (Source: NIH | Reflux in Infants). Reflux, or spitting up, is common in babies because the muscle between the esophagus and stomach is not fully developed (Source: NIH | Reflux in Infants). As your baby grows, this muscle matures and they should no longer spit up (Source: NIH | Reflux in Infants). Unlike a stomach bug, reflux typically does not cause diarrhea or fever.

🚨 Seek Immediate Emergency Care If Your Baby Has:

These are signs of a serious condition:

Green or bloody vomit.

Being very sleepy, confused, or hard to wake up.

Breathing very fast or struggling to breathe.

A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass (the &quot;glass test&quot;).
