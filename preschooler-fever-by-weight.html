Dosing Fever Medication for Preschoolers: By Weight, Not Age

When your preschooler has a fever, giving the correct dose of medication is essential for safety and effectiveness. The single most important rule is to dose by your child&#x27;s weight in kilograms (kg), not their age . If you do not know your child&#x27;s weight, you can use their age as a secondary guide, but weight is always preferred .

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) Dosing

Paracetamol is a safe and effective first-line medication for reducing fever and pain in young children .

Standard single dose: 10–15 mg per kg of body weight per dose .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 60–75 mg per kg per day, typically divided into no more than 4 doses in 24 hours .

Frequency: Give every 4–6 hours as needed, with a minimum of 4 hours between doses . Do not give more than 4 doses in 24 hours .

Age restriction: Paracetamol should not be given to children under 2 years of age without a doctor&#x27;s guidance . Fever in a baby under 12 weeks requires immediate medical attention .

Ibuprofen Dosing

Ibuprofen is also effective for fever and pain in children.

Standard single dose: 5–10 mg per kg of body weight per dose .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 30–40 mg per kg per day, typically divided into no more than 3 doses in 24 hours .

Frequency: Give every 6–8 hours as needed . Do not give more than 3 doses in 24 hours .

Age restriction: Do not give ibuprofen to children under 6 months of age unless directed by a doctor .

Important Safety Rules

Choose one medication. For most children, the American Academy of Pediatrics does not recommend alternating between paracetamol and ibuprofen, as it increases the risk of dosing errors .

Use the correct measuring tool. Always use the syringe or dosing cup that comes with the medicine. Never use a kitchen teaspoon or tablespoon .

Check for combination products. Do not give medicines with more than one active ingredient to children under 6 years of age. Many cold remedies also contain paracetamol – check labels to avoid accidental overdose .

Treat the child, not the number. The decision to give fever medication should be based on the child&#x27;s discomfort and degree of suffering, not solely on the thermometer reading .
