Fever in a 2-Year-Old: When to Call the Doctor

Fever is a common sign that your child&#x27;s body is fighting an infection. While most fevers are harmless and resolve on their own, knowing when to contact your pediatrician is important for your child&#x27;s safety.

What is a Fever?

A fever is generally considered a temperature of 38.5°C (101.3°F) or more in children, and 38.0°C (100.4°F) or more in babies under three months . For the most reliable reading, use a digital thermometer rectally in children under 4 years of age .

When to Call the Doctor

You should contact your child&#x27;s healthcare provider if your 2-year-old has a fever and:

The fever lasts longer than three days .

They are very ill, unresponsive, restless, or confused .

They have a stiff neck .

They have difficulty breathing or are breathing very quickly .

They have a skin rash that doesn&#x27;t fade when pressed (for example, with a clear glass) .

They are in a lot of pain or have an unusually shrill cry .

They refuse to drink or show signs of dehydration (e.g., no urine for 12 hours, sunken eyes, dry mouth) .

Symptoms have worsened since last being seen by a doctor .

Trust Your Instincts

If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away. Fever in children can be caused by many things, from harmless viral infections to more serious conditions .
