Fever in a 2-Year-Old: When to Call the Doctor

Fever is a common sign that your child's body is fighting an infection. While most fevers are harmless, there are clear guidelines to help you decide when to contact your pediatrician.

What is a Fever?

A fever is defined as a body temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher . The normal body temperature is around 37°C, and it's important to remember that fever itself is not dangerous—it is the body's natural way of coping with viruses and bacteria.

When to Call the Doctor

For a 2-year-old, most fevers can be managed at home . However, you should call your pediatrician in the following situations:

Fever lasting more than 3 days Temperature rises above 104°F (40°C) repeatedly Your child still "acts sick" once their fever is brought down Your child seems to be getting worse Signs of dehydration —not drinking well, urinating less than once every 8 hours, no tears when crying, or dry mouth The fever is accompanied by other concerning symptoms like a severe headache, earache, stiff neck, repeated vomiting or diarrhea, or an unexplained rash Signs That Require Immediate Medical Attention

You should go to an emergency room or call 911 if your child has a fever and any of the following "red flags":

Difficulty breathing — fast breathing, retractions (sucking in muscles under the ribs), wheezing, or grunting Extreme drowsiness or difficulty waking A stiff neck or sensitivity to light A rash that does not fade when pressed (non-blanching rash) A seizure (febrile convulsion), especially if it lasts longer than 5 minutes Blue lips, tongue, or nails A bulging fontanelle (soft spot on the head) Important Considerations

If your child looks well, is drinking, and behaving normally, you can wait and monitor the fever for a day or two .

Trust your instincts. If you are worried about your child's condition, contact your pediatrician. Parents are the best judges of whether their child is behaving abnormally .

If you are ever unsure, it is always best to seek medical advice .
