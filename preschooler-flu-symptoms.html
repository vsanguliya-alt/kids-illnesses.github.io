Flu Symptoms in Preschoolers: What Parents Need to Know

The flu (influenza) is a viral infection that can affect children of all ages. Symptoms of the flu come on suddenly and may include fever or feeling feverish with chills (Source: NIH). Some people may also have vomiting and diarrhea (Source: NIH).

Common Flu Symptoms in Preschoolers

In preschool-aged children, flu symptoms can include:

Fever – may be high; often comes on suddenly

Chills or shivering – child may feel cold to the touch

Cough – dry or persistent

Sore throat – may cause feeding difficulties

Runny or stuffy nose – nasal congestion

Body aches – may cause fussiness or irritability

Fatigue – unusual tiredness or lethargy

Vomiting and diarrhea – more common in young children than in adults (Source: NIH)

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider

If your preschooler has symptoms of flu and is very sick or you are worried about your illness, contact your health care provider (Source: NIH). Certain people are more likely to have complications from the flu, including those with chronic health conditions such as asthma, diabetes, and heart disease (Source: NIH).

Seek immediate medical attention if your child has flu symptoms and:

Has difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Has blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Is limp, floppy, or difficult to wake

Is not feeding well or refuses to feed

Shows signs of dehydration

Has a seizure

Has symptoms that improve but then return or worsen

Important Complication: Reye&#x27;s Syndrome

Never give aspirin to a child with flu symptoms. Symptoms of Reye&#x27;s syndrome often start 1 to 3 weeks after a child recovers from a virus like flu or chickenpox (Source: NHS). Reye&#x27;s syndrome is a rare but serious condition that can cause swelling in the liver and brain.

Diagnosis

To diagnose the flu, health care providers will first do a medical history and ask about your symptoms (Source: NIH). They may also perform a rapid flu test using a nasal swab.

Prevention

The flu vaccine is the best way to prevent flu in preschoolers. The vaccine is recommended for all children aged 6 months and older.
