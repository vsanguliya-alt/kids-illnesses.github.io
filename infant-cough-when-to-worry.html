Infant Cough: When to Worry

It can be scary when your baby is sick, especially when it is not an everyday problem like a cold or a fever [citation:0]. While most coughs in babies are caused by viral infections and will resolve on their own, certain signs indicate a medical emergency.

Seek Emergency Care Immediately (Call 999/112)

Take your baby to the nearest Emergency Department immediately if they have any of the following signs:

Breathing Difficulty: Struggling for each breath, breathing that is very fast, or breathing that stops or pauses [citation:0].

Blue or Pale Skin: Their skin, lips, or tongue turn blue, pale, or grey [citation:0]. On darker skin, check the palms of the hands and soles of the feet [citation:0].

Abnormal Sounds: Grunting with each breath out or a harsh, high-pitched noise when breathing in (stridor) that is present even when the baby is calm [citation:0].

Altered Mental State: The baby is limp, floppy, difficult to wake up, very drowsy, or confused [citation:0].

Seizure or Fit: Having a seizure .

Choking: You suspect the baby has choked on a small object [citation:0].

Fever in a Very Young Infant: A baby under 3 months old with a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or above [citation:0]. Note: This does not apply if the fever is in the 48 hours following vaccinations and there are no other concerning symptoms .

Contact Your Healthcare Provider Urgently

You should seek medical advice from a GP or call a health helpline (such as 111) if your baby has:

Signs of Dehydration: Fewer than 3 wet nappies in 24 hours, sunken eyes, or a very dry mouth [citation:0].

Persistent Fever: A temperature of 38°C or above for more than 5 days .

Feeding Difficulty: Taking less than half of their normal amount of milk .

Excessive Irritability: Unable to be settled .

Worsening Cough: The cough is getting worse or has lasted more than 3 weeks .

Vomiting: Coughing causes vomiting [citation:0].

General Advice

Trust Your Instincts: If your baby looks or acts very sick, or you are simply worried, contact your healthcare provider right away [citation:0].

Safe Care: Do not give cough or cold medicines to children under two years of age . Do not give honey to infants under 1 year old .
