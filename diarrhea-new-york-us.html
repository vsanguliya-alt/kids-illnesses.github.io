Diarrhea in Children: A New York Parent's Guide

Diarrhea is a common illness in children. It is defined as having three or more loose, watery stools in a single day . While most cases are mild and short-lived, diarrhea can lead to serious dehydration if not managed properly. Rotavirus is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children, and the infection often causes vomiting as well .

Common Causes

The most frequent causes of diarrhea in children include viruses, bacteria, and food allergies.

Viral Infections: Rotavirus and norovirus are common culprits. These viruses are highly contagious and spread easily in daycare settings .

Bacterial Infections: Harmful bacteria like E. coli

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call a doctor about diarrhea?

If your child has difficulty breathing, persistent high fever, lethargy, or symptoms lasting beyond expected, contact a healthcare provider.

Is diarrhea normal at this age?

Many variations are normal. Red-flag signs (listed above) mean you should seek care.

What helps at home?

Hydration, rest, and age-appropriate comfort measures. Always follow NHS/AAP guidance for medications.

How long does it last?

Most childhood episodes resolve within a few days. Persistent or worsening symptoms need evaluation.
