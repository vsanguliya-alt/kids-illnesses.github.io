Vomiting in Children: A Houston Parent's Guide

Vomiting is a common symptom in children, often associated with viral infections like gastroenteritis. The main symptoms of gastroenteritis include vomiting and diarrhea (Source: NIH). While most cases are mild and resolve on their own, vomiting can lead to dehydration, which is a serious concern, especially in young children.

Understanding Vomiting in Children

Vomiting in children is most often caused by a viral infection, sometimes called the "stomach flu." Rotavirus is a common culprit, especially in younger children. These viruses are highly contagious and can cause significant fluid loss.

When to Call Your Pediatrician

It is critical to recognize the warning signs of dehydration and more serious illness. You should contact your child's health care provider right away if they have:

Vomiting that prevents them from keeping fluids down for more than 8 hours.

Signs of dehydration, such as decreased urination, dry mouth, or no tears when crying.

Vomiting that contains blood or a yellow-green color (bile).

A high fever.

Severe abdominal pain.

A stiff neck, severe headache, or sensitivity to light.

A change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for vomiting?

Call your doctor if your child has vomiting that prevents them from keeping fluids down for more than 8 hours, has signs of dehydration, has vomit with blood or bile, a high fever, or severe abdominal pain.

How long does vomiting last?

Vomiting from a viral infection typically lasts 1 to 2 days, but it can sometimes last longer.

What home remedies can help with vomiting?

The most important thing is to keep your child hydrated. Start with small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution (Pedialyte) or clear liquids like water or broth. Avoid sugary drinks and do not force fluids. Once they can keep fluids down, you can slowly introduce bland foods like crackers and toast.

What should I feed my child after vomiting?

Begin with clear liquids and gradually advance to bland foods like crackers, toast, and bananas as your child tolerates them. Avoid dairy, fatty, or spicy foods.

How can I prevent vomiting?

Good handwashing is key to preventing the spread of viruses that cause vomiting. Also, practice safe food handling to avoid food poisoning.
