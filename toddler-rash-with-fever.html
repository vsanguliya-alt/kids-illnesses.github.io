Toddler Rash with Fever: What Parents Need to Know

The sources provided contain information about one specific cause of rash with fever in toddlers: scarlet fever. Scarlet fever is a contagious infection that mostly affects young children. The first signs of scarlet fever can be flu-like symptoms, including a high temperature, a sore throat and swollen neck glands. A rash appears 12 to 48 hours later (Source: NHS).

Scarlet Fever: Key Features

If your toddler has scarlet fever, the rash has specific characteristics (Source: NHS):

The rash makes your skin feel rough, like sandpaper

On white skin the rash looks pink or red

On brown and black skin it might be harder to see a change in colour, but you can still feel the rash and see the raised bumps

A white coating also appears on the tongue

What Parents Should Know

While the sources provide information about scarlet fever, the most important takeaway for parents is:

A rash with fever in a toddler should be evaluated by a healthcare provider to determine the cause and appropriate treatment.

If your toddler has a rash with fever, contact your healthcare provider if:

The rash does not fade when pressed with a glass (non-blanching rash) – this requires immediate medical attention

Your child has difficulty breathing

Your child is lethargic or difficult to wake

Your child has a stiff neck

Your child is not drinking fluids or shows signs of dehydration

The fever lasts more than 3 days

The rash is painful, blistered, or oozing

You are worried about your child
