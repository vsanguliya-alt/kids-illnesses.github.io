What Parents Should Know

While the sources do not provide specific guidance, the most important concern with any gastrointestinal illness is dehydration. Preschoolers can become dehydrated quickly, which can be dangerous.

Signs of dehydration in preschoolers include:

Decreased urination (fewer wet diapers or trips to the toilet)

Dry mouth and lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes

Unusual drowsiness or irritability

Thirst

When to seek medical help:

Your preschooler has signs of dehydration

Your preschooler has a fever

Vomiting or diarrhea lasts more than 24 hours

You see blood in the stool or vomit

Your child has severe abdominal pain

Your child is not able to keep fluids down

You are worried about your child

Distinguishing from Reflux: It is important to differentiate a stomach bug from reflux. Reflux (GER) occurs when food or stomach acid flows back from the stomach into the esophagus, and is common in healthy infants (Source: NIH). Unlike a stomach bug, reflux does not typically cause diarrhea or fever.

Trust your instincts – if you are worried about your child, call your health care provider right away.
