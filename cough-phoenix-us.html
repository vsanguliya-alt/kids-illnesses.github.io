Cough in Children: A Phoenix Parent's Guide

A cough is one of the most common reasons parents bring a child to the doctor in Phoenix, especially during the dry winter months and the region's prolonged allergy seasons. Most childhood coughs are caused by viral infections and are temporary, lasting about one to two weeks as the body clears the mucus and repairs the airway irritation. However, knowing how to manage a cough, when to use home remedies, and when to seek medical care is key.

Understanding a Child's Cough

Coughing is a natural and important reflex that helps children clear mucus, debris, or irritants from their airways. Most coughs in children are due to viral upper respiratory infections, such as the common cold, which can also cause congestion, runny nose, and sore throat. Healthy preschool children in daycare can have up to eight viral respiratory infections with a cough every year, each lasting about 10 days.

When to Call Your Pediatrician

Contact your pediatrician if your child's cough persists for more than three weeks, or if the cough causes them to vomit frequently. You should also seek medical advice if a cough or cold gets worse instead of better, particularly with a fever that comes on later in the illness, as this may indicate a developing bacterial infection.

When to Go to the Emergency Room

Seek emergency care immediately if your child has a cough and any of these signs:

Difficulty breathing or labored breathing, especially with belly breathing

A seal-like barking cough (which can indicate croup)

Stridor (noisy breathing that is high-pitched or creaky)

Wheezing (a whistling sound on the exhale)

Blue or grey lips, tongue, or face

Severe malaise: not interacting, lethargic, or has no appetite

Home Treatment

Treat the child, not the cough. If your child has a cough but is otherwise acting normally, they may not need medication.

Honey (for children over 1 year): A teaspoon of honey may help loosen a cough and soothe a sore throat. Children ages 1 and older can be given 0.5 to 1 teaspoon (2.5 to 5 milliliters) of honey to treat a cough. Never give honey to children younger than 1 year old because honey can contain bacteria harmful to babies.

Saline nose drops or sprays: Saline helps flush the nasal cavity of mucus that causes cough and moisturizes the nasal passages.

Cool-mist humidifier: Using a cool-mist humidifier at night helps thin mucus and ease congestion. Choose cool mist instead of hot water to prevent burns.

Steam therapy: Standing in a steamy shower for 10 minutes can help loosen phlegm. For a croup-like cough, the combination of steam followed by cold air tends to quiet down the cough.

Hydration: Encourage your child to drink plenty of fluids. Warm liquids can soothe an irritated throat.

Prop your child's head up: When kids lie flat, mucus can build up in the sinuses, interfering with restful sleep.

Cough medicine: Over-the-counter cough and cold medications should not be given to children under the age of 4 due to risks of serious side effects. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends against giving children younger than 4 over-the-counter cold and cough medications.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for a cough?

Call your doctor if your child's cough lasts more than 3 weeks, worsens instead of improving, or interferes with daily activities like sleeping, playing, or eating.

How long does a cough last?

Most colds take three to five days to peak, but the symptoms can last up to two weeks—so coughing for 10 to 14 days for a common cold is not unusual, especially in pediatrics. A post-viral cough can take up to three weeks to disappear as the trachea repairs itself.

What home remedies can help a child's cough?

Honey (for children over 1 year), a cool-mist humidifier, steam therapy, saline nose drops, and plenty of fluids are effective home remedies to soothe a cough.

Is it safe to give my child cough medicine?

No. Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines are not recommended for children under 4 years old. For children ages 4 to 6, they should only be used if recommended by a healthcare provider.

What does whooping cough sound like?

Whooping cough causes coughing fits that may be followed by a high-pitched "whoop" sound when the child inhales. The cough can cause vomiting and exhaustion.

How can I help my child sleep with a cough?

Prop up your child's head with a pillow to reduce mucus buildup, use a cool-mist humidifier in their room, and consider giving honey before bed (if over 1 year old) to soothe the cough.
