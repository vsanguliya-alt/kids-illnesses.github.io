Causes of Wheezing in a Baby

Wheezing is a high-pitched whistling sound that occurs when a baby breathes out, indicating that the airways in the lungs are narrowed or partially blocked. In a baby, this symptom can have several different causes, some of which require immediate medical attention.

Viral Infections: The Most Common Trigger

By far, the most frequent cause of a sudden episode of wheezing in infants is a viral respiratory infection, often from viruses like respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), rhinovirus, or human metapneumovirus. In children under 2, this is frequently diagnosed as bronchiolitis, a common chest infection that causes inflammation and mucus buildup in the small airways (bronchioles). This can cause a cough that often brings up mucus, shortness of breath, wheezing, a low fever, and chest tightness (Source: NIH | Acute Bronchitis).

Anaphylaxis: A Life-Threatening Emergency

It is crucial to be aware that wheezing can be a sign of a severe allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. This is a life-threatening emergency that requires immediate action. The most common causes in infants are reactions to foods (especially peanuts), medications, and stinging insects (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis).

If your baby has wheezing along with any of these other signs, call 911 immediately (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis):

Skin symptoms: Itching, hives, redness, or swelling

Mouth symptoms: Itching or swelling of the lips or tongue

Throat symptoms: Tightness, trouble swallowing, or swelling of the back of the throat

Breathing problems: Shortness of breath, coughing, or chest tightness (in addition to wheezing)

Other signs: Dizziness, fainting, or vomiting

Asthma and Reflux: Other Possibilities

While less common in this age group, asthma can also cause wheezing. Asthma often starts during childhood, usually before age 5, and can cause coughing, especially at night or early morning, breathing problems, and wheezing (Source: NIH | Asthma in Children).

Additionally, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) can cause wheezing or trouble breathing in infants (Source: NIH | Reflux in Infants). Reflux occurs when food or milk comes back up from the stomach into the esophagus, and in some cases can lead to symptoms like wheezing (Source: NIH | Reflux in Infants). Contact your baby&#x27;s health care provider if your baby has these symptoms, especially if your baby isn&#x27;t gaining weight (Source: NIH | Reflux in Infants).
