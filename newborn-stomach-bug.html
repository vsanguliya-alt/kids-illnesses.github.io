What Parents Should Know

While the sources do not provide specific guidance, the most important concern with any newborn gastrointestinal illness is dehydration. Newborns can become dehydrated quickly, which can be dangerous.

Signs of dehydration in newborns include:

Fewer wet diapers than usual (less than 6 in 24 hours)

Dry mouth and lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes or a sunken soft spot on the head

Unusual drowsiness or irritability

When to seek medical help:

Your newborn is under 3 months old with any vomiting or diarrhea

Your newborn is not feeding well or refusing to eat

Your newborn has signs of dehydration

Your newborn has a fever

Vomiting or diarrhea lasts more than 24 hours in a newborn

You see blood in the stool or vomit

You are worried about your newborn

Distinguishing from Reflux: It is important to differentiate a stomach bug from reflux. Reflux (GER) occurs when food or stomach acid flows back from the stomach into the esophagus, and is common in healthy infants (Source: NIH). Unlike a stomach bug, reflux does not typically cause diarrhea or fever.

Trust your instincts – if you are worried about your newborn, call your health care provider right away.
