Wheezing in Children: A Chicago Parent's Guide

Wheezing is a high-pitched whistling sound that occurs when a child breathes out. It is a sign that the airways in the lungs are narrowed or blocked. While it can be alarming, it is important to understand the potential causes and know when to seek medical help in Chicago.

Common Causes of Wheezing

Acute Bronchitis: This is an inflammation of the larger airways that can cause shortness of breath, wheezing, a low fever, and chest tightness . In some cases, a child may need inhaled medicine to open their airways if they are wheezing .

Asthma: This chronic condition causes the airways to become inflamed and narrow, leading to wheezing, coughing, and tightness in the chest . Symptoms of asthma in children can include coughing (especially at night or early morning), breathing problems like shortness of breath or rapid breathing, and wheezing that causes a whistling sound when they breathe out .

Allergic Reactions: Wheezing can be a symptom of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) . Other symptoms of anaphylaxis can include itching, hives, swelling, shortness of breath, and chest pain or tightness . If you suspect a severe allergic reaction, seek emergency care immediately.

When to Seek Medical Help

Contact your child's health care provider if they have wheezing and:

Have a fever.

Are having trouble breathing or are breathing fast.

Have a cough that doesn't go away.

Are not feeding or eating well.

Look very sick or are not acting like themselves.

In an emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room if your child:

Is having severe difficulty breathing.

Has blue or pale lips or face.

Is struggling to speak or cry.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor for wheezing?

Call your doctor if your child has a fever, is having trouble breathing, has a cough that doesn't go away, is not feeding well, or seems very sick.

How long does wheezing last in children?

The duration of wheezing depends on the cause. With treatment for an asthma flare-up, symptoms often improve within a few hours or days. Bronchitis can cause wheezing for a few days to a week.

What home remedies can help with wheezing?

Home remedies that may help include using a cool-mist humidifier to add moisture to the air, keeping your child hydrated, and ensuring they are in a smoke-free environment. However, if wheezing is severe, seek medical care immediately.

Is wheezing always a sign of asthma?

No, wheezing in children can be caused by other conditions like acute bronchitis or a viral infection, especially in younger children. It is important to have a healthcare provider evaluate your child to determine the exact cause.

What does wheezing sound like in a child?

Wheezing is a high-pitched, whistling sound that is often most noticeable when your child breathes out.
