Toddler Vomiting Home Care: A Practical Guide

Vomiting in toddlers is most often caused by a viral tummy bug (gastroenteritis), which is very common in young children and spreads easily in nurseries and schools . The good news is that vomiting from a stomach bug typically stops within 1 to 3 days . Your main job at home is to prevent dehydration.

Focus on Fluids: Preventing Dehydration

Babies and toddlers are especially vulnerable to dehydration because their bodies don't hold onto water as well, and they have less fluid to lose to begin with . Don't wait until you see signs of dehydration to act .

How to Give Fluids

Offer small amounts frequently. Give your toddler 5 mL (1 teaspoon) of fluid every 2 to 3 minutes . If they can keep that down, you can increase to up to 30 mL (2 tablespoons) every 5 minutes .

What to give: Offer clear fluids such as:

An oral rehydration solution (like Pedialyte) – these are best for replacing lost salts and minerals Watered-down apple juice (half juice, half water) for children over 1 year Barley water or rice water What to avoid: Do not give fruit juice or soda pop – they contain too much sugar and lack the essential minerals your child needs . Also, do not give anti-vomiting or anti-diarrhoea medicines to a child unless prescribed by a doctor .

Eating

No solid food for 8 hours. During the first 8 hours without vomiting, stick to clear fluids only .

After 8 hours. If your child hasn't vomited for 8 hours, you can slowly start offering bland, easy-to-digest foods like crackers, toast, plain biscuits, rice, or mashed potatoes .

Don't force food. It's more important to keep fluids down. If they don't want to eat, that's okay as long as they are drinking .

Recognizing Dehydration: When to Call the Doctor

Check for wet nappies. This is the most reliable way to tell if your toddler is hydrated. Urine output should be:

3–5 months At least every 3 hours 6–23 months At least every 6 hours 2 years and older At least every 8 hours Call your doctor immediately if you notice any of these signs of dehydration :

No wet nappy or urine for 8 hours or more Dry mouth and lips Crying without tears Unusual sleepiness or irritability Sunken eyes Red Flags: When to Seek Emergency Care

Go to the Emergency Department or call 999/112 immediately if your toddler:

Has green or blood-stained vomit Is vomiting and unable to keep down any fluids Has blood in their poo Is breathing very fast, working hard to breathe, or has pauses in breathing Is difficult to wake up, very sleepy, or confused Looks pale, blue, mottled, or feels unusually cold to the touch Has a fit (seizure) Has a rash that does not disappear with pressure (the Glass Test) Stop the Bug from Spreading
