Fever Medication for Infants: Dosing by Weight

When your infant has a fever, the most accurate way to determine the correct dose of medication is by their weight in kilograms (kg), not their age . This is critical because giving the wrong amount is a common mistake when parents dose by age alone .

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) Dosing

Paracetamol is the preferred first-line fever medication and the only one recommended for infants under 3 months of age .

Standard single dose: 10–15 mg per kg of body weight . Do not exceed a maximum single dose of 1000 mg .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 60 mg per kg per day, divided into 4 to 6 doses .

Frequency: Give every 4–6 hours as needed . Do not give more than 4 to 5 doses in 24 hours .

Age restriction: Check with your healthcare provider before giving paracetamol to a child under 2 years of age . Fever in a baby under 12 weeks requires immediate medical attention; do not self-treat .

Ibuprofen Dosing

Ibuprofen is an alternative for infants over 3 months of age, but should not be given to infants under 6 months old unless directed by a doctor .

Standard single dose: 7–10 mg per kg of body weight . Do not exceed a maximum single dose of 600 mg (for children over 12 years) .

Maximum daily dose: Do not exceed 20–30 mg per kg per day , divided into 3 to 4 doses .

Frequency: Give every 6–8 hours as needed . Do not give more than 3 to 4 doses in 24 hours.

Practical Dosing Example

For liquid paracetamol (commonly 160 mg/5 mL in the US), the dose is weight-based .

For a child weighing 24–35 lbs, the dose would be 5 mL . For 36–47 lbs, the dose is 7.5 mL . Always use the dosing syringe or cup provided with the medicine . Never use a kitchen teaspoon.

Important Safety Rules

Choose one medication. The American Academy of Pediatrics generally does not recommend alternating between paracetamol and ibuprofen due to the risk of dosing errors . Use only one at a time.

Check for combination products. Do not give medicines with more than one active ingredient to children under 6 years of age . Many cold remedies also contain paracetamol – check labels to avoid accidental overdose.

Treat the child, not the number. The decision to give fever medication should be based on the child&#x27;s discomfort, not solely on the thermometer reading . Antipyretics are meant to improve well-being, not to normalize temperature.
