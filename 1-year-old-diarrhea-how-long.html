Diarrhea in a 1-Year-Old: How Long It Lasts and What to Do

Diarrhea in a 1-year-old can be concerning, but it is often a short-term issue. Most cases of acute diarrhea—which is diarrhea that lasts a short time—resolve on their own . This guide will help you understand the typical duration of diarrhea and when it may signal a more serious problem.

How Long Does Diarrhea Typically Last?

Acute diarrhea is a common problem and usually lasts about one or two days, but it may last longer . In many cases, it goes away on its own without the need for medical treatment.

In young children, a common cause of diarrhea is a virus like rotavirus, which is the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children . These viral infections typically run their course within a few days. However, you should monitor your child closely for signs that they are recovering or if the condition is worsening.

When to Call the Doctor

While acute diarrhea is often mild, there are times when you should contact your child's healthcare provider. You should seek medical advice if your child's diarrhea:

Lasts more than a few days . If acute diarrhea persists beyond a few days, it could be a sign of a more serious problem.

Is accompanied by a high fever.

Causes signs of dehydration, such as a dry mouth, no tears when crying, or fewer wet diapers.

Contains blood or pus.

Is associated with severe abdominal pain.

Understanding Chronic Diarrhea

If your child has diarrhea that lasts at least four weeks, it is considered chronic diarrhea . This can be a symptom of a chronic disease, such as Crohn's disease or problems with how the colon functions, like irritable bowel syndrome (Source: NIH | Diarrhea). Chronic diarrhea symptoms may be continual, or they may come and go (Source: NIH | Diarrhea). If you notice this pattern, it's essential to see a doctor for a proper diagnosis.

What You Can Do at Home

While your child has diarrhea, you can help them feel better by:

Keeping them hydrated by offering small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution or other clear fluids.

Monitoring their wet diapers to ensure they are not becoming dehydrated.

Contacting your pediatrician for advice, especially if you are worried about your child's health.
