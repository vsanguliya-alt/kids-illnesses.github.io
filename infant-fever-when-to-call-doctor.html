Infant Fever: When to Call the Doctor

It is hard when your baby is sick. It can be scary when your baby has a fever, especially when you are not sure if it is a serious problem (Source: NIH). The most important rule is: trust your intuition. If you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH). If you have concerns about your baby's health, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH).

When to Call the Doctor Immediately

You should call your healthcare provider right away if your baby has a fever and:

Is under 3 months old – any fever (temperature of 38°C / 100.4°F or higher) in a baby under 3 months requires immediate medical evaluation. Do not wait.

Is between 3 and 6 months old with a temperature of 38.9°C (102°F) or higher.

Has a fever lasting more than 3 days – persistent fever needs medical evaluation.

Is not feeding well – taking less than half of their normal amount of milk.

Is unusually fussy or irritable – cannot be settled or is crying more than usual.

Is lethargic or difficult to wake – appears drowsy, limp, or not responding normally.

Has signs of dehydration – fewer wet diapers than usual, dry mouth, no tears when crying, or sunken eyes.

Has vomiting or diarrhea – especially if unable to keep fluids down.

Has a rash – any new rash with a fever should be evaluated.

Has a seizure (fit) – this requires urgent medical attention.

Has a stiff neck – or seems sensitive to light.

Has difficulty breathing – breathing fast, labored, or with grunting sounds.

When to Seek Emergency Care (Call 112/999)

Seek emergency care immediately if your baby:

Is under 3 months old with any fever

Has blue or pale skin, lips, or tongue

Is limp, floppy, or unresponsive

Has a seizure lasting more than 5 minutes

Has a rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass

Has signs of severe dehydration

General Principles

Trust your instincts – you know your baby best. If something feels wrong, call (Source: NIH).

Do not wait – if you are worried, call right away. It is always better to be safe.

Do not give aspirin to a child with a fever.

Use caution when giving cough and cold products to kids (Source: NIH).

Keep your baby comfortable – light clothing and plenty of fluids.
