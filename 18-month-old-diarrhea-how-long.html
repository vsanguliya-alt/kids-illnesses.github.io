Diarrhea in an 18-Month-Old: How Long It Lasts and What to Do

Diarrhea in an 18-month-old is often caused by a viral infection like rotavirus, which is one of the most common causes of severe diarrhea in young children (Source: NIH | Rotavirus Infections). While it can be concerning, most cases are short-term and manageable at home. This guide will help you understand the typical duration and when it may signal a more serious problem.

How Long Does Diarrhea Typically Last?

Acute diarrhea, which is diarrhea that lasts a short time, is a common problem (Source: NIH | Diarrhea). For viral infections like rotavirus, vomiting and diarrhea may last from three to eight days (Source: NIH | Rotavirus Infections). This is the typical course for a stomach bug, and the symptoms usually resolve on their own as the body fights off the infection.

When to Call the Doctor

While most cases of diarrhea are mild, it is crucial to monitor your child for signs of dehydration or a more serious condition. Contact your child's healthcare provider immediately if your child has any of the following signs:

Change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

A high fever (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Vomiting that is frequent or prevents them from keeping fluids down (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Six or more loose stools in a day (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Severe pain in the abdomen or rectum (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

Symptoms of dehydration, such as thirst, dry mouth, headache, dark-colored urine, and urinating less than normal (Source: NIH | Gastroenteritis).

🚨 Emergency Signs

Call 911 or go to an emergency room immediately if your child shows signs of severe dehydration or is very unwell.

What You Can Do at Home

The primary goal is to prevent dehydration. Offer your child small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution (like Pedialyte) or other clear fluids.
