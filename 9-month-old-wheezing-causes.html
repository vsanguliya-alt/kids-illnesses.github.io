Causes of Wheezing in a 9-Month-Old

Wheezing is a high-pitched whistling sound that occurs when a baby breathes out, indicating that the airways in the lungs are narrowed or partially blocked. In a 9-month-old, this symptom can have several different causes, some of which require immediate medical attention.

Viral Infections: The Most Common Trigger

By far, the most frequent cause of a sudden episode of wheezing in infants and young children is a viral respiratory infection, often from viruses like respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), rhinovirus, or human metapneumovirus. In children under 2, this is frequently diagnosed as bronchiolitis, a common chest infection that causes inflammation and mucus buildup in the small airways (bronchioles). This can cause a cough that often brings up mucus, shortness of breath, wheezing, a low fever, and chest tightness (Source: NIH | Acute Bronchitis). This type of viral-induced wheeze is often called "reactive airways disease" or "preschool wheeze."

Anaphylaxis: A Life-Threatening Emergency

It is crucial to be aware that wheezing can be a sign of a severe allergic reaction called anaphylaxis. This is a life-threatening emergency that requires immediate action. The most common causes in infants are reactions to foods (especially peanuts), medications, and stinging insects (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis).

If your baby has wheezing along with any of these other signs, call 911 immediately (Source: NIH | Anaphylaxis):

Skin symptoms: Itching, hives, redness, or swelling

Mouth symptoms: Itching or swelling of the lips or tongue

Throat symptoms: Tightness, trouble swallowing, or swelling of the back of the throat

Breathing problems: Shortness of breath, coughing, or chest tightness (in addition to wheezing)

Other signs: Dizziness, fainting, or vomiting

Other Possible Causes of Wheezing

While viruses and severe allergies are the most common culprits, it is important to be aware of other possible causes.

Foreign Body Aspiration: If a baby has a sudden onset of coughing, choking, and wheezing that seems to come out of nowhere, it could mean they have inhaled a small object like a peanut or a small toy part. This is a medical emergency and most common in children aged 1 to 4 years, but can happen in infants as well.

Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD): GERD can cause wheezing by allowing stomach acid to irritate the airways.

Airway Abnormalities: Less common causes include airway malacia (floppy airways), which can be a structural issue present from birth.

Because the causes of wheezing in a 9-month-old are varied and some are life-threatening, it is important to consult a pediatrician for an accurate diagnosis. They will listen to the baby's breathing pattern and consider the history of the episode to determine the most likely cause and appropriate management.
