Wheezing in Children: Common Causes

Wheezing is a high-pitched whistling sound that occurs when your child breathes out. It is a sign that the airways in the lungs are narrowed or blocked. Recognizing the potential causes can help you understand when to seek medical care.

Asthma: A Primary Cause of Wheezing

One of the most common causes of wheezing in children is asthma. This chronic condition causes the airways to become inflamed and narrowed, leading to wheezing, coughing, and tightness in the chest (Source: NIH).

Asthma Triggers

An asthma attack can happen when your child is exposed to a trigger. Triggers vary by child and can change over time (Source: NIH). Common triggers include:

Trigger Category Examples

Allergens Dust mites, mold, pets, pollen from grass/trees/weeds, waste from pests like cockroaches and mice (Source: NIH)

Nonallergic triggers Breathing in cold air, certain medicines, household chemicals, outdoor air pollution, tobacco smoke (Source: NIH)

Infections Common colds and the flu (Source: NIH)

Exercise Exercise-induced asthma occurs during physical activity, especially when the air is dry (Source: NIH)

Symptoms of Asthma in Children

In addition to wheezing, asthma symptoms include (Source: NIH):

Coughing, especially at night or early morning

Breathing problems – shortness of breath, rapid breathing, or gasping for air

Dark circles under the eyes

Wheezing – causes a whistling sound when breathing

Risk Factors

Certain factors can raise your child's risk of having asthma, including being exposed to secondhand smoke when your mother is pregnant with you or when you are a child (Source: NIH).

Acute Bronchitis

Acute bronchitis is another condition that can cause wheezing. It causes a cough that often brings up mucus, and it can also lead to shortness of breath, wheezing, a low fever, and chest tightness. In some cases, a child may need inhaled medicine to open the airways if they are wheezing (Source: NIH).

Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic Reaction)

Wheezing can also be a symptom of a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction called anaphylaxis (Source: NIH).

Common Causes of Anaphylaxis:

Reactions to foods (especially peanuts)

Medications

Stinging insects

Exercise and exposure to latex (Source: NIH)

Symptoms of Anaphylaxis

Wheezing in anaphylaxis is accompanied by other symptoms affecting multiple organs, including (Source: NIH):

System Symptoms

Skin Itching, hives, redness, swelling

Nose Sneezing, stuffy nose, runny nose

Mouth Itching, swelling of lips or tongue

Throat Itching, tightness, trouble swallowing, swelling

Chest Shortness of breath, coughing, wheezing, chest pain/tightness

Heart Weak pulse, passing out, shock

Gastrointestinal Vomiting, diarrhea, cramps

Nervous system Dizziness or fainting

If someone is having a serious allergic reaction, call 911 immediately (Source: NIH).
