Cough in a Baby: When to Worry

A cough in a baby is common, often part of a viral cold. While it can be uncomfortable, there are important signs that indicate a cough may be a sign of a more serious problem requiring immediate medical attention.

🚨 Emergency Signs: When to Call 911 or Go to A&amp;E

Seek emergency care immediately if your baby&#x27;s cough is accompanied by any of the following:

Blue or gray lips or face: This is a sign of low oxygen levels.

Difficulty breathing: This includes fast breathing, working hard to breathe (using chest or neck muscles), or making a &quot;whooping&quot; sound when breathing in after a coughing fit.

Severe coughing fits: Fits of rapid coughing that are followed by a high-pitched &quot;whoop&quot; sound, or coughing that leads to vomiting or exhaustion are signs of a serious infection like whooping cough (Source: NHS | Whooping cough). Babies under 6 months old with whooping cough have an increased chance of having problems (Source: NHS | Whooping cough).

A seizure.

🟡 When to Call Your Pediatrician Urgently

Contact your child&#x27;s doctor immediately if the cough is accompanied by:

A high fever.

Signs of dehydration, such as fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, or no tears when crying.

A cough that has not improved after a few days or is getting worse.

Wheezing or a high-pitched sound when breathing in (stridor).

What You Can Do at Home

For a mild cough, there are safe ways to help your baby feel more comfortable at home:

Do not give cough medicine to children under two years old (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Use a cool-mist humidifier in your baby&#x27;s room (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Use saline nose drops and a bulb syringe to clear a stuffy nose, which can help reduce coughing (Source: NIH | Cold and Cough Medicines).

Always trust your instincts. If you are worried about your baby&#x27;s cough, contact your pediatrician for advice.
