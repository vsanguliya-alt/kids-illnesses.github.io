Ear Infections and Antibiotics in Children: What Parents Need to Know

Ear infections are the most common reason parents bring their child to a doctor. In fact, three out of four children will have at least one ear infection by their third birthday. The infection usually affects the middle ear and is called otitis media (Source: NIH).

When Are Antibiotics Needed?

Not every ear infection requires antibiotics. Your health care provider will diagnose an ear infection by looking inside the ear with an instrument called an otoscope (Source: NIH).

Antibiotics are typically required in specific situations:

Severe infections – your provider will determine if the infection is severe enough to warrant antibiotics (Source: NIH)

Infections in young babies – infants are at higher risk for complications and may require antibiotics (Source: NIH)

Additionally, if your child has a perforated eardrum, you may need antibiotics if you have an ear infection, or to prevent getting an ear infection while the eardrum heals (Source: NHS). A perforated eardrum usually gets better on its own within 2 months, but antibiotics may be necessary (Source: NHS).

What Else Could Be Causing Ear Pain?

Not all ear pain means an ear infection that needs antibiotics. Other causes include:

Earwax build-up

An object stuck in the ear (do not try to remove it yourself – see a GP)

Perforated eardrum (particularly after a loud noise or accident)

Baby teething

Dental abscess (Source: NHS)

When to See a Doctor

Your child should see a healthcare provider if they have:

Ear pain with a change in hearing

Ear pain with pain when swallowing

Ear pain with a high temperature (Source: NHS)

For glue ear (fluid in the middle ear without active infection): A GP should be able to tell if it's glue ear by looking for fluid inside the ear. If your child has had glue ear for more than 3 months, they may be referred for further evaluation (Source: NHS). Glue ear can affect a child's speech development and progress at school if it lasts a long time (Source: NHS).

Key Takeaway

Situation Antibiotic Recommendation

Mild ear infection in older child May not need antibiotics – watchful waiting may be appropriate

Severe infection or infection in young baby Antibiotics likely required

Ear infection with perforated eardrum May need antibiotics

Glue ear (fluid without infection) Antibiotics not typically needed

Always follow your healthcare provider's guidance regarding antibiotics for your child's ear infection. Never use leftover antibiotics or give medications prescribed for someone else.
