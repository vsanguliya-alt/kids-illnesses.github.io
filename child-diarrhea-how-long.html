How Long Does Diarrhea Last in Children? A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Diarrhea—loose, watery stools occurring three or more times in one day—is a common and distressing symptom in young children (Source: NIH). The duration depends on the underlying cause, but most cases are acute and resolve within a few days.

Typical Duration

For viral gastroenteritis, often called the &quot;stomach flu,&quot; diarrhea typically lasts from three to eight days. For example, in a rotavirus infection—a common cause of severe diarrhea in children—once a child gets the virus, it takes about two days to become sick, and vomiting and diarrhea may last from three to eight days (Source: NIH).

When to Seek Medical Help

While many cases resolve on their own, diarrhea can lead to dehydration, which is a serious concern—especially in infants and young children. Contact your healthcare provider right away if your child experiences any of the following warning signs (Source: NIH):

For infants and children, seek immediate medical attention if they have:

Change in mental state, such as irritability or lack of energy (Source: NIH)

Diarrhea lasting more than 2 days (Source: NIH)

Six or more loose stools in a single day (Source: NIH)

Severe pain in the abdomen (belly) or rectum (Source: NIH)

Stools that are black and tarry or contain blood or pus (Source: NIH)

Symptoms of dehydration, such as:

Thirst and dry mouth (Source: NIH)

Headache (Source: NIH)

Dark-colored urine (Source: NIH)

Urinating less than normal (Source: NIH)

Preventing Dehydration at Home

The most important home care measure is preventing dehydration. Have your child drink plenty of liquids to replace fluids lost through diarrhea (Source: NIH). Offer oral rehydration solutions (ORS) or small, frequent sips of clear fluids. Avoid sugary drinks, as they can worsen diarrhea.

Key Takeaway

Acute diarrhea lasts a short time—typically 3 to 8 days for viral infections (Source: NIH).

Red flag: If diarrhea persists beyond 2 days in a child, or if you notice any of the warning signs above, contact your healthcare provider immediately (Source: NIH).
