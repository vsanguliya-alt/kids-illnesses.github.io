What Parents Should Know

While the sources do not provide specific guidance, the most important concern with any gastrointestinal illness is dehydration. Toddlers can become dehydrated quickly, which can be dangerous.

Signs of dehydration in toddlers include:

Decreased urination (fewer wet diapers or trips to the toilet)

Dry mouth and lips

No tears when crying

Sunken eyes

Unusual drowsiness or irritability

Thirst

When to seek medical help:

Your toddler has signs of dehydration

Your toddler has a fever

Vomiting or diarrhea lasts more than 24 hours

You see blood in the stool or vomit

Your child has severe abdominal pain

Your child is not able to keep fluids down

You are worried about your child

Trust your instincts – if you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH). Well-child visits are important to your toddler&#x27;s health (Source: NIH). It is always better to be safe and seek medical advice when in doubt.
