Flu Symptoms in Children: What Parents Need to Know

Influenza (the flu) is a respiratory infection that can affect children of all ages. While the provided sources focus on Reye&#x27;s syndrome, which is a serious complication that can occur after a viral infection like the flu, and differentiate the flu from other conditions, they do not provide a comprehensive list of typical flu symptoms. This article outlines what is known from the provided materials regarding flu-related concerns.

Flu vs. Common Cold vs. Stomach Flu

It is important to distinguish the flu from other common illnesses:

Common Cold: Symptoms usually start a few days after infection. Some symptoms can last for 10days (Source: NIH). The common cold is caused by viruses and does not typically cause high fever or severe body aches like the flu.

Stomach Flu (Gastroenteritis): The main symptoms include vomiting and diarrhea (Source: NIH). This is a different illness from respiratory flu, which primarily affects the respiratory system.

Important Flu-Related Complication: Reye&#x27;s Syndrome

Parents must be aware of Reye&#x27;s syndrome, a very serious condition that can develop 1 to 3 weeks after a child recovers from a virus like flu or chickenpox (Source: NHS).

Critical Warning: Reye&#x27;s syndrome is linked to aspirin use in children. If your child has recently had flu or chickenpox, never give them aspirin.

Symptoms of Reye&#x27;s Syndrome (Medical Emergency)

If your child shows any of these symptoms after a flu infection, seek emergency medical care immediately (Source: NHS):

Symptom Description

Difficulty breathing Struggling to breathe

Limp or floppy Not responding normally; head may fall to the side, backwards, or forwards; difficulty lifting head or focusing on face

Persistent vomiting Keeps being sick (vomiting)

Drowsiness or lack of energy Unusually tired or lethargic

Behavioral changes Not their normal self – irritable or aggressive

When to Contact a Healthcare Provider

For any child, you should contact your provider if they have symptoms that concern you, such as (Source: NIH):

Trouble breathing or fast breathing

Fever that lasts longer than 4 days

Symptoms that last more than 10 days without improvement

Symptoms that improve but then return or worsen

Worsening of chronic medical conditions

Note: There is no vaccine to protect against the common cold, but the flu vaccine is recommended annually for all children aged 6 months and older (Source: NIH).
