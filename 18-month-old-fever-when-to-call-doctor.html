Fever in an 18-Month-Old: When to Call the Doctor

Fever is a common sign that your child's body is fighting an infection. While most fevers are harmless, there are clear guidelines to help you decide when to contact your pediatrician.

What is a Fever?

A fever is defined as a body temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher . The normal body temperature is around 37°C, and it's important to remember that fever itself is not dangerous—it is the body's natural way of coping with viruses and bacteria .

When to Call the Doctor for an 18-Month-Old

For a child over 3 months of age, most fevers can be managed at home . However, you should call your pediatrician in the following situations:

Fever lasts more than 2–3 days without other symptoms Your child has a fever and is showing signs of dehydration —not drinking well, urinating less than once every 8 hours, no tears when crying, or dry mouth The fever is accompanied by other concerning symptoms like a severe headache, earache, stiff neck, or repeated vomiting/diarrhea You are worried about your child's condition, even if you're not sure why Signs That Require Immediate Medical Attention

You should contact your doctor right away or go to the emergency room if your child has a fever and any of the following "red flags":

Difficulty breathing — fast breathing, retractions (sucking in muscles under the ribs), wheezing, or grunting Altered consciousness — extreme lethargy, difficulty waking up, or unusual irritability A stiff neck or bulging fontanelle (soft spot on the head) A rash that does not fade when pressed (petechial or purpuric rash) A seizure (febrile convulsion), especially if it lasts longer than 5 minutes Signs of severe dehydration Fever lasting 5 days or more Important Considerations

For infants under 3 months of age, any fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher requires immediate medical attention .

If your child looks well, is drinking, and behaving normally, you can wait and monitor the fever for a day or two .

If you are ever unsure about your child's condition, it is always best to trust your instincts and contact a healthcare professional .
