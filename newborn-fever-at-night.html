Newborn Fever at Night: What Parents Need to Know

A fever at night can feel alarming. The house is quiet, and your baby feels warm. It can be scary when your baby is sick, especially when it is not an everyday problem like a cold or a fever (Source: NIH). Understanding what to do and when to seek help can help you respond calmly and effectively.

What Is a Fever?

A fever is a body temperature that is higher than normal. A fever is not a disease. Your baby gets a fever because their body is trying to kill the virus or bacteria that caused the infection. Fever also activates your baby&#x27;s immune system (Source: NIH).

Why Fever Often Spikes at Night

You are not imagining it. Fevers frequently become more noticeable or worsen at night. The body temperature of all humans naturally rises slightly in the late afternoon and evening. A child who had a mild fever during the day can spike significantly higher after bedtime. Additionally, stress hormones like cortisol decrease at night, allowing the immune system to become more active, which can raise body temperature.

What Is Considered a Fever?

A normal body temperature for newborns ranges between 36.4°C and 37.5°C. A fever is typically defined as a temperature of 38°C or higher.

When to Call a Doctor or Seek Emergency Care

Seek immediate medical attention if your baby:

Is under 3 months old with a temperature of 38°C or higher. This requires urgent evaluation; do not wait until morning.

Has a fever above 40°C that does not come down with medication.

Is having difficulty breathing.

Has a stiff neck or is sensitive to light.

Has a rash that does not fade when pressed.

Has a seizure (febrile seizure). A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes is a medical emergency.

Is difficult to wake, extremely drowsy, or confused.

Shows signs of dehydration: fewer wet nappies, dry mouth, no tears when crying.

How to Care for Your Baby at Night

Keep your baby comfortable in light clothing and a comfortably cool room. Do not over-bundle them.

Offer plenty of fluids frequently to prevent dehydration.

Monitor regularly, but avoid waking your baby just to take their temperature.

Consider medication like paracetamol or ibuprofen if your baby is uncomfortable or distressed, using age- and weight-appropriate doses. Never give aspirin to a child with a fever.

Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, call your healthcare provider. It can be scary when your baby is sick, but learning information about your baby&#x27;s condition can help ease your worry (Source: NIH).
