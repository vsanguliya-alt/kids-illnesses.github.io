Infant Cough Home Remedies: Safe Options for Babies Under 2

Coughing in infants can be distressing for parents, but most coughs are caused by viral infections that will resolve on their own. The most important rule is: do not give cough or cold medicines to children under two years of age (Source: NIH). This includes over-the-counter cough suppressants, expectorants, decongestants, and antihistamines. Never give aspirin to children (Source: NIH).

Instead, focus on safe, non-medication remedies to keep your baby comfortable while their body fights the infection.

Safe Home Remedies for Infant Cough

1. Cool Mist Humidifier

Using a cool mist humidifier in your baby&#x27;s room adds moisture to the air. This can help soothe irritated airways and loosen mucus, making it easier for your baby to breathe and cough productively (Source: NIH).

2. Saline Nose Drops or Sprays

Saline nose drops or sprays can help thin nasal secretions. This is especially helpful if the cough is caused by post-nasal drip from a stuffy nose (Source: NIH).

3. Nasal Suctioning with a Bulb Syringe

After using saline drops, you can use a bulb syringe to gently suction out mucus from your baby&#x27;s nose. This can clear the nasal passages and reduce coughing triggered by mucus draining down the throat (Source: NIH).

4. Plenty of Fluids

Ensure your baby stays well-hydrated. For infants, this means offering breast milk or formula frequently. Fluids help keep mucus thin and easier to clear.

Understanding Cough Medicines

There are different types of cough and cold medicines, including:

Nasal decongestants – unclog a stuffy nose

Cough suppressants – quiet a cough

Expectorants – loosen mucus in the lungs

Antihistamines – stop runny noses and sneezing

Pain relievers – ease fever, headaches, and minor aches and pains (Source: NIH)

However, none of these should be given to a child under 2 years of age (Source: NIH). Many cold and cough medicines contain the same active ingredients, increasing the risk of accidental overdose if multiple products are used (Source: NIH).

When to Call the Doctor

While the sources provided do not list specific warning signs for infant cough, you should contact your healthcare provider if:

Your baby is under 3 months old with any cough

Your baby has difficulty breathing or fast breathing

Your baby has a fever (especially under 3 months)

Your baby is not feeding well or shows signs of dehydration

The cough lasts more than 10 days without improvement

You are worried about your baby
