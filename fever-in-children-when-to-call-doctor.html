Fever in Children: When to Call the Doctor

Understanding Fever

A fever is a body temperature that is higher than normal. A normal temperature can vary from person to person, but it is usually around 98.6 °F (37 °C) (Source: NIH | Fever). It is important to understand that a fever is not a disease. It is usually a sign that your body is trying to fight an illness or infection (Source: NIH | Fever).

When your child has a fever, their body is actively working to kill the virus or bacteria that caused the infection. Most bacteria and viruses do well at normal body temperature, but when fever develops, it becomes harder for them to survive. Fever also activates your body's immune system (Source: NIH | Fever).

Common Causes of Fever

Infections cause most fevers (Source: NIH | Fever). These infections may be caused by viruses such as the flu, norovirus, or rotavirus—with rotavirus being the most common cause of acute diarrhea in children (Source: NIH | Diarrhea). Other causes of fevers include certain medicines (such as some antibiotics, blood pressure medicines, and anti-seizure medicines) as well as some childhood vaccines (Source: NIH | Fever).

Accompanying Symptoms: Diarrhea and Vomiting

Fever in children is often accompanied by other symptoms. Diarrhea is defined as loose, watery stools. You have diarrhea if you have loose stools three or more times in one day. Acute diarrhea is a common problem that usually lasts about one or two days, though it may last longer before resolving on its own (Source: NIH | Diarrhea).

Vomiting is another common symptom—it forces the contents of the stomach up through the food pipe and out of the mouth. Nausea and vomiting can be caused by infections of the stomach or bowels, such as the "stomach flu" (Source: NIH | Vomiting).

When to Call the Doctor

Fever lasting or very high: Treatment depends on the cause of your fever. If the fever is very high, your health care provider should be consulted (Source: NIH | Fever).

Diarrhea lasting more than a few days: Diarrhea that persists beyond a few days may be a sign of a more serious problem. Chronic diarrhea—defined as diarrhea that lasts at least four weeks—can be a symptom of a chronic disease (Source: NIH | Diarrhea).

When vomiting signals a serious problem: Nausea and vomiting may be early warning signs of more serious medical problems, such as blockages (Source: NIH | Vomiting). If your child cannot keep fluids down, or if vomiting is accompanied by severe pain, seek medical attention.

Additional Signs Requiring Urgent Care

Seek immediate medical evaluation if your child's fever is accompanied by:

Signs of dehydration (such as fewer wet diapers, no tears when crying, sunken eyes)

Severe pain

Difficulty breathing

A stiff neck

Confusion or unusual drowsiness

A seizure

Special note for infants: Any fever in a baby under 3 months of age requires immediate medical attention, as their immune systems are still developing.
