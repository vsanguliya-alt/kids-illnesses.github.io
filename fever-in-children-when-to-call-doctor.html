Fever in Children: When to Call the Doctor

Understanding Fever

Fever is a common symptom in children, often signaling that the body is fighting an infection. Most young children get sick at some point, and it can be hard to know when to call the doctor. Trust your intuition—if you are worried about your child, you should call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH | Toddler Health). The same applies to infants: if you are worried about your baby, call your health care provider immediately (Source: NIH | Common Infant and Newborn Problems).

When to Call the Doctor for a Fever

Contact your child's pediatrician if the fever is accompanied by:

Age-specific fever guidelines: For infants under 3 months, any fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher requires immediate medical attention. For children 3 to 12 months, call if the fever reaches 102.2°F (39°C) or higher. For children over 2 years, call if the fever lasts more than 24 to 48 hours without an obvious cause (Source: NIH | Fever Medical Encyclopedia).

Signs of dehydration: If your child is not drinking enough fluids, has a dry mouth, no tears when crying, or has fewer wet diapers than usual.

Behavior changes: If your child is unusually sleepy, irritable, or not acting like themselves.

Other symptoms: If the fever is accompanied by vomiting, diarrhea, severe headache, stiff neck, or a rash.

Underlying health conditions: If your child has a chronic health condition, such as a heart or lung problem.

Emergency Signs Requiring Immediate Care

Go to the nearest emergency room or call 911 if your child has a fever and:

Is younger than 3 months with a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.

Is very sleepy, difficult to wake, or confused.

Has a stiff neck or severe headache.

Has trouble breathing (e.g., breathing fast, grunting, or using chest muscles to breathe).

Has a purple or dark red rash that does not fade when pressed.

Has a seizure that lasts more than 5 minutes or is a first-time seizure.

Shows signs of severe dehydration (no urination for 8hours, sunken eyes, or no tears).

General Guidelines for Managing a Fever

Keep your child comfortable and dressed in light clothing.

Offer plenty of fluids to prevent dehydration.

Use fever-reducing medications like acetaminophen or ibuprofen only if recommended by your child's doctor.

Do not give aspirin to children with fever, as it is linked to Reye's syndrome.
