Fever in Children: When to Call the Doctor

A fever is a temporary rise in your child's body temperature in response to an illness or infection (Source: NIH | Fever Medical Encyclopedia). It is a common sign that the body is fighting off an infection, often viral or bacterial (Source: NIH | Fever). While fever itself is a defense mechanism and usually harmless, knowing when to seek medical advice is essential for your child's safety.

What Temperature Is a Fever?

A child has a fever when their temperature reaches or exceeds certain levels depending on the measurement method (Source: NIH | Fever Medical Encyclopedia):

Rectally (in the bottom): 100.4°F (38°C) or higher

Orally (in the mouth): 99.5°F (37.5°C) or higher

Axillary (under the arm): 99°F (37.2°C) or higher

When to Call Your Pediatrician

Contact your child's healthcare provider right away in these situations:

Infants under 3 months old: Any fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher requires immediate medical evaluation (Source: NIH | Fever Medical Encyclopedia). Because their immune systems are still developing, a fever in a newborn can be a sign of a serious infection.

Children 3 to 6 months old: A fever of 102.2°F (39°C) or higher should be evaluated promptly.

Children 6 to 24 months old: If the fever lasts more than 24 hours, even without other symptoms, you should consult a doctor.

Children over 2 years: If the fever lasts more than 3 days (72 hours), it's time to call your doctor.

Any age: If the fever is very high (over 104°F / 40°C) (Source: NIH | Fever) or does not respond to fever-reducing medicine.

Your child appears very ill, is irritable, has a stiff neck, or cannot be consoled.

Signs of dehydration such as a dry mouth, no tears when crying, or fewer wet diapers (less than 4 in 24 hours for infants).

The fever is accompanied by a seizure (febrile seizure) (Source: NIH | Fever Medical Encyclopedia). While many febrile seizures are short and harmless, you should still notify your pediatrician.

When to Seek Emergency Care (Go to the Emergency Room)

Go to the nearest emergency room or call 911 immediately if your child has a fever and:

Is younger than 3 months with a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.

Is very sleepy, difficult to wake, or confused.

Has a stiff neck or severe headache.

Has trouble breathing (e.g., breathing fast, grunting, or using chest muscles to breathe).

Has a purple or dark red rash that does not fade when pressed (non-blanching rash).

Has a seizure that lasts more than 5 minutes, or has a first-time seizure.

Shows signs of severe dehydration (e.g., not urinating for 8hours, sunken eyes, or no tears).

Important Note: Never give aspirin to a child with a fever (Source: NIH | Fever). It is associated with a rare but serious condition called Reye's syndrome. If your child has a fever, focus on keeping them comfortable, offering fluids to prevent dehydration, and following your pediatrician's advice on medication.
