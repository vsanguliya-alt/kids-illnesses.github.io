18-Month-Old Constipation Relief: A Parent's Guide

Constipation in an 18-month-old can be a distressing experience for both child and parent. It is a common condition, affecting up to 30% of children, and is usually manageable with home care . This guide provides clear, evidence-based strategies for relief and outlines when to seek professional medical advice.

Recognizing the Signs

Constipation is characterized by the passage of hard, dry, or lumpy stools, often accompanied by straining, pain, and a frequency of fewer than two bowel movements per week .

Your child may show signs of trying to avoid the pain of pooing, such as clenching their buttocks, standing on tiptoes, or adopting a "dancelike" posture . Other symptoms can include a swollen tummy, loss of appetite, and general irritability .

Home Care Strategies for Relief

A consistent, multi-pronged approach is often the most effective way to manage constipation in toddlers.

1. Adjust Diet and Fluids

Increase Fluid Intake: Ensure your child drinks plenty of water throughout the day. This helps to soften the stool . For a toddler, aim for six to eight cups of fluid daily, including water and milk .

Add High-Fiber Foods: Gradually introduce more fiber-rich foods into their diet, such as fruits (pears, prunes, peaches), vegetables (peas, beans, broccoli), and whole grains . This helps the gut muscles work more effectively .

Limit Constipating Foods: Reduce the intake of foods that can contribute to constipation, such as excessive dairy products, bananas, and applesauce .

2. Establish a Toileting Routine

Encourage your child to sit on the potty or toilet after meals, as this is when the natural urge to go is strongest .

Make it a calm and unhurried experience. Use a footstool so their feet are flat on the floor to make passing a stool easier . Always praise them for trying, even if they don't produce a poo .

3. Encourage Physical Activity

Regular physical activity and movement can help stimulate the digestive system and promote regular bowel movements .

4. Medication with Caution

Never give your child laxatives or enemas without first consulting a doctor . A doctor may recommend an osmotic laxative (like polyethylene glycol) to soften the stool, especially for long-standing constipation . This type of medication is typically used for several weeks to months to allow the bowel to heal and break the cycle of painful pooing .

When to Call the Doctor

While home care is effective for most cases, you should seek medical advice if your child has constipation and:

Symptoms last longer than two weeks and are not improving with at-home treatment .

You see blood in their stool or bleeding from the rectum .

They have constant or severe tummy pain, bloating, or vomiting .

They experience unexplained weight loss .

They lose control of their bowel actions and begin soiling their underwear .
