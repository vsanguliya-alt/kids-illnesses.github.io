Child Vomiting in Children 0: Complete Parent Guide

Vomiting in children is a common and often concerning symptom for parents. However, in most cases, it will stop without specific medical treatment and is typically caused by a virus that will get better on its own . This comprehensive guide will help you understand how to manage vomiting at home, when to seek medical advice, and what to do in an emergency.

The Basics: Why Children Vomit

The most common cause of vomiting in children is acute gastroenteritis, often referred to as the "stomach flu" . This is an infection of the gut that usually lasts a few days . Other common causes include food allergies, urinary tract infections, ear infections, and, in rare cases, more serious conditions like appendicitis .

How to Manage Vomiting at Home

For the majority of cases, home care is all that's needed. The most important priority is to prevent dehydration . Here are the steps you should follow:

1. Focus on Fluids, Not Solids

For the first 12 to 24 hours of vomiting, avoid giving your child solid foods . Instead, encourage small, frequent sips of clear fluids to prevent dehydration and reduce the risk of triggering further vomiting . Oral rehydration solutions (ORS) like Pedialyte are the best choice to help replace lost electrolytes . You can also give water, but ORS is more effective for dehydration.

Estimated Fluid Needs (From HealthyChildren.org) :

For a child weighing 11lbs (5kg): About 16ounces of ORS per day.

For a child weighing 16lbs (7kg): About 23ounces of ORS per day.

2. When to Start Solids Again

After your child has kept fluids down for several hours, you can start offering bland foods like crackers, toast, or rice . Gradually reintroduce a normal diet if they are feeling better and are not dehydrated.

3. What NOT to Do

Avoid over-the-counter anti-nausea medications unless specifically prescribed by your pediatrician for that illness .

Never give aspirin to a child with vomiting, especially if they have had a recent viral illness (like flu or chickenpox), as it can trigger Reye's syndrome—a very rare but dangerous condition .

Red Flags: When to Call a Doctor

You should seek medical advice if your child:

Cannot keep down any fluids .

Is vomiting for more than a day or two .

Shows signs of dehydration: dry mouth, no tears when crying, peeing less than usual (or fewer wet nappies in babies), or unusual drowsiness .

Has a fever along with vomiting.

Stops breast or bottle feeding while ill .

Is a baby and has vomited 3 or more times in the past 24 hours .

Emergency Signs: Call 999 or Go to A&E Immediately

Seek immediate emergency care if your child's vomiting is accompanied by any of the following :

Vomit that is green (yellow-green), contains blood, or looks like coffee grounds.

A stiff neck and pain when looking at bright lights, which can be a sign of meningitis.

Severe tummy pain.

Sudden confusion or being difficult to rouse.

A pale, blotchy, or blue-tinged skin, lips, or tongue (on darker skin, check the palms of the hands).

Severe difficulty breathing (grunting, gasping, or a stomach sucking under the ribcage).

A sudden, severe headache.

Understanding Cyclical Vomiting Syndrome

A small number of children may experience cyclical vomiting syndrome (CVS). This is a rare condition characterized by severe episodes of nausea and vomiting that last for a few hours up to a few days, with periods of feeling completely well in between . Episodes can be triggered by stress, certain foods, or lack of sleep . CVS often starts in children around 5 years old and can improve with age . In some cases, medicines can help treat or prevent episodes .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor for my child's vomiting?

Call your pediatrician if your child cannot keep fluids down, shows signs of dehydration (e.g., fewer wet nappies, no tears, dry mouth), has a high fever, or is a baby who has vomited 3 or more times in 24 hours .

Q: How long does a stomach virus normally last?

Vomiting from gastroenteritis usually lasts 1 to 2 days . However, diarrhea can persist for a few more days. If vomiting continues for more than a day or two, speak to your GP .

Q: What home remedies help with vomiting?

The best home remedy is to prevent dehydration. Give your child small, frequent sips of water or an oral rehydration solution (like Pedialyte). Wait several hours after the last vomit before offering bland solid foods like toast or crackers .

Q: Is it okay to give my child anti-nausea medicine?

No, you should not give over-the-counter antiemetics. Only give these medications if they have been specifically prescribed by your pediatrician for that particular illness .

Q: Does a stomach bug always include diarrhea?

Often, but not always. Viral gastroenteritis frequently causes both vomiting and diarrhea. However, it can sometimes present with only vomiting .

Q: When should I go to the ER for vomiting?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has green or bloody vomit, a stiff neck, severe tummy pain, is confused or difficult to wake, or has a sudden, severe headache .
